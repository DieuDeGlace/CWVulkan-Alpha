# Alpha client terrain light source lifecycle

This contract describes the upstream sources of the terrain point-light candidates in the supported original Alpha client. The evidence root is `disassembled alpha cube world`. The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`, 3,885,568 bytes, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, with preferred image base `0x00400000`. Addresses below are RVAs. The original disassembly in `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` and individually identified original function files provide the supporting evidence. No reconstruction, runtime observation, replacement implementation or other contract was used for this document.

The bounded subject is source gathering by renderer `0x0AC260`, the directly inspected source-list publication/reset paths, and two producers plus the expiry phase of its fading-source list. Numeric source kinds and byte offsets identify evidence; they do not assign unproved gameplay names. This does not close every creator of sector objects, creatures, equipment, buffs or world-list records.

## Candidate identity and collection order

The renderer starts a new temporary candidate collection. Its records occupy 48 bytes each. Three signed 64-bit fixed-point coordinates occupy `+0x00`, `+0x08` and `+0x10`, at 65,536 units per world unit. Radius at `+0x18`, RGB at `+0x1C`, `+0x20`, `+0x24`, and distance priority at `+0x28` are binary32. The append helper copies these 44 meaningful bytes; the final four bytes are outside that semantic copy. A candidate carries no source identifier or source pointer, and this append does not merge matching positions or identities. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, renderer initialization `0x0AC3C0–0x0AC3D6`, append helper `0x0C11F0`; `decompiled/client/functions/004c11f0.c`.

The inspected renderer has twenty direct candidate-append sites. Their source groups occur in this order: current cached chunk sources; kind-1 records from client list `+0x2F8`; fading records from client list `+0x800748`; fourteen creature-dependent sites; then three sector-dependent sites. A source group is traversed in its original container sequence. Creature-dependent sites are considered in the table order below for each creature before advancing to another creature. Within each visited sector, its first object list precedes its second item-like list. The collection reaches the common sort call at `0x0B0E84` after these groups. This establishes insertion order, not a stable ordering of equal priorities after sorting. Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, direct calls to `0x0C11F0` from `0x0ACAA6` through `0x0B0DCD`; `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0B0E42–0x0B0E89`.

The distance priority is measured from each completed candidate position to the client's fixed-point reference position at `+0x140`. The fixed-point dot helper forms each signed 64-bit product and divides it by 65,536 before summing the three terms. The signed 64-bit result is rounded to binary32, then multiplied by binary32 `1/65536`. This retains the intermediate fixed-point quantization; it is not a distance from the eventual terrain chunk. Evidence: original helpers `0x03AC20`, `0x03B4A0`, `0x0120F0`, and candidate producers, including `0x0AC93B–0x0ACA14`; `decompiled/client/functions/0043ac20.c` and `0043b4a0.c`.

Unless another precision is stated, floating fields and arithmetic described here are binary32, and decimal constants identify the original binary32 literals. No common RGB normalization or clamp occurs while these candidates are gathered.

## Shared view admission and day factor

The renderer's distance argument, called L here, is the binary32 sum of client field `+0x1D4` and literal `22.399999618530273` (`0x41B33333`). The common admission helper `0x07F760` first compares fixed-point squared distance from client `+0x140` against a quantized squared limit. It squares L in binary32, multiplies by binary32 65,536, and truncates that result to signed 64-bit. Distance equal to the resulting limit survives; a larger distance fails. The supplied culling radius does not enlarge this distance limit.

Only after that distance test does the helper test six planes. It adds the client's signed 64-bit shifts at `+0x1D8` and `+0x1E0` to source X and Y, leaving Z unshifted. Each coordinate is rounded to binary32 before multiplication by `1/65536`. The six 16-byte plane records begin at client `+0x1000FA4`. Each plane uses its four binary32 coefficients: X and Y products are added, then the Z product, then the constant, then the supplied culling radius. An ordinary negative result fails; zero survives. All six must survive. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AC4E0–0x0AC50C`, `0x07F760–0x07F933`; `decompiled/client/functions/0047f760.c` and `0043ac20.c`.

The common day-dependent scalar, called D here, is computed once before gathering. The client's signed 32-bit clock at `+0x800440` is converted to binary32, multiplied by 2, divided by 86,400,000, and reduced by 1. That value is promoted to binary64 for the original precise power routine with exponent 4, and the result is rounded to binary32. Its difference from 1 is promoted to binary64 for power with exponent 5, then rounded to binary32 as D. This computation has no clamp. Cached kind-13 sources use a color multiplier obtained by subtracting binary32 half of D from 1. The sector kinds specified below use the binary32 difference of 1 and D. Clock ranges and initialization outside this local computation remain dependencies. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AC3E1–0x0AC44A`, precise power thunk `0x28D8D4`, literal pool near RVA `0x302A94`.

## Cached chunk sources and publication

Client `+0x2E0` points to cache entries of extent `0x268`; `+0x2DC` is the dimension, and signed 32-bit fields `+0x2AC` and `+0x2B0` are the scan origins. The renderer scans increasing first coordinate, then increasing second coordinate, each for the dimension-sized interval, and excludes coordinates outside `0` through `0x7FFFF`. The ring entry uses second-coordinate remainder times dimension plus first-coordinate remainder. A source list in each entry has current head/count at `+0x240/+0x244` and pending head/count at `+0x248/+0x24C`. Its critical section begins at `+0x250`.

The cached list's allocations are 72-byte nodes, including their two 32-bit links at `+0x00/+0x04`. Node-relative fields are: 32-bit kind at `+0x08`; signed 64-bit fixed position at `+0x10/+0x18/+0x20`; three additional 32-bit fields at `+0x28/+0x2C/+0x30`; binary32 RGB at `+0x34/+0x38/+0x3C`; and a 32-bit flag field at `+0x40`. The light test reads only its low byte's bit `0x01`. The semantic copy omits node bytes `+0x0C..+0x0F` and `+0x44..+0x47`. Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00688b20.c`, `00459ab0.c`, `004570a0.c`; renderer `0x0AC4E0–0x0AD47E`.

The inspected terrain worker gathers from a sector's list at `+0x04`, preserving that list's sequence. For each source, X and Y fixed coordinates are converted to binary64, multiplied by binary64 `1/32`, truncated to signed 64-bit, then divided by signed 65,536. Both resulting chunk coordinates must match the requested chunk coordinates. Before cloning an admitted source, the worker samples a voxel at its fixed coordinates divided by 65,536. It writes source node `+0x30` as binary32: 255 for low-five-bit voxel kind 13; the greater of the voxel's first unsigned byte and 5 for kind 0 or 2; zero for other kinds. This field is not the RGB triplet consumed by the light gather. The worker tail-appends semantic copies into a temporary list, then assigns that sequence into the chunk's pending list while holding the chunk critical section. Evidence: worker `0x09D910`, especially `0x0A0CD2–0x0A0F24` and publication near `0x0A11C2`; `decompiled/client/functions/0049d910.c`, `005c03f0.c`; binary64 literal at RVA `0x302A60`.

The assignment helper preserves the supplied sequence. It overwrites existing destination nodes in sequence, appends where the source sequence is longer, and removes an excess destination suffix. It neither deduplicates identities nor consumes the supplied list. In the renderer, the inspected refresh path first requires a zero result/count from its cache-key lookup. Under the chunk critical section, a nonzero pending geometry count at `+0x14` admits the geometry refresh and pending-to-current source assignment at `0x0AC646`. A zero count skips that assignment. Thus current sources are not replaced unconditionally on every rendered frame. The current-source traversal is protected by a subsequent entry into the same chunk critical section, released after that entry's traversal. The cache-key's complete production and every route that schedules this refresh are not closed here. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AC592–0x0AC65F`, `0x0AD40D–0x0AD434`; assignment `0x0570A0`.

For each current source with flag bit `0x01`, the renderer tests its stored position through `0x07F760` with culling radius 16 and L. A per-chunk signed 32-bit phase counter starts at zero. Only a source that passes this test advances it by 33. The admitted sources therefore use phase offsets 33, 66, 99, and so forth in their admitted list order. The candidate position is the stored fixed position, without an additional nonzero offset. Its radius is generated from client signed 32-bit field `+0x8003A0`: convert that field to binary32, multiply by `0.01`, add the current counter converted to binary32, promote to binary64 for the precise cosine call, round to binary32, multiply by `0.5`, then add `15`. RGB is copied from node `+0x34/+0x38/+0x3C`, with the previously described day multiplier only when kind is 13. Rejected and flag-clear nodes do not advance this radius phase. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AC758–0x0ACAA6`, loop continuation `0x0AD40D–0x0AD41D`, cosine thunk `0x28D7A4`.

Chunk construction creates empty current and pending lists and initializes `+0x250` with spin value `0x80000400`; it initializes signed coordinate fields `+0x18/+0x1C` to -1. Reset `0x086BA0` holds that critical section while invalidating those coordinates, clearing both source lists, clearing related geometry lists, and zeroing `+0x238/+0x23C`. It does not zero every old point-light array byte. These facts establish cache-local creation, replacement and removal, but not the creation/removal policy of the underlying sector source list. Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/004596e0.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x086BA0–0x086CF4`.

## World-list and fading sources

The next group traverses client list `+0x2F8`. Only records whose 32-bit field at `+0x60` equals 1 contribute. Their position is the three signed 64-bit fixed coordinates at record `+0x18`; radius is 16; RGB is binary32 `(0.5, 0.5, 0.2)` multiplied by the record's binary32 field at `+0x4C`. This local branch does not call the common view helper. It appends in list order. Creation, deletion, ownership and the broader meaning of these records remain outside the audited boundary. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AF334–0x0AF47C`, append `0x0AF442`.

The following group traverses client list `+0x800748`. Its 48-byte payload contains fixed position at `+0x00/+0x08/+0x10`, binary32 RGB at `+0x18/+0x1C/+0x20`, binary32 radius at `+0x24`, signed 32-bit age at `+0x28`, and signed 32-bit duration at `+0x2C`. The list uses 56-byte allocations including links. Append `0x0863D0` copies all 48 payload bytes, inserts at the tail and increments the 32-bit count, without identity merging or replacement. Erase `0x077C60` unlinks and deallocates the selected non-sentinel node and decrements the count. Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00451ff0.c`, `0046d710.c`, `004863d0.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x077C60–0x077CA1`.

Each resident fading payload contributes its stored position and radius. Age and duration are separately converted to binary32 before division. The fade is the binary32 difference of 1 and that quotient; an ordinary negative fade is replaced with zero. There is no upper clamp. Each stored RGB component is multiplied by that fade, and even a zero-color candidate is appended. This local gathering branch performs neither expiry nor the common view test. A source exactly at its positive duration therefore remains a candidate with zero RGB. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AF486–0x0AF5C5`.

Two inspected creation routes operate during client frame `0x088EE0` while processing 72-byte event records. The shared outer admission resolves the record's eight-byte identifier at `+0x08` to a nonnull target. A nonzero byte at record `+0x44` then admits a separate lookup of the leading eight-byte identifier. If that source resolves, its items are collected by helper `0x03DF60` and traversed in the returned order. Every item whose averaged color's fourth component is greater than zero appends a fading payload: position from event `+0x20`, the averaged RGB, radius 20, age 0, duration 400. Subsequently, nonzero event byte `+0x46` performs its own lookup of the leading identifier; a nonnull result appends one payload at the same event position, RGB `(0.3, 0.3, 0.5)`, radius 16, age 0, duration 400. Both routes can append for one event, with the item-derived records first. Their wider packet/ownership admission and item-collection helper selection policy are not audited here. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, target lookup near `0x0949B0`, `0x094C00–0x094E0E`, appends `0x094D21` and `0x094E09`.

Later in that frame, each fading payload's age receives the frame's effective integer duration. The native age addition is 32-bit; the subsequent comparison is signed. Nodes are collected for removal only when new age is greater than duration, then erased after the traversal. Equality survives. Creation at the preceding event sites therefore occurs before this same frame's age increment. The ordinary main loop obtains elapsed time from the 32-bit `timeGetTime` sample difference and passes it to the client update before rendering. Within the update, nonzero client byte `+0x8006E5` changes the integer duration to its quotient by four, truncated toward zero, with a minimum of 1; the ordinary branch retains the supplied duration. This describes the reached age phase, not every early return, pause or exceptional elapsed-time case. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x08C100` region, `0x0963CB–0x0964AD`; original main-loop helper `0x0C85F0`, client virtual entry at vtable RVA `0x2FFDC8 + 0x28`.

A second inspected producer is later in the renderer. Its creature traversal reaches a presentation branch only for ordinary non-positive health at creature `+0x16C`; positive health clears byte `+0x1D10` along its alternative path. The non-positive-health branch requires byte `+0x1D10` nonzero and a common view-helper pass using a position derived from rendered position `+0x1350` and vertical field `+0x1180`. It executes intervening presentation work, then only kind 144 at `+0x64` appends a fading payload: position from main fixed position `+0x10`, RGB `(1, 0.8, 0.5)`, radius 16, age 0, duration 800. The branch clears byte `+0x1D10` afterward, including when no payload is appended. This append at `0x0B4118` is after fading-source gathering and the terrain light upload near `0x0B1C0B`; the new payload cannot enter that already completed candidate collection. The exact presentation-specific culling radius/distance arguments, writer of the trigger byte and all surrounding creature-presentation gates remain unresolved. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0B39E0–0x0B3AC6`, `0x0B409D–0x0B4129`.

Attachment A starts with local binary32 vector `(0.5, 1, 0.3 times creature field +0x88)`. It is rotated around Z using the angle at `+0x30`, then converted to fixed-point and added to rendered position. Attachment B rotates local `(0, 1, 0)` around Z using angle `+0x137C`, converts that vector to fixed-point, and adds rendered position. Float-to-fixed conversion multiplies each component by binary32 65,536 before signed 64-bit truncation. The two item float attachments are converted first, then shifted by the negations of the creature's signed 64-bit fields `+0x1D18` and `+0x1D20` in X/Y, with no Z shift. For action source 10, equality with the helper threshold rejects the conditional action set. The four unconditional action values do not take that clock test. Its color scale converts signed action clock to binary32, multiplies by `0.01`, calls the original cosine adapter `0x00E420`, rounds the returned cosine to binary32, multiplies by `0.1`, then adds 1. The complete attack-speed/stat dependencies of threshold helper `0x03CAA0` are left as a named original dependency, rather than replaced with an assumed fixed duration. The type-9 source lookup reads the resident list and does not consume the buff in this gathering branch. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0AFFA9–0x0B0184`; `decompiled/client/functions/0043caa0.c`, `0040e420.c`; resident lookup `0x043F10`.

Q is a numeric counter ratio, without an inferred gameplay name: signed 32-bit creature `+0x70` converted to binary32, divided by helper `0x044CB0`'s signed integer result converted to binary32. Ordinary exact zero skips all four item sites; finite nonzero values, including negative values, pass this test. No ratio clamp occurs. That helper multiplies creature binary32 `+0x17C` by a selected constant and truncates to signed 32-bit. If item `+0xAA8` has category 3, subtype 6 selects 60, subtype 8 selects 80, subtypes 11, 12, 15, 16 and 17 select 20, and other subtypes select 50. When category is not 3, low-byte bit `0x10` of `+0x7E` selects 20; otherwise it selects 50. Zero denominators and exceptional floating values are not assigned an ordinary ratio contract. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0B0184–0x0B01C1`; `decompiled/client/functions/00444cb0.c`.

The shared item-color helper starts four binary32 sums at zero and scans a signed 32-bit positive count at item `+0x114`. It visits eight-byte subrecords in increasing index order and qualifies unsigned bytes at `+0x17 + 8 times index` only when at least 128. With the actual white four-component base color and scalar 1 supplied here, byte 128 contributes RGB `(S, S times 0.5, S times 0.1)`; 129 contributes `(S times 0.3, S, S times 0.5)`; 130 contributes `(S times 0.3, S times 0.5, S)`; 131 contributes `(S times 0.8, S times 0.8, S)`. S is the binary32 result of `1 + 1 times 0.5`; these four cases contribute fourth component 3. Qualifying bytes 132 through 255 contribute `(0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 1)`. Each component is accumulated in visitation order. If there are qualifying records, the sums are multiplied by binary32 reciprocal of their count; otherwise the result remains zero. No random draw is made by these helpers. Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/004c6cc0.c`, `004c7250.c`; original helpers `0x0C6CC0`, `0x0C7250`.

## Sector objects and item-like sources

After creatures, the renderer derives an inclusive sector rectangle from the controlled-creature pointer at client `+0x8006D0` and its main fixed X/Y. Each fixed component is divided by 65,536 and reduced to signed 32-bit, converted to binary32, then offset by minus or plus L. The result is multiplied by binary32 `1/256` and truncated toward zero to signed 32-bit. Increasing first-coordinate sectors are visited outside increasing second-coordinate sectors. Missing lookup results are skipped. This is a controlled-position-dependent gathering rectangle; it is not an unrestricted scan of all loaded sectors. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0B06EF–0x0B0875`, sector lookup `0x034D10`.

The first sector list is at `+0x0C`. Its object records use a 32-bit kind at `+0x00`, fixed position at `+0x08`, and binary32 size vector at `+0x24`.

- Kind 50 adds its size-vector Z component to the fixed base position before the common view test, using culling radius 20 and L. It appends that shifted position, radius 20, and RGB `(1.2, 1, 0.8)` multiplied by the binary32 difference of 1 and D.
- Kinds 51 and 65 first test the unshifted base position with culling radius 32 and L. Only after admission do they add size-vector Z. Their candidate radius is 20, and RGB `(1.2, 0.8, 0.4)` is multiplied by the same day-dependent difference. Therefore their tested and emitted positions differ.

Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0B08B1–0x0B0C0D`, appends `0x0B0A64` and `0x0B0BC7`.

The second sector list is at `+0x30`. Its local producer requires 32-bit field `+0x140` equal to zero and nonzero magnitude of helper `0x0C71C0`'s returned RGB. That helper returns `(0.1, 0.3, 0.3)` for first-byte/second-byte pair `(11,19)`, `(0.6, 0.6, 0.2)` for `(18,0)`, `(0, 0.6, 0.2)` for `(18,1)`, and zero for all other pairs. The emitted position is the signed 64-bit fixed position at `+0x118` with exactly three world units added to Z. That position passes the common helper with culling radius 16 and L; candidate radius is also 16. RGB is copied without D scaling. The color helper is called once for the admission magnitude and again for the emitted RGB. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, `0x0B0C0D–0x0B0DCD`; `decompiled/client/functions/004c71c0.c`, `0043abf0.c`.

## Ordering, lifetime and remaining limits

Candidate gathering reads the surviving source state anew for this renderer invocation. It does not by itself create, refresh or remove creatures, their buffs, their items, either sector source group, or kind-1 world-list records. The cached-source clone/publish/reset paths and fading-source append/age/erase paths above are the closed lifecycle subset. The source containers' iterator order is preserved up to candidate append, but their global insertion policies are not inferred. Equal-key sorting, per-chunk capacity selection and shader attenuation are downstream dependencies, outside this contract.

The common view test, item-color averaging, cosine-based radius/color arithmetic, candidate copying and fading-list age/removal contain no random draw in the inspected routines. That does not make the surrounding renderer draw-free: cached-source processing contains separate conditional presentation draws after a candidate append and before the next source, including calls around `0x0AD3B9–0x0AD3CB`. The later kind-144 fading producer also follows intervening presentation work. Their complete presentation guards and draw counts are not closed here, so this contract does not authorize moving source processing across those effects or treating its admitted-order phase as a random flicker.

The contract covers ordinary finite numeric states and valid source/container storage. It does not close signed fixed-point multiplication overflow, 32-bit timer wrap, invalid or changing source counts, NaNs/infinities, zero-duration division, nondefault floating control state, allocation failure, corrupted lists, all worker/renderer outer gates, or cross-thread mutation protection beyond the explicitly identified chunk critical section. It makes no client/server equivalence claim and assigns no unproved entity ownership policy to the numeric source kinds.
