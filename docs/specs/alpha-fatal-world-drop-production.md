# Fatal inventory-unit world-drop production

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD.

This independently authored contract closes the ordinary inventory-unit route at the end of a fatal-loot helper, together with its nested world-drop production helper. It does not close every fatal-loot branch. The supported original images are `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, and `binary/Server.exe`, SHA-256 `1ffbcbce251e1506a63c9ce18ab0009d5553fcbb9e6202be2bc3a7d870d34fb1`. Both use preferred base `0x00400000`. Addresses below are RVAs; evidence paths are relative to `Documents/disassembled alpha cube world`. Original instructions and individually identified original files supplied the evidence; no prior contract, reconstruction, implementation or runtime findings were used.

## Fatal call and inventory admission

In the client hit routine, the target pointer is obtained from the world creature-map entry selected using hit-record bytes `+0x08–+0x0F`; the entry's value at node `+0x18` supplies the actor pointer. On the inspected ordinary fatal branch, the post-application binary32 health at actor `+0x16C` is at or below zero, is stored as zero, and preceding fatal state/output work occurs before the call to `0x19B180` at `0x19622E`. The server has the independently verified zero-health write and call at `0x0CF24E`. The complete upstream hit admission and earlier fatal effects are outside this contract. The inventory route receives the actual actor pointer; it does not enumerate by a creature-map key or replace that pointer with a lookup by the actor's own ID. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195AAB–0x195AF7`, `0x196133–0x19622E`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0CF153–0x0CF24E`.]

Both fatal-loot helpers first reject actor byte `+0x60` equal to 0 or 3, or a set `0x0800` bit in the 16-bit word at actor `+0x7E`. After admission they make an explicit discarded random call and execute earlier equipment/generated/material/currency branches. Those earlier branches can consume variable draws and invoke other producers; their full effects and draw totals are deliberately not closed here. The inventory-unit branch later requires actor byte `+0x60` exactly 1. Actor kind, class/specialization and own identifier do not add another test inside that inventory branch. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19B1C0–0x19B1EE`, `0x19C163–0x19C167`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2B20–0x0D2B4E`, `0x0D3AC3–0x0D3AC7`; `decompiled/client/functions/0059b180.c`, `decompiled/server/functions/004d2ae0.c`.]

Inventory outer begin/end pointers are actor `+0x11DC/+0x11E0`. Their nonempty range and positive signed element count admit traversal of all groups in ascending order, using 12-byte group descriptors. Each group uses its first two pointer words as entry begin/end, traversed in ascending order with `0x11C`-byte entries. An entry consists of a 32-bit quantity followed by its item at entry `+4`. Item category must be nonzero and quantity must be signed-positive. Zero and negative quantities produce no unit attempts. For each admitted entry a signed unit counter starts at zero and advances until it reaches the quantity reread from that entry. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19C16D–0x19C1F7`, `0x19C329–0x19C39F`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D3ACD–0x0D3B57`, `0x0D3C89–0x0D3CFF`.]

Category 1 with subtype 1 is excluded at the unit branch. Its counter still advances, but that iteration performs no placement draws and calls no drop helper. Every other admitted unit supplies the same entry item's address to the nested helper. The loop does not decrement, clear, erase, normalize or copy back the inventory quantity/item. It rereads the entry quantity, group end and outer group storage during traversal; validity under concurrent mutation, reallocation or callbacks is not established. Ordinary conclusions assume those ranges remain valid. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19C200–0x19C20F`, `0x19C318–0x19C39F`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D3B60–0x0D3B6F`, `0x0D3C78–0x0D3CFF`.]

## Per-unit supplied placement and random order

Each nonexcluded unit first makes an X draw, then a Y draw. Each signed 32-bit result is converted to binary32; it is multiplied by binary32 2, divided by binary32 32767, then subtracted from binary32 1. Z offset is binary32 positive zero. There is no normalization of the horizontal vector. X, Y and Z are then processed in order: multiply each binary32 offset by 65536, store to binary32, load to x87, and call the original conversion helper to obtain a signed 64-bit integer truncated toward zero for ordinary finite values in this bounded range. Those three integers are added, using low-word addition and carry into the high word, to the actor's three raw 64-bit coordinates beginning at `+0x10`. These are current position reads for that attempt, not the separate cached `+0x1B0/+0x1B4/+0x1B8` fields used by other fatal work. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19C20F–0x19C2E5`, conversion `0x28D946–0x28D9BA`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D3B6F–0x0D3C45`, conversion `0x14A946–0x14A9BA`.]

The client constant locations are RVA `0x345DC0` for 1 (`0x3F800000`), `0x345E10` for 2 (`0x40000000`), `0x2FD338` for 32767 (`0x46FFFE00`), `0x2FCE7C` for 65536 (`0x47800000`) and `0x2FCE6C` for 360 (`0x43B40000`). The server independently supplies those same bit patterns at RVAs `0x1586DC`, `0x158708`, `0x158834`, `0x15878C` and `0x158780` respectively. Multiplication, division and subtraction are separate scalar binary32 operations. The x87 conversion helper's integer correction is relevant; the initial rounding instruction alone is not the final conversion rule. Floating exception/control behavior outside ordinary finite representable inputs remains unclosed. [Evidence: both original images, stated constant bytes; the placement/conversion intervals above.]

After forming the three raw coordinate sums, a third caller draw is converted to binary32, multiplied by 360 and divided by 32767. The nested call receives, in order, item address, address of the raw 64-bit triplet, that binary32 angle-like scalar, binary32 `0.75` (`0x3F400000`), and the caller's supplied collection pointer; world receiver is in ECX. The actor's own ID and map key are not supplied as drop-record fields by this call. The third caller draw is consumed even though the nested helper subsequently replaces its stored angle-like field on a successful initial residency lookup. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19C2BD–0x19C31E`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D3C1D–0x0D3C7E`.]

## Nested record construction and first residency lookup

The nested helpers construct a local `0x148`-byte record with an item at its start. Before any residency lookup, they copy the supplied raw triplet, store the supplied angle-like scalar, and multiply the constructor's binary32 scalar at record `+0x134`, bits `0x3D924925`, by the supplied 0.75. This is one binary32 multiplication; under round-to-nearest the resulting bits are `0x3D5B6DB8`. They next make one random call, store signed remainder modulo 300 plus 500 at record `+0x13C`, and read the receiver's 32-bit `+0x800160` into record `+0x144`. That read is after the random call and before residency lookup. Record `+0x140` stays constructor-zero, and byte `+0x138` stays zero. The complete meaning of those scalar/timing-like members is not inferred. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AEB0–0x19AF5D`, constructor `0x04A970–0x04A9E8`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2810–0x0D28BD`; `decompiled/server/functions/0041d8d0.c`, RVA `0x01D8D0`.]

Initial residency is obtained from the raw Y coordinate first, then X: signed 64-bit division by 65536 truncates toward zero, only the low 32-bit quotient is retained, and that signed 32-bit value is divided by 256 with truncation toward zero. X and Y results are passed to the resident lookup. This is the original two-stage truncation boundary, not an asserted arithmetic shift of the raw 64-bit position. Lookup takes no Z argument. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AF42–0x19AFA2`, signed division helper `0x28D9E0`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D28A2–0x0D2902`; `decompiled/server/functions/0054aa30.c`, RVA `0x14AA30`.]

The client lookup `0x034D10` and server lookup `0x006290` reject signed horizontal lookup components outside 0 through 65535. Their parent lookup rejects components outside 0 through 1023 and reads the receiver's pointer grid at `+0xBC`, with first-component significance in the `first × 1024 + second` index. A nonnull parent supplies a pointer from `+0x10018`, indexed by `first-remainder-modulo-64 × 64 + second-remainder-modulo-64`. These are read-only pointer lookups in the inspected helpers; they do not load, create or retry missing residency. Broader population and lifetime are unclosed. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00434d10.c`, `00434a90.c`, RVAs `0x034D10/0x034A90`; `decompiled/server/functions/00406290.c`, `00406210.c`, RVAs `0x006290/0x006210`; original helper calls in both nested bodies.]

If this initial result is null, the nested helper returns immediately. It has already consumed its modulo-300 draw and read receiver `+0x800160`, but it has not copied the supplied item into the record, made its angle replacement draw, appended a resident record or inserted a collection key. The outer unit counter still advances; no retry or inventory restoration is performed by this path. Thus a nonexcluded attempted unit consumes four direct random calls when initial residency is absent. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AF95–0x19AFB9`, `0x19B168–0x19B178`, `0x19C323–0x19C33E`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D28F5–0x0D2919`, `0x0D2AC8–0x0D2AD8`, `0x0D3C83–0x0D3C9E`.]

## Successful residency, item copy and vertical scan

After a nonnull initial result, the item is assigned semantically into the record, preserving its category/subtype, 32-bit `+0x04/+0x08`, bytes `+0x0C/+0x0D/+0x0E`, raw level word `+0x10`, all 32 eight-byte extension entries and 32-bit extension count `+0x114`. No quantity is supplied, no item-level normalization occurs, and no count clamp is added by the drop helper. Item bytes `+0x02/+0x03`, `+0x0F`, `+0x12/+0x13` remain unspecified by the semantic copy. A new random call then replaces record `+0x130` with binary32 draw-times-360 divided by 32767. The earlier supplied value no longer determines that field, but its caller draw remains consumed. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AFA8–0x19AFDC`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042c5e0.c`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2908–0x0D293C`; `decompiled/server/functions/00402a70.c`.]

The scan's starting signed 32-bit vertical integer is the low quotient of supplied signed-64 Z divided by 65536, with one additional decrement whenever the raw signed-64 Z is negative. That decrement also occurs for an exact negative multiple; it is not generally equivalent to floor division. Signed-64 Y and then X are divided by 65536, retaining low 32-bit quotients for the horizontal scan coordinates. The scan changes only its vertical integer. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AFE1–0x19B034`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2941–0x0D2994`.]

At every iteration, horizontal integers outside 0 through `0xFFFFFF` select a fallback record. Otherwise they are divided by 256 toward zero and the resident lookup is repeated. A missing repeated lookup also selects fallback. A present lookup supplies a 32-byte column descriptor at its `+0xA8` base with index `second-remainder-modulo-256 × 256 + first-remainder-modulo-256`. A zero computed column address or scan integer below column signed base `+0x10` selects fallback. If the scan integer is below base plus signed count `+0x1C`, its four-byte value is obtained through the column service. Above that extent, a different fallback is selected according to whether the scan integer is positive. Overflow, corrupt column extents and invalid backing pointers are not repaired by these branches. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19B036–0x19B0E7`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042f730.c`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2996–0x0D2A47`; `decompiled/server/functions/00405f20.c`.]

For an in-range column value whose fourth byte's low five bits are zero, scan integer at or below zero and a clear `0x40` bit replace that value with the low-height fallback. The final low-five-bit classification is then tested: 0 or 2 decrements the signed scan integer and repeats; every other value terminates. There is no maximum scan length. Original initializer routines establish the relevant fallback fourth bytes as `0x00`, `0x82` and `0x01`: the positive-above-extent value continues with class 0, the low-height value continues with class 2, and missing/below-base fallback terminates with class 1. These are numeric classifications, not substituted environmental names. Their initialization execution, later ambient mutation and malformed nontermination remain explicit limits. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19B0CF–0x19B11F`, initializers `0x2FAAD0`, `0x2FAB00`, `0x2FAB30`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2A2F–0x0D2A7F`, initializers `0x157110`, `0x157140`, `0x157170`; corresponding original initializer decompilations.]

## Record append and collection publication

On termination, Z is replaced by signed-32 scan-integer-plus-one, sign-extended and multiplied by 65536 into the raw 64-bit record coordinate. X and Y retain their supplied raw values. The first nonnull residency pointer, saved before the item copy and scan, supplies the append receiver at resident `+0x30`. A later repeated lookup's result is not substituted as the append owner. In particular, missing residency encountered during the scan selects the terminating fallback under the initialized values; it does not take the initial-null early return or undo the saved first pointer. Pointer freshness and synchronization are not guaranteed here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AF9A`, `0x19B104–0x19B140`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D28FA`, `0x0D2A64–0x0D2AA0`.]

The `0x148`-byte resident entry contains the semantic item; raw position at `+0x118/+0x120/+0x128`; replacement binary32 angle-like scalar at `+0x130`; scaled binary32 member `+0x134`; zero byte `+0x138`; modulo-300-plus-500 integer `+0x13C`; zero integer `+0x140`; and receiver clock-field snapshot `+0x144`. Its copy helper transfers these members in that order after the semantic item copy. Record padding `+0x139–+0x13B` is not established. Resident append adds the entry at the current end and advances that end by `0x148`, with capacity/allocation as supplied services. It does not insert the source inventory quantity into this record. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0044d590.c`, `0044a8c0.c`, `0044a970.c`; `decompiled/server/functions/0041f5b0.c`, `0041d820.c`, `0041d8d0.c`; the verified nested producer writes above.]

Only after resident append returns does the helper insert the saved resident pointer into the caller-supplied collection. The insertion compares that pointer's 32-bit representation unsigned, retaining an existing equal key rather than adding another. The key itself is copied into the new node at `+0x10`; it is not an actor ID, entry key, item ID or a separately serialized drop event. Equal-resident key coalescing does not suppress resident drop records already appended. The additional byte forwarded from client global RVA `0x36B7CA` or server `0x18423E` is passed through this insertion boundary; no per-event payload or transport send is established by that argument. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19B140–0x19B163`, `0x18E7D0–0x18E8D0`; `decompiled/client/functions/004c9580.c`, `0062fbc0.c`; `binary/Server.exe`, RVAs `0x0D2AA0–0x0D2AC3`; `decompiled/server/functions/004da680.c`, `004c7180.c`, `004d9b50.c`.]

The collection pointer is forwarded from the hit caller through the fatal helper to each nested unit attempt, independently of the separate output aggregate used by preceding fatal effects. The nested helper does not clear it. On a successful ordinary attempt the sequence is therefore resident entry append, then pointer-key insertion, then outer unit-counter advance. No rollback after a failing allocation/insertion is established, and no atomicity is implied. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195A96–0x195AA5`, `0x196221–0x19622E`, `0x19B1AE–0x19B1BA`, `0x19C2BD–0x19C33E`, `0x19B11F–0x19B178`; the independently inspected server call at `0x0CF24E`, unit and nested-helper intervals above.]

## Limits and random accounting

For each nonexcluded inventory unit, the direct order is caller X draw, caller Y draw, caller angle draw, nested modulo-300 draw, initial residency lookup, item semantic copy, and—only on nonnull residency—nested replacement-angle draw. The scan and final append/key calls contain no additional direct random call in their inspected game bodies. Hence an ordinary attempt consumes four draws on initial-null residency and five on initial-nonnull residency, provided its later services complete. Excluded category-1/subtype-1 units consume none of these draws. Both images reach the named `MSVCR110.dll!rand` import, client IAT RVA `0x2FC468`, server IAT RVA `0x1582F4`. No per-unit seed, reseed or random-state restoration is performed here. Earlier fatal-loot branches prevent deriving a total draw count for an entire fatal actor from this bounded route. [Evidence: the original caller/unit/nested bodies and import slots cited above.]

This contract closes the supplied-item unit path and its ordinary arithmetic, query, copying and publication boundaries. It does not close earlier equipment/item generators, total fatal loot, inventory construction, all generic hit admissions, region/column population and concurrent lifetime, floating exceptions, allocator/security/runtime failures, collection retention/clear schedules, later dropped-item simulation, pickup or network serialization/forwarding. No whole-frame guarantee or end-to-end client/server transport relationship follows from the independently verified local routes. The original installation and all earlier held artifacts remain untouched. [Evidence scope: `binary/Cube.exe` and `binary/Server.exe`, the identified functions and individually cited original files only.]
