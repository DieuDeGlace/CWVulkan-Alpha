# Alpha client coordinate scalar producers

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD. This original-only contract covers client scalar producers at RVAs `0x1C4DD0` and `0x1C4800`, their shared scalar arithmetic, and the numeric path directly reached by the latter's conditional correction. The results are called the first and second scalar below. No environmental meaning, physical unit, or complementary relationship is assigned to them.

The supported original image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, under the evidence root `disassembled alpha cube world`. All addresses below are RVAs; the image base is `0x00400000`. Evidence paths are relative to that root. Exported function listings supplied leads only; the original executable's instructions and stored data independently established parameter types, return values, arithmetic order, and constants. No reconstruction or other contract was used.

## Inputs, outputs, and data dependencies

Both producers act on an owner object passed as the call receiver and take two stack arguments, both **signed 32-bit integer coordinates**, called X (first argument) and Y (second argument) here. Neither coordinate is a binary32 input. The floating-point type that the decompiled listing shows for one parameter of RVA `0x1C4800` does not describe the native call: the executable loads each argument as a four-byte integer and uses signed integer conversions. Each producer removes its two arguments on return and returns a binary32 scalar loaded onto the x87 stack. The decompiled listings' empty return types and missing final arithmetic do not describe the return behavior. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005c4dd0.c`, RVA `0x1C4DD0`; `005c4800.c`, RVA `0x1C4800`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4DEC–0x1C4F0B`, `0x1C481B–0x1C4951`, `0x1C5262–0x1C529C`, and `0x1C4CA7–0x1C4DC7`.]

The shared sampling source is a two-dimensional table of four-byte pointers beginning at owner `+0x4000BC`. There are 1024 pointer positions per row; the row is selected by the first-axis (X-derived) index and the position within the row by the second-axis (Y-derived) index. Each nonnull pointed record supplies signed four-byte coordinates at record `+0x00` (first axis) and `+0x04` (second axis). The first producer, RVA `0x1C4DD0`, samples a **binary32 value at record `+0x10`**. The second, RVA `0x1C4800`, samples a **binary32 value at record `+0x0C`**. Those scalar loads reinterpret the stored four bytes as binary32; they do not numerically convert a 32-bit integer. Record `+0x08` is not a scalar input to these two routines. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, table accesses at `0x1C4F7A–0x1C4F8A` and `0x1C49BD–0x1C49C7`; coordinate loads and sign extension at `0x1C4F90–0x1C5004`; scalar loads and products at `0x1C5225–0x1C5235` and `0x1C4C68–0x1C4C78`.]

Neither producer populates that table, generates its record coordinates or scalar attributes, reads a world-seed field directly, or writes a cached result. Their output nevertheless depends on the currently populated pointers and pointed values. A stable result requires stable supplied data as well as the numeric execution conditions described below.

## Shared coordinate warp and candidate order

The original integer coordinates determine an inclusive rectangular candidate window. For each axis, its lower endpoint is the signed quotient of the coordinate minus 16384 by 16384, and its upper endpoint is the signed quotient of the coordinate plus 16384 by 16384. The addition or subtraction occurs in wrapping 32-bit arithmetic first. The native sign correction and arithmetic shift implement division truncated toward zero, including for negative values; this is not floor division. Coordinate overflow is not saturated before the division. The window is determined from the original coordinates before warping and is not adjusted afterwards. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4DFA–0x1C4E65` and `0x1C4826–0x1C4891`.]

The warp makes two calls to the deterministic two-input helper at RVA `0x0C0EF0`, in this order:

1. The first argument is Y converted exactly from signed 32-bit integer to binary64 and multiplied in binary64 by the stored approximation to `0.0005`; the second argument is binary64 `3423`. The helper's binary32 result is multiplied by binary32 `3` and then by binary32 `256`. This product is the displacement for X.
2. The first argument is X converted to binary64 and multiplied by the same stored coefficient; the second argument is binary64 `23421`. The binary32 result is multiplied by `3` and then by `256`. This product is the displacement for Y.

Each original coordinate is separately converted from signed integer to binary32, its displacement is added in binary32, and the sum is converted to a signed 32-bit integer by truncation toward zero, giving the warped X and warped Y. The warp therefore couples the axes and includes binary32 rounding of the coordinate before the integer result; it is not an integer addition of an independently truncated displacement. Both helper calls occur before any candidate is validated, even if the later scan returns the fallback. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4E34–0x1C4F0B` and `0x1C483E–0x1C4951`.]

The candidate scan visits increasing first-axis index, with increasing second-axis index inside each first-axis value. Endpoints are inclusive. At any visited candidate, a negative first-axis or second-axis index, a first-axis index above 1023, a second-axis index above 1023, or a null table entry immediately ends the routine with the fallback. The routines neither skip such an entry nor clamp the window to valid table bounds. An empty window, or a scan in which no record was selected, also returns the fallback. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4F12–0x1C50B6` and `0x1C4957–0x1C4AF6`.]

## Distance, weights, and the two scalar relations

For each candidate and each axis, the producer sign-extends the record coordinate and the warped coordinate to 64 bits, scales each by 65536 with a 16-bit left shift, and subtracts the warped value from the record value in 64-bit arithmetic. It converts that signed 64-bit difference to binary32 through an x87 integer load followed by a single-precision store, then multiplies by binary32 `1/65536` in SSE. Each scaled component is squared in binary32, the two squares are added in binary32, and the sum is truncated to a signed 32-bit integer. That integer is the distance value used for selection and weighting. In the ordinary finite, representable case it is a truncated squared separation; no square root is taken. Performing the division by 65536 before the binary32 narrowing is not established as equivalent. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4F90–0x1C5058` and `0x1C49CD–0x1C4A98`; repeated second-pass arithmetic at `0x1C512F–0x1C51F1` and `0x1C4B89–0x1C4C2E`.]

The first pass retains the smallest distance by **signed** 32-bit comparison. The first visited candidate initializes the selection regardless of its distance. A later candidate replaces it only for a strictly smaller distance, so equal distances keep the earlier candidate. Only the minimum distance value is carried into the second pass; neither routine simply returns the nearest record's scalar. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C5058–0x1C5071` and `0x1C4A98–0x1C4AB7`.]

The second pass repeats the same ordered candidate scan, including the same validity checks, and recomputes each distance from the current record data. It subtracts the first-pass minimum with wrapping 32-bit subtraction, converts that signed difference to binary32, and multiplies by the stored binary32 approximation to `0.0000005`. The product is replaced by 1 only when the comparison is ordered and the product exceeds 1. The candidate's weight is 1 minus the retained product, in binary32. There is no separate lower clamp on the product and no upper clamp on the weight. With ordinary consistent distances the nearest distance receives weight 1 and sufficiently larger distances receive weight zero; the exact native arithmetic, including overflow and conversion limits, takes precedence over that description. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C51F1–0x1C5221` and `0x1C4C2E–0x1C4C64`.]

Both accumulations start at positive zero. In scan order, the weight is added to the denominator with one binary32 addition, then the record's binary32 scalar is multiplied by the weight and the product is added to the numerator with one binary32 addition. The product is evaluated even when the weight is zero. When the denominator compares ordered greater than zero, a binary32 division of numerator by denominator gives the weighted scalar. Otherwise, including an unordered comparison, the fallback is returned. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C50BC–0x1C50D8`, `0x1C5217–0x1C5270`, `0x1C4AFC–0x1C4B1B`, and `0x1C4C5A–0x1C4CC0`.]

For the **first scalar**, the weighted value of record `+0x10` is the final normal result. For the **second scalar**, the weighted value of record `+0x0C` is the base result and may receive the correction below. Both producers use binary32 **0.5**, bits `0x3F000000`, as their fallback. There is no unconditional final clamp of either weighted value to the interval from zero to one. The two routines are not established as complements, and their distinct record attributes cannot be substituted for one another. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, first return `0x1C5262–0x1C529C`; second return `0x1C4CA7–0x1C4DC7`.]

## Conditional correction to the second scalar

After producing a positive-denominator weighted value, RVA `0x1C4800` calls the lookup helper at RVA `0x087DA0` on the same owner. Its two arguments are the original, unwarped X and Y, each divided by 2048 with truncation toward zero, X first. The lookup is called before the weighted value is tested against the correction threshold. It returns null when either lookup coordinate is outside the signed range 0 through 8191. For valid coordinates it reads, through RVA `0x034A90`, the owner pointer table at `+0xBC` with 1024 pointers per row, the row chosen by the X lookup coordinate divided by eight and the position by the Y lookup coordinate divided by eight. A null pointer gives a null result. For a nonnull coarse record it returns the address of a subrecord inside it: subrecords have a stride of `0x68` bytes, begin at coarse record `+0x14018`, and are indexed by the X remainder modulo eight times eight plus the Y remainder modulo eight. Nothing is allocated or created on this path. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4CC0–0x1C4CEB`, `0x087DA0–0x087E1F`, and `0x034A90–0x034ABF`; `decompiled/client/functions/00487da0.c` and `00434a90.c`.]

Correction occurs only when the base value compares **ordered below** binary32 `0.2` (bits `0x3E4CCCCD`), the lookup returned a nonnull subrecord, and that subrecord's four-byte tag at `+0x18` equals 3. The producer then forms the original X and Y, each sign-extended and scaled by 65536 into a signed 64-bit value held by the caller, and passes the addresses of those two values (X first, Y second) to the numeric helper at RVA `0x1F8E10`, with the subrecord as receiver. The first scalar makes neither this lookup nor this helper call. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4CF0–0x1C4D50`.]

The helper's result is stored to binary32. The producer subtracts it from 1 in binary32. If that difference compares ordered at or below zero, the contribution is positive zero; otherwise, including an unordered comparison, the contribution is the difference squared in binary32. The contribution is multiplied by binary32 `0.3` (bits `0x3E99999A`) and the base value is added in binary32. A corrected result that compares ordered above 1 is replaced with exactly 1. An unordered corrected result is kept, and there is no lower clamp. If any admission condition fails, the base value is returned unchanged. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C4D55–0x1C4D9B`.]

For the tag-3 path reached here, RVA `0x1F8E10` uses signed 64-bit reference coordinates at subrecord `+0x00` (first axis) and `+0x08` (second axis), a binary32 divisor parameter at `+0x10`, and the tag at `+0x18`. If the divisor parameter compares ordered below binary32 `0.001` (bits `0x3A83126F`), the helper returns zero immediately; equality and an unordered comparison continue. The helper has separate branches for tags 11, 12 and 14 and for tag 13; every other tag value, including 3, takes the branch described here. Because the caller requires tag 3, those other branches are not reached by this producer and are not specified. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1F8E10–0x1F8E66`; `decompiled/client/functions/005f8e10.c`.]

The reachable tag-3 arithmetic has these established dependencies and order:

1. The supplied 64-bit Y value is loaded into x87 and stored to binary64, multiplied in binary64 by `-0.0025`, and converted to a signed 64-bit integer by the runtime helper at RVA `0x28D946`. The supplied X value is then treated the same way. These conversions truncate toward zero for finite representable inputs. The results are called the Y offset and X offset.
2. For the first lattice call, the first binary64 argument is the 64-bit difference `0x00000080AD580000` minus the X offset, converted to binary64 through x87 and multiplied in binary64 by `1/65536`. The second argument is `0x00000001617D0000` minus the Y offset, converted and scaled the same way.
3. The returned binary32 value is multiplied in binary32 by `256` and then by `65536`, and the runtime helper converts it to a signed 64-bit integer. The subrecord reference X is subtracted from this integer and the supplied X value is added, both in wrapping 64-bit arithmetic. The resulting signed difference is narrowed to binary32 through x87 and multiplied by binary32 `1/65536`.
4. For the second lattice call, the first argument is `0x000000000D5F0000` minus the X offset and the second is `0x0000000000700000` minus the Y offset, each converted to binary64 and multiplied by `1/65536`. The result is multiplied by `256` and then `65536` and converted to a signed 64-bit integer. The reference Y is subtracted and the supplied Y value added in 64-bit arithmetic, and the difference is narrowed to binary32 and multiplied by `1/65536`.
5. The scaled X and Y differences are each squared in binary32 and the two squares are added in binary32. The divisor parameter is squared separately in binary32, the sum is divided by that square in binary32, and the binary32 quotient is returned on the x87 stack.

[Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, reachable branch RVA `0x1F9092–0x1F92B5`; runtime conversion helper RVA `0x28D946–0x28D9BA`; `decompiled/client/functions/005f8e10.c` and `0068d946.c`.]

The helper does not clamp this normalized squared value. Its integer operations use native wrapping 64-bit results; exceptional or overflowing conversions are not given a replacement geometric meaning. No environmental name is assigned to the tag-3 subrecord, its divisor parameter, or the resulting correction.

## Shared deterministic lattice helper

RVA `0x0C0EF0` takes two binary64 values on the stack (the caller removes them), not a world pointer or mutable random-state object. Each value is truncated to a signed 32-bit integer. For each axis, the next corner index is formed by adding binary64 1 to the converted base index and truncating again; for an index of `0x7FFFFFFF` this yields the integer-indefinite pattern `0x80000000` when the invalid exception is masked, which equals the wrapped value. The fractional part on each axis is the binary64 value minus its truncated index, so negative inputs produce negative fractions measured from the truncated index, not from a floor. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0C0EF0–0x0C0F77`; `decompiled/client/functions/004c0ef0.c`.]

Four corner values are produced from the corner index pairs (base first-axis, base second-axis), (next first-axis, base second-axis), (base first-axis, next second-axis) and (next first-axis, next second-axis). For each pair the helper adds the first-axis index to 57 times the second-axis index, then exclusive-ors the sum with itself shifted left by 13 bits. It squares that mixed value, multiplies by `0x0000EC4D`, adds `0x0131071F`, multiplies by the mixed value again, and adds `0xD208DD0D` (the image subtracts `0x2DF722F3`, which is the same modulo 2^32); every integer step keeps the low 32 bits. The final value is masked with `0x7FFFFFFF`, converted to binary64, multiplied by the exact binary64 value `1/1073741824`, and subtracted from binary64 1. This is deterministic coordinate hashing, not a random draw or seed update. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0C0F16–0x0C0F77` and `0x0C0FC1–0x0C10D4`.]

Each axis fraction is multiplied in binary64 by the stored approximation `3.1415927`. The helper calls `MSVCR110.DLL!_libm_sse2_cos_precise`, through the import thunk at RVA `0x28D7A4`, first on the scaled first-axis fraction and then on the scaled second-axis fraction. Each axis coefficient is 1 minus the returned binary64 cosine, multiplied by binary64 0.5. The two corners sharing the base second-axis index are blended as the first corner times one minus the first-axis coefficient plus the second corner times the first-axis coefficient; the two corners sharing the next second-axis index are blended the same way. The first blend is multiplied by one minus the second-axis coefficient, the second blend by the second-axis coefficient, and the two products are added. Every interpolation step is a separate binary64 operation in that product-then-addition order. The binary64 result is narrowed to binary32 by an x87 single-precision store and reloaded for return. No reassociation, fused operation, floor-based neighbor selection, or deferred narrowing is established. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0C0F54–0x0C10F9`; import table entry used by RVA `0x28D7A4`; `decompiled/client/external_functions.csv`.]

Narrowing of signed 64-bit integers to binary32 or binary64, and the lattice helper's final narrowing, use x87 loads and stores under the ambient x87 controls. Signed 32-bit-to-floating conversions and the producers' scalar arithmetic use SSE; float-to-signed-32-bit conversions explicitly truncate. The runtime signed-64-bit conversion helper rounds with the x87 integer store under the current mode and then corrects by one toward zero according to the sign of the residual; it does not install a private x87 control word. Ambient x87 precision, rounding and exception controls, SSE rounding and denormal controls, and the imported cosine's behavior remain execution conditions. No routine covered here establishes a private numeric environment. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, the conversion ranges cited above and RVA `0x28D946–0x28D9BA`.]

There is no distance-range saturation. A masked invalid SSE conversion, including a squared-distance sum outside the signed 32-bit range, produces the integer-indefinite pattern `0x80000000`; later signed comparisons and 32-bit subtraction act on those bits. Unmasked exceptions can prevent completion. NaN sample scalars are not filtered, and multiplying one by a zero weight still evaluates a floating-point product. This contract does not extend the ordinary weighted-average interpretation to malformed pointed data or exceptional numeric inputs.

## Adjacent cached-value boundary

Adjacent wrappers at RVAs `0x1C8310` and `0x1C8350` take X, Y and an optional third pointer argument, and first call the lookup at RVA `0x0347A0` with all three. If it returns a nonnull cell address, they return the binary32 value at cell `+0x04` (wrapper `0x1C8310`, second scalar) or cell `+0x08` (wrapper `0x1C8350`, first scalar). Only a null lookup result leads to a call of the corresponding producer with X and Y. They do not recompute or validate a nonnull cell's stored value. These cell fields belong to a different record from the producers' sample records. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x1C8310–0x1C837F`; `decompiled/client/functions/005c8310.c` and `005c8350.c`.]

That cell lookup accepts only X and Y from zero up to but excluding `0x01000000`. Without the optional pointer it locates a container through RVA `0x034D10` using the coordinates divided by 256; that routine in turn reads the owner table at `+0xBC` through RVA `0x034A90` with those values divided by 64, and selects a container pointer at coarse record `+0x10018` indexed by the X remainder modulo 64 times 64 plus the Y remainder modulo 64. With the pointer supplied it instead requires X and Y to lie within the 256-by-256 extent starting at 256 times the object's four-byte values at `+0x60` and `+0x64`. A nonnull container's pointer at `+0xA8` addresses 32-byte cells indexed by the Y remainder modulo 256 times 256 plus the X remainder modulo 256. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0347A0–0x03486F` and `0x034D10–0x034D80`; `decompiled/client/functions/004347a0.c`, `00434d10.c`, and `00434a90.c`.]

## Random draws and call counts

There are **zero random draws or seed mutations** in the two producers, the shared lattice helper, the reachable tag-3 path, and the inspected lookup and cache-read routines. Each producer makes two lattice calls, and each lattice call makes two imported cosine calls. An admitted tag-3 correction whose divisor passes its initial threshold adds two lattice calls, four cosine calls, and four calls to the signed-64-bit conversion helper. Candidate count does not change these counts because candidate sampling calls no random generator. This statement does not cover sample or cell construction, unrelated owner updates, other tags of RVA `0x1F8E10`, or library internals. [Evidence: original routines and native call sites cited throughout this contract.]
