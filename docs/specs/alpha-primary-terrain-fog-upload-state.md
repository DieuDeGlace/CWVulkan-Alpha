# Alpha primary terrain fog upload state

Status: **FINAL / STABLE / HELD**. Source-author freeze: 2026-11-17.

This contract covers the state that supplies the original Alpha client's primary terrain fog distance and gradient vertex constants. It distinguishes the frame update, the smoothed camera angle, and the map-mode selector. It does not cover the complete shader, all render passes, or every producer of world or input state.

## Evidence and identity

The source-author policy and project rules were read before evidence inspection. Findings are from original client PE bytes, original disassembly, and individually selected original decompiled routines. No replacement engine, exporter, SDK, tests, logs, captures, implementation handoff, or Git history were inspected. There was no native execution. Evidence paths are relative to the `disassembled alpha cube world` root.

The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`: 3,885,568 bytes, PE machine `0x014C` (x86), preferred image base `0x00400000`, PE timestamp `0x51EA955E`, image size `0x003BC000`, SHA-256 `84A7A132A84D4282338E7EA45A1940D32066D64E39CBAFED8F2418D5A6DC30BF`. Routine evidence is under `decompiled/client/functions`; address-bearing instructions are in `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`. All addresses in this document are RVAs within the supported client unless explicitly marked as object offsets or register numbers.

The initializer at `0x059C40` assigns the client virtual table at `0x2FFDC8`. Its slots at byte offsets `+0x28` and `+0x2C` resolve to update `0x088EE0` and draw `0x0AC260`. This identifies the receiving object independently of other objects that happen to use identically numbered offsets. The fields studied here are client binary32 distance at `+0x1D4`, client binary32 current angle at `+0x1A4`, and a one-byte selector at `+0x8006E4`.

## Frame time and shared weight

The frame caller `0x0C85F0` requires a non-null graphics device and client. It reads the 32-bit millisecond clock through the original by-name `WINMM.dll!timeGetTime` import at IAT RVA `0x2FC5C8`. The previous sample is a 32-bit value at RVA `0x36B234`. A previous value of zero is treated as an unset sample and replaced with the current sample before taking the difference. The update argument at `0x0C8633` is the low 32 bits of current minus previous, without a caller-side numerical clamp. The actual current sample is retained at `0x0C86E6` before draw dispatch at `0x0C86EC`. In particular, consecutive samples that cross the ordinary unsigned clock rollover can still yield a small positive delta.

The fog and angle paths both pass that argument to the weight operation at `0x0AC150`. It interprets the bits as a **signed** 32-bit count. A zero or negative count leaves the weight at positive zero. A positive count applies one binary64 approach-to-one step per millisecond. The initial weight is zero; each step adds to the current weight the product of the supplied coefficient and one minus the current weight. The binary32 coefficient is widened exactly before this work; each binary64 subtraction, multiplication and addition rounds separately. The final weight is stored as binary32 and returned by the x87 interface. An input with its sign bit set therefore does not become billions of steps. This is a rounded recurrence, and replacing it with a single exponentiation is not an established equivalence.

## Fog distance producer

The initializer sets client `+0x1D4` to positive zero at `0x059E9A`. The two confirmed active-client frame stores are the blended distance at `0x089316` and the floor overwrite at `0x089368`. The update section begins near `0x089020` under the world critical section reached through `0x201CB0` from the embedded world at client `+0x2E4`.

The target derives from the nearest relevant hole in the current render-chunk square, bounded by the nominal fog distance. The original operations at `0x089034`–`0x0892C9` establish the following:

1. The initial squared candidate is the binary32 square of client `+0x1D0`. The initializer gives this nominal distance by multiplying the signed chunk-window width at `+0x2DC`, converted to binary32, by 0.5 and then 32, with separate binary32 rounding. The traced constructor width is 40 (`0x059F39`), giving nominal distance 640 at `0x05AB36`.
2. The signed horizontal chunk square starts at the coordinates at `+0x2AC` and `+0x2B0`, with side length `+0x2DC`; X increases outside the increasing Y traversal. Coordinates outside 0 through `0x7FFFF` inclusive on either axis do not produce candidates. For qualifying coordinates, the slot comes from the array at client `+0x2E0`, with a 616-byte stride and local X varying fastest. Slot coordinates are computed by the remainders of the world chunk coordinates divided by the window width.
3. A slot with zero dword at slot `+0x0C` is a candidate. If that dword is nonzero, the slot is a candidate only when its two signed coordinate words at `+0x18` and `+0x1C` differ from the requested chunk pair. A populated slot with a matching pair is skipped. The equality and inequality helpers for the two dwords are at `0x068840` and `0x0688A0`.
4. For a candidate `(X,Y)`, on each horizontal axis the fixed-point sample is the center of its 32-block chunk: coordinate times 32 plus 16 blocks. The raw signed-64 position uses 65,536 raw units per block. The calculation subtracts the client's corresponding signed-64 position at `+0x140` and `+0x148`, converts each raw difference to binary32, and scales each by the binary32 value 2^-16 at `0x2FCD94`. It squares the two differences separately and sums the squares in binary32. Z does not enter this comparison. An ordered strictly smaller squared result replaces the current candidate; a tie leaves it alone.
5. The selected binary32 square is widened to binary64 at `0x0892B4`; the square root uses the `_libm_sse2_sqrt_precise` import through thunk `0x28D9C8` and IAT RVA `0x2FC460`. Its result is narrowed to binary32, then the binary32 constant with bits `0x41B33333` (approximately 22.39999962, data RVA `0x302A94`) is subtracted. This is the target distance before smoothing. The target itself is not clamped to the floor at this stage.

The fog coefficient passed at `0x0892D9` is binary32 `0x3A83126F` (nominally 0.001). If its frame weight is `w`, the stored candidate is the sum of the old distance multiplied by one minus `w` and the target multiplied by `w`. The complement, both products, and the sum are separate binary32 operations. No exponential approximation, fused multiply-add, or replacement of the signed frame-time argument with an unsigned count is established by these instructions.

After storing that result, the updater replaces it with binary32 `0x38D1B717` (approximately 0.00009999999747) if the ordered result is strictly below that constant, or if the requested world identity does not match the active one. These rules are not restricted to positive deltas. The native threshold and overwrite have the same bit pattern. The equality case alone does not trigger the lower-bound overwrite. The mismatch compares dwords at client `+0x800A50` and `+0x800448`, then compares the requested byte string at `+0x800A54` against the active byte string at `+0x378`, including exact lengths. The active length/capacity are at `+0x388`/`+0x38C`; requested length is at `+0x800A64`. The byte-string compare is at `0x00C520`. In particular, mismatch forces a floor overwrite even when the smoothed candidate is greater than the floor. There is no second numerical upper clamp at this store.

The separation between update and draw also matters. The draw routine reads `+0x1D4` at `0x0AC4E0`, adds the same 22.4 constant for its terrain traversal, and passes the unadjusted stored value to the primary fog upload at `0x0ADA12`. The read for drawing does not resume the previous frame's target calculation.

## Current camera angle producer

Client `+0x1A4` is the first component of a three-component current camera rotation, with corresponding target component at `+0x1B0`. Use the current component, in degrees, at the fog boundary. Do not substitute the target component, a converted radian angle, or a position component. The draw routine also reads the current value in the separate map rendering branch at `0x0ADEEB`; that branch's additional arithmetic is not the fog gradient normalization.

The initializer first clears `+0x1A4` and then sets it to 120 degrees (`0x42F00000`) at `0x05AADF`, copying that value to the target at `0x05AAF5`. Three other confirmed client paths explicitly set the target to 90 and copy it into the current component: stores `0x0825B8`, `0x083F36`, and `0x0841C4`. The path at `0x083E70` does so only when the signed selection at `+0x800A0C` is outside the extent of its pointer list. The other two stores are on entry to their respective routines, alongside interface and camera state changes. The mouse-button release handler also sets the target to 120 at `0x07E159` on its interface choice-1 branch; that instruction does not store to the current component.

The ordinary current-angle update is the call at `0x0965E4` to the three-component blend at `0x057460`, which writes the first component at `0x0574CF`. The immediately preceding optional entity-pointer distance check rejoins this calculation whether it clears the pointer or keeps it. No test of `+0x8006E4` gates the local blend. The coefficient comes from the power wrapper at `0x011D10`: base is binary32 `0x3BA3D70A` (nominally 0.005), and the exponent is the signed dword at client `+0x190` converted to binary32 and multiplied, in binary32, by `0x3C23D70A` (nominally 0.01). Both operands then widen to binary64 for `_libm_sse2_pow_precise` through `0x28D8D4`, with IAT RVA `0x2FC454`. The result is narrowed to binary32 before use as the coefficient of the signed-count weight operation.

With the resulting weight, the current value is the sum of the old current component multiplied by one minus the weight and the target component multiplied by the weight. The complement, products and sum round separately in binary32. The blend itself has no angle clamp and no shortest-arc adjustment. The observed mouse-motion producer at `0x07EA00` adjusts the target, not the current value. Its camera branches scale signed `+0x18C` by binary32 `0x3BA3D70A`, then by either the second motion input or the input object's `+0xD8` minus `+0xE0` displacement; client dword `+0x194` chooses subtraction when nonzero and addition when zero. These motion branch writes clamp their finite result to 0 through 180 degrees. The latter branch requires the virtual input/UI predicate to be nonzero, helper `0x250AE0` to return zero, and client byte `+0x04` to be nonzero. Earlier widget-specific motion paths can consume the input. Separate update branches around `0x089D2D`, `0x089D9E`, and `0x08A863` set the target's first component to 100 while the respective interface state is active and the state at `+0x800898` is inactive.

The native inputs to the power calculation are established as fields and bit patterns. The exhaustive lifecycle of `+0x190`, configuration labels, all menu triggers, and complete out-of-range floating-point behavior are outside this contract.

## Selector state and interaction gates

Use `+0x8006E4` as a mode selector, not as a fog coefficient or an underwater flag. The client initializer clears it with a two-byte zero store starting at the selector (`0x059FF7`). The following active writers were confirmed:

| Path | Writer RVA | Effect and gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map virtual-key toggle | `0x07E7E5` | Low-byte key `0x4D` (M) replaces the selector with 1 when it was zero, otherwise with 0. It does not set the teleport companion byte. |
| Escape virtual-key clear | `0x07E777` | Low-byte key `0x1B` (Escape) clears the selector, and separately toggles client byte `+0x8008F2`. |
| Selected object interaction | `0x097424` | A qualified world-object interaction with type dword 45 sets the selector and the companion byte `+0x800DE4` to 1. |
| Confirmed selection release | `0x07DFED` | A qualified left-mouse release in the teleport map path clears the selector after writing the controlled position and resetting the selected and hovered coordinate pairs. |

The two key writers belong to the `WM_KEYDOWN` handler, routed by native virtual slot `+0x08` to `0x07E1B0` through the window procedure `0x0C83F0`; translated character messages take a different virtual slot. The writers require the active-state entries for widgets at client `+0x800880`, `+0x800890`, `+0x800888`, `+0x800894`, and `+0x80088C` all to be zero. A widget active entry is the dword selected by that widget's `+0x3C` state object, using the index at state `+0x68` into the dword array pointed to by state `+0x94`. Helper `0x2531E0` must also return zero, and the input owner through client `+0x800A14` must have byte `+0x180` clear. The toggle is therefore conditional on native input admission. This dispatch does not itself test a key-repeat bit before the traced toggle.

The object-interaction writer requires client byte `+0x800A40` clear, current client byte `+0x0F` nonzero, and the preceding sample of that byte at data RVA `0x36B138` zero. The update copies the current byte into the sample at `0x09B596`. The selector writer lies on the branch that bypasses the nonpositive controlled-actor `+0x16C` handling; for finite values it requires that float to be positive. Then the first signed coordinate at `+0x800A84` must be nonnegative. The lookup at `0x1A0910` must return a record for the two zone coordinates and the nonnegative in-range object index in that three-dword selection. The record's type dword must be exactly 45. The selector is set without a prior-zero requirement. This path sets the companion byte in the same interaction. An original UTF-16 `Teleport` prompt at data RVA `0x301830` is selected by the later map update when the companion byte is set and the selected and hovered zone pairs agree and the selected pair is not `(-1,-1)` (`0x08B62B`–`0x08B688`). This is evidence for the interaction's teleport role; it does not identify the original display name of object type 45.

The release path is reached from `WM_LBUTTONUP` with native argument zero through client virtual slot `+0x20` at `0x07DDD0`. It requires a nonzero selector, a nonzero companion byte, a selected zone pair at `+0x800DDC` not equal to `(-1,-1)`, and exact equality to the hovered pair at `+0x800DD4`. On confirmation the controlled actor's position at actor `+0x10` is set to the center of that zone in X and Y (zone coordinate times 256 plus 128, with 65,536 raw units per block) and zero in Z. No terrain height is used in this write. The zone pairs are both reset to `(-1,-1)` before clearing the selector. When release enters the selector-active path but the teleport tests fail, it copies the hovered pair into the selected pair instead and does not clear the selector. The write at `0x07DFED` does not clear the companion byte itself: when a subsequent frame update sees the selector clear, it clears that byte and resets the selected pair at `0x097347`–`0x09735A`.

## Primary uploads

The traced terrain draw follows the ordinary branch at `0x0AD684`. At `0x0AD47E`–`0x0AD494`, a nonzero active-state entry for the widget at client `+0x800898` directs rendering through a different path that bypasses this upload sequence. This loading/interface switch is separate from the gradient selector. The primary shader wrapper comes from client `+0x800580`. Its successful initialization at `0x047E10` assigns vertex constant indices 30 and 31 to wrapper offsets `+0x60` and `+0x64` respectively (`0x047EDF`, `0x047EE6`). Each helper uses the graphics device at wrapper `+0x1C` and its virtual offset `+0x178` to send one binary32 four-component vertex constant.

| Call site and helper | Destination | Uploaded value and gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0x0ADA12` to `0x048100` | **c30** | If client `+0x1D4` is ordered and strictly positive, upload one divided by that binary32 distance, in `.x`, and positive zero in `.yzw`. The scalar division is binary32. Nonpositive or unordered inputs skip the call to set the register; they do not upload a zero vector. |
| `0x0ADA3D` to `0x048090` | **c31** | Begin with 90 degrees if the selector is nonzero; otherwise read client `+0x1A4` as binary32 degrees. Upload the selected angle minus 80, divided by -30, in `.x`, and positive zero in `.yzw`. Subtraction and division are separate binary32 operations. The helper does not clamp the result or skip this upload for any angle. |

The gradient offset and divisor bits are `0x42A00000` and `0xC1F00000` at data RVAs `0x2FCE50` and `0x345E9C`. The selector substitutes 90 for the input angle at the call site; it neither overwrites the current-angle field nor disables the distance upload. With ordinary nearest rounding, the 90-degree choice supplies about -0.33333334 in c31.x; the constructor's 120-degree current value would supply about -1.33333337 when selector clear. These are arithmetic consequences, not measured shader values.

## Limits

This is a static trace of the matched image. It does not establish the native runtime control settings for every call, the full math-library accuracy of power and square root, the rendered fog color or shader evaluation, or the accepted behavior of other passes that use the same helper. The named fields' confirmed stores and tested gates do not certify the absence of all possible indirect writers in the binary. The complete history of the world-identity fields, alternate interaction/UI paths and the mode flags outside this contract remain open. No engine parity or native runtime acceptance is claimed.
