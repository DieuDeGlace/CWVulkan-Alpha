# Alpha column scalar cache publication

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD after the author's complete readback. Scope: original client column ownership, scalar population, cache reads and the identified eviction/reset paths. This is a bounded static contract, not a claim that every helper reachable from world generation has been closed.

## Evidence and terminology

The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, image base `0x00400000`. Every source path below is relative to the original evidence root. Evidence consists of that image and the original `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` and individually identified `decompiled/client/functions/` exports. No reconstruction or other contract supplies facts in this document.

“Column cell” means the 32-byte object addressed through a zone's `+0xA8` array. “Cached scalar 04,” “cached scalar 08” and “cached scalar 0C” name fields by offset and confirmed producer association. They carry no inferred environmental label. “Point record” means the separate object selected through the world table at `+0x4000BC`; it is not a column cell or the region object selected through world `+0xBC`.

The main source anchors are the two wrappers at RVAs `0x1C8310` and `0x1C8350`; cell lookup `0x0347A0`, zone lookup `0x034D10` and region lookup `0x034A90`; zone builder `0x1E4850`; zone constructor/destructor `0x2240F0`/`0x224390`; cell constructor `0x050BF0`; region construction `0x1DA280`; point-record creation `0x1D7A70`; single-zone eviction `0x1A4890`, region eviction `0x1A4800`, point-record eviction `0x1A4780` and world seed reset `0x1A52E0`. These anchors and the narrower instruction ranges cited below define the audit boundary.

## Addressing and cache admission

Both wrappers receive a world context, two 32-bit integer column coordinates and a zone pointer that may be null. Their first operation relevant to the result is the same cell lookup at RVA `0x0347A0`. The coordinate arguments are passed as their original 32-bit words; neither wrapper converts them to floating point, rescales them, clamps them or changes a rejected coordinate before the fallback call. The cache hit does not depend on the scalar's value. Positive zero, negative zero, `0.5`, nonfinite payloads and any other stored bit pattern are not tested as validity markers. The wrapper simply loads the chosen binary32 field if the cell pointer is nonzero. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1C8310–0x1C8342` and `0x1C8350–0x1C8382`; `decompiled/client/functions/005c8310.c` and `005c8350.c`.]

Cell lookup admits each coordinate only when its signed value is at least zero and below `0x01000000` (16,777,216). With a null zone hint it divides each coordinate by 256 and performs the zone lookup. With a nonnull hint it instead checks the coordinate rectangle represented by signed 32-bit zone fields `+0x60` and `+0x64`: the column must be at least the corresponding zone coordinate multiplied by 256 and below that product plus 256. A mismatched hint returns a null cell immediately; it does not retry the world table with a null hint. A matching hint bypasses the world region and zone tables entirely. No world identity, seed, generation number or pointer-lifetime test accompanies that rectangle check. For a live, normally formed matching zone, the cell lies at the array base plus 32 times the sum of local X and 256 times local Y. Both local values are the admitted nonnegative coordinates modulo 256. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/004347a0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA `0x0347A0–0x034870`.]

The null-hint hierarchy has two further read-only boundaries. RVA `0x034D10` accepts signed zone coordinates from zero through 65,535, divides them by 64 and asks RVA `0x034A90` for a region. The latter accepts signed region coordinates from zero through 1,023 and loads the pointer at world `+0xBC`, indexed with first coordinate times 1,024 plus second coordinate. A resident region's zone slot is at region `+0x10018`, indexed with local zone X times 64 plus local zone Y. A null region or zone pointer produces a miss. None of these three lookup routines allocates, generates, inserts, reloads from disk, acquires a critical section or asks whether scalar construction has finished. Cell lookup does not separately reject a null `zone+0xA8` array pointer before adding the cell offset. Its usable-cell result therefore presumes a live, normally initialized zone, not merely a nonnull zone word. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/00434a90.c`, `00434d10.c` and `004347a0.c`; corresponding original assembly at RVAs `0x034A90–0x034AC6`, `0x034D10–0x034D88` and `0x0347A0–0x034870`.]

The array's local-cell ordering differs from the region and zone pointer-table order. Local X is the low index in the 256-by-256 cell array, while local zone Y is the low index in the region's 64-by-64 zone pointer table. These are separate address calculations and must not be treated as one uniform table orientation.

| Wrapper | Cache-hit read | Cache-miss call with original coordinates | Cache mutation in wrapper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RVA `0x1C8310` | binary32 cell `+0x04`, loaded into the x87 return register | producer RVA `0x1C4800` | none |
| RVA `0x1C8350` | binary32 cell `+0x08`, loaded into the x87 return register | producer RVA `0x1C4DD0` | none |

The miss is a computation-only fallback at this boundary. Each wrapper returns its producer's result without storing it in a cell, allocating a missing zone, calling the zone builder or substituting the hint's coordinates. Thus a wrong hint can force recomputation even when the world contains a suitable zone, and repeated misses do not become hits through these wrappers. Conversely, a valid matching hint can expose its own existing cell independently of whether the world table currently contains that zone. That statement is about admission with a live pointer; it does not make a destroyed or dangling hint safe. [Source: wrapper ranges above, especially RVAs `0x1C8328`, `0x1C8330–0x1C8342`, `0x1C8368` and `0x1C8370–0x1C8382`.]

## Initial cell contents and the three confirmed scalar fields

A normally constructed zone allocates space for 65,536 cells of 32 bytes, with a four-byte array count preceding the first cell. The allocation request is `0x200004` bytes; the constructor records a count of `0x10000`, invokes the element constructor at RVA `0x050BF0` with stride `0x20`, and places the first element pointer in zone `+0xA8`. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/006240f0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x22428B–0x2242D6`.]

The original image's element constructor initializes cell `+0x04`, `+0x08` and `+0x0C` to all-zero words, which are binary32 positive zero when read as these scalars. It also zeroes `+0x10`, `+0x14`, `+0x18` and `+0x1C` after setting the cell's virtual-table pointer. The initialization contains no separate scalar-validity bit or seed tag. Positive zero in a constructed cell therefore means its initialized numeric payload; the cache readers do not distinguish it from a producer result of zero. [Source: `binary/Cube.exe`, original instruction bytes at RVA `0x050BF0–0x050C2A`; the assembly export omits the body at this label.]

The generator establishes these producer associations by direct calls followed immediately by binary32 stores:

| Cell field | Direct producer used during zone construction | Store RVA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `+0x04` | `0x1C4800` | `0x1E4B73` |
| `+0x08` | `0x1C4DD0` | `0x1E4B8D` |
| `+0x0C` | `0x1EF040` | `0x1E4BA7` |

Cached scalar 0C is independently confirmed by a consumer: the routine at RVA `0x1C56E0` calls the same cell lookup, loads cell `+0x0C` on a hit and calls producer `0x1EF040` on a miss, narrowing that fallback to a binary32 local before later use. The consumer proceeds to a strict greater-than-zero gate and downstream color/block-selection work. This establishes the cache/producer correspondence without assigning an environmental interpretation to the scalar. Producer `0x1EF040` independently inspects point-record byte `+0x08` for numeric value 1 and uses a positive-zero failure exit. Its full spatial weighting and the downstream color formula are outside this publication contract. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1C5D0A–0x1C5D3F`, `0x1EF040–0x1EF152` and `0x1EF58B–0x1EF5A0`; `decompiled/client/functions/005ef040.c`.]

The neighboring cell words have different confirmed access patterns. RVA `0x0348B0` returns `+0x10`, RVA `0x19FF50` returns `+0x14` and RVA `0x034B70` returns `+0x1C` as raw 32-bit quantities. In the block access paths, `+0x10` is the signed integer vertical origin, `+0x18` is the pointer to four-byte block entries and `+0x1C` is their signed integer count; these delimit the represented vertical interval. This audit does not give `+0x14` an independently recovered semantic label or reinterpret those getters as extra float-cache wrappers. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/004348b0.c`, `0059ff50.c`, `00434b70.c`, `0042f7e0.c` and `0042f730.c`.]

## Construction, overwrite order and publication

The builder at RVA `0x1E4850` accepts signed 32-bit zone coordinates from zero through 65,535. Inputs outside that range return null before construction. For admitted coordinates it first asks RVA `0x1DA280` to provide a three-by-three neighborhood of regions around the requested zone's parent region. The first-coordinate offset is traversed from minus one through plus one as the outer loop; the second-coordinate offset follows the same order as the inner loop. It then reads the requested zone with RVA `0x034D10`. A nonnull existing zone returns immediately. This return occurs before allocating a new zone, before the zone-specific random reseed and before all three scalar-fill calls. Re-entering this builder for an existing zone is therefore not a scalar refresh operation, although its preceding region-neighborhood calls can have their own effects. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E489B–0x1E495F`; `decompiled/client/functions/005e4850.c`.]

The region-provider boundary also matters when prerequisites are missing. RVA `0x1DA280` bounds its region coordinates to zero through 1,023 and returns an already resident world `+0xBC` region without entering its construction branch. Only its missing-region branch requests the five-by-five neighborhood of point records through RVA `0x1D7A70`. That point-record routine has its own zero-through-1,023 bounds, returns an existing world `+0x4000BC` pointer unchanged, and otherwise constructs and eventually inserts a record in that separate table. Consequently, requesting an existing region is not proof that a subsequently missing point-record slot has been repopulated. The detailed region and point-record numeric generation and file-loading paths are larger dependencies and are not specified here. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/005da280.c`, entry construction branch; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1DA2DF–0x1DA36F` and region insertion at `0x1DC628`; `decompiled/client/functions/005d7a70.c`, entry and final insertion.]

For a missing zone, the builder allocates a `0xC8`-byte zone and runs its constructor. It reseeds the native random generator from the low 32 bits of world `+0x800188` plus zone Y shifted left by 16 plus zone X, then writes zone X and Y to zone `+0x60` and `+0x64`. The global origins are those zone coordinates shifted left by eight. Other preparation work occurs before the scalar loop. This is not a claim about the complete random-draw schedule of the large builder or those preparation helpers. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E495F–0x1E4ADB`, especially `0x1E4996–0x1E49EE`.]

The scalar loop traverses global X from the zone's X origin through origin plus 255 as the outer loop and global Y from its Y origin through origin plus 255 as the inner loop. It addresses each of the 65,536 cells once during this loop. At each coordinate it first calls producer `0x1C4800` and stores the x87 result as binary32 in cell `+0x04`. It then calls `0x1C4DD0` with the same world and integer coordinates and stores binary32 in `+0x08`. Finally it calls `0x1EF040` and stores binary32 in `+0x0C`. The next column begins only after the third store. The loop calls the direct producers, not the cache wrappers, and does not consult the old initialized scalar words. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E4ADB–0x1E4BF4`, calls at `0x1E4B6E`, `0x1E4B88` and `0x1E4BA2`.]

These three stores overwrite the constructor's zeros unconditionally with the returned scalar values. There is no test for a preferred old value, missing-input marker, finite value or success code and no conditional preservation of a previous scalar within the fill loop. Each result is stored before the next producer is called, so this is an ordered sequence of three separate writes, not an atomic triple. The stores are binary32 narrowing boundaries; a cache-hit wrapper subsequently loads that stored binary32 into x87 without additional arithmetic. The wrapper itself adds no output clamp or corrective transform. This document does not prescribe a floating-point control environment beyond the instructions and memory widths observed here.

Two direct producer failure values are especially relevant to publication. Producers `0x1C4800` and `0x1C4DD0` each inspect point-record slots in world `+0x4000BC`; a required null slot or an invalid required record index branches to their failure exit, rather than creating that record. Their final accumulated-weight test can take that same exit. Both exits load the original binary32 constant at RVA `0x345DA0`, whose bits are `0x3F000000`, exactly `0.5`. This is separate from the constructor's zero initialization and producer `0x1EF040`'s positive-zero failure exit. If either first producer reaches that exit during scalar filling, the loop stores `0.5` in its corresponding cache field and continues; there is no scalar validity tag that requests a later retry. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1C4996–0x1C49CD`, `0x1C4B30–0x1C4B69`, `0x1C4CA7–0x1C4CC0`, `0x1C4DB1–0x1C4DCA`, `0x1C4F53–0x1C4F90`, `0x1C50F0–0x1C512F` and `0x1C5262–0x1C529F`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x345DA0`.]

After the scalar fill the builder performs substantial block, object and other generation work. The identified direct write that makes the new zone reachable from its parent's ordinary zone-pointer table is much later, at RVA `0x1EE05A`. The slot is the requested local zone X times 64 plus local zone Y, and the stored value is the constructed zone pointer. All initial scalar-fill iterations precede that explicit publication store on the normal construction path. The function then performs local cleanup and returns the zone pointer. The ordinary null-hint lookup requires a nonnull table slot; this identified publication path supplies it after the initial fill. Other exposure through unclosed helper branches is not universally excluded. This is an instruction-order statement, not a general synchronization or cross-thread visibility guarantee. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, slot-index calculation at RVAs `0x1E6B63–0x1E6B9C`, publication and return at `0x1EE04E–0x1EE0B9`.]

The builder itself can use the new zone earlier by passing its pointer as the hint to cell lookup. Its later generation includes paired cache-hit reads of `+0x04` and `+0x08`, each with the corresponding direct-producer fallback, before the final table insertion. These reads have the same rectangle/lifetime premises as other hinted lookups. Initial fill before ordinary table publication does not mean the pointer was unavailable to the builder's own helpers. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E6BDD–0x1E6C69`; additional scalar reads in the same builder are visible around `0x1EB094–0x1EB132`.]

## Preservation through the traced block writers

The identified cell-storage resize routine at RVA `0x050C50` changes the four-byte entry allocation at `+0x18`, count `+0x1C` and, where specified by its second numeric adjustment argument, the signed origin `+0x10`. It zero-initializes new entry storage, copies existing entries as required and replaces/frees the entry allocation. Its zero-or-negative-size path frees entry storage, adjusts the origin and clears pointer/count. No path in this routine writes cell `+0x04`, `+0x08` or `+0x0C`. The original image was used to resolve normal continuation after deletion calls that the text exports annotate as nonreturning. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/00450c50.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` and `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x050C50–0x050D8A`.]

The block-entry writer at RVA `0x04E700` uses that resize routine when its index is below zero or outside the current count, then writes four-byte block entries and, in the applicable overwrite branch, their high flag bit. It leaves the three scalar fields alone. The world-coordinate block writer at RVA `0x04E7A0` obtains the existing column with RVA `0x0347A0` and delegates to this entry writer, including its below-origin fill; the bounded column mutation at RVA `0x071920` likewise delegates for an admitted vertical index. These traced block changes can alter a column's block storage without invalidating or recomputing its three scalar caches. The later direct cell accesses inspected in the zone builder use `+0x10`, `+0x18` and `+0x1C` for this separate storage. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/0044e700.c`, `0044e7a0.c` and `00471920.c`; original builder cell/block instructions at RVAs `0x1E54F5–0x1E5504`, `0x1E554A–0x1E55BB` and `0x1E57F2–0x1E590E`.]

This preservation result is limited to the named writers and inspected direct accesses. It is not a proof that no other routine can ever write a cell through an alias. The large zone builder and all of its object-placement, loading and other helper branches have not been exhaustively closed for arbitrary indirect memory writes. No universal “immutable after fill” property is asserted.

## Eviction, prerequisite loss and seed transitions

For valid zone coordinates, single-zone eviction at RVA `0x1A4890` locates the parent region and existing zone slot. If either is missing it does nothing. For a nonnull zone it enters the world critical sections at `+0x8000D8` then `+0x8000C0`, clears the region's zone pointer, leaves those critical sections in reverse order, conditionally invokes the zone-save path when world byte `+0xB4` is zero, and invokes the old zone's deleting destructor. There is no scalar refill or in-place scalar reset on this path. A following ordinary cache lookup misses because its zone slot is null; a following successful zone reconstruction creates new cells and runs the scalar fill again. The internal scalar readers acquire neither of those critical sections. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/005a4890.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A4890–0x1A4976`.]

The owner destruction is explicit. The zone's virtual-table first target is RVA `0x224760`, which invokes destructor `0x224390` and frees the zone for deletion flag 1. The destructor handles zone `+0xA8` as a counted array; its nonempty-array call uses flags 3 through the cell's first virtual target, RVA `0x224700`. That target iterates all cells with the recorded count and 32-byte stride using cell destructor `0x050C30`, then frees the array allocation. The cell destructor frees its separate `+0x18` block storage. This removes the scalar-owning objects rather than turning their scalar payloads into a reusable “invalid” state. A retained hint after this destruction is a dangling pointer, not a supported stale-cache read. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/00624760.c`, `00624390.c`, `00624700.c` and `00450c30.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, virtual-table entries at RVAs `0x31E280` and `0x2FFD90`, and cell-destructor continuation at `0x050C30–0x050C45`.]

Whole-region eviction at RVA `0x1A4800` separately bounds region coordinates to zero through 1,023. For a resident region it calls the region-save path, enters world `+0x8000C0` then `+0x8000D8`, clears the world `+0xBC` region slot, leaves them in reverse order and invokes the deleting destructor. The original region virtual-table first target is RVA `0x1C3FB0`, which invokes region destructor `0x1C3CC0`; that destructor visits all 64-by-64 zone pointer slots at region `+0x10018` and destroys nonnull zones. This invalidates ordinary column lookup by removing its owner hierarchy. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/005a4800.c`, `005c3fb0.c` and `005c3cc0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A4800–0x1A4883` and `0x1C3CF8–0x1C3D1D`; `binary/Cube.exe`, region virtual table at RVA `0x31DCCC`.]

Point-record eviction at RVA `0x1A4780` operates on the other table, world `+0x4000BC`. For an admitted resident record it enters world `+0x8000C0` then `+0x8000D8`, clears that point-record pointer, leaves those locks in reverse order and frees the record. It does not visit the world `+0xBC` region table, zone slots, cell arrays or the three cached scalar words. Therefore, conditionally on a zone remaining alive and reachable, removal or later replacement of a point-record prerequisite alone does not force its existing scalar-cache hit to recompute. A cache miss instead takes the direct producer path, whose first two producer failures can return `0.5` as described above. This conditional result does not assert that the native maintenance scheduler normally leaves a retained zone with missing prerequisites. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/005a4780.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A4780–0x1A47F8`; wrapper and producer ranges above.]

One identified maintenance caller, RVA `0x06A8A0`, processes existing zones before wider region and point-record eviction. In its normal retention branch it keeps a zone when its squared integer zone-coordinate distance from a retained request is below 16. It keeps a region when both absolute region-coordinate differences from a retained request's parent region are below 3, and point records use the wider below-5 per-axis condition. For the same region-table slot its ordered calls are individual-zone eviction, possible whole-region eviction, then possible point-record eviction. A changed world-selection branch can remove these retention exceptions and subsequently invoke seed reset. These observed ordering and padding differences are reasons not to turn the conditional stale-cache case into a claim that this caller routinely exposes it. Request-set construction, all caller scheduling, the full time budget and inter-thread exclusion are outside this contract. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/0046a8a0.c`, eviction traversal and changed-selection branch; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, calls at RVAs `0x06AE23`, `0x06AEBE`, `0x06AF25` and reset call `0x06AFAB`.]

Seed reset at RVA `0x1A52E0` does more than change a key. It first stores the supplied 32-bit seed in world `+0x800164`, reseeds the random generator, takes a random result modulo 100,000 into world `+0x800188` and generates further seed-derived fields. Only afterward does it traverse all 1,024-by-1,024 region-table slots. Each resident region can first cause zone saves when world `+0xB4` is zero, then its deleting destructor is called and its world `+0xBC` pointer is cleared. The corresponding point-record entry four megabytes farther into the world is then freed if nonnull and cleared. Normal completion of this traversal leaves both pointer tables empty of their previous owners. The original bytes confirm the point-record pointer-clear instruction after its free call, which is absent from the assembly text export. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/005a52e0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A5323–0x1A534F` and `0x1A5543–0x1A55E2`; `binary/Cube.exe`, omitted continuation at RVAs `0x1A55C0–0x1A55CD`.]

The reset's ordering establishes a bounded transition: seed and seed-derived state change before the traversal has destroyed every old zone. Before a particular old zone is destroyed, its scalar words have not been rewritten merely because the seed changed, and a permitted read of that still-live cell uses its stored payload without a seed-epoch check. This is not a promise that reading concurrently with reset is allowed, synchronized or safe. Region destruction precedes clearing its world pointer in this reset, so a pointer observed during destruction need not designate a usable owner. After a normally completed reset, null-hint lookup cannot recover the previous zone through those tables. A supplied pointer is not validated against the cleared hierarchy; after owner destruction its use remains invalid. There is no traced in-place scalar conversion from old seed to new seed.

Missing world region/zone pointers are thus misses, not hidden cell preservation locations. Missing point-record prerequisites are different: they can affect a direct producer while an existing independently owned column cache is still admitted. A completed reset removes both kinds of owner; single-zone/region eviction removes column owners; point-record eviction alone removes prerequisites. Those distinctions follow the actual pointer stores and destruction calls rather than a scalar-value heuristic.

## Precision, malformed state and limits

The contracted read and publication widths are binary32 at cell `+0x04/+0x08/+0x0C`, 32-bit pointers in the supported image and signed 32-bit coordinates for the stated admission tests. Coordinate products, shifts, slot indexing and address arithmetic are native 32-bit operations. Within the normally admitted zone/column ranges the listed origins and local indices have the bounds described above. The wrappers themselves add no random draw, generation call, scalar store or seed comparison. Producer internals, noise arithmetic, file restoration and the complete region-generation random schedule remain separate dependencies; their identification here does not close their formulas.

This is not a malformed-memory parser contract. Invalid world or zone pointers, an invalid array count, allocation failure, null array storage inside a published zone, nonfinite scalar payloads and overflowed hint metadata have no recovered safe-recovery guarantee. A nonzero matching zone hint is dereferenced without ownership validation. The single-zone eviction helper does not repeat the complete signed zone-coordinate admission gate used by the ordinary zone lookup: its parent division and signed remainder arithmetic are not a guarantee of safe handling for arbitrary negative inputs. The eviction behavior above is for the valid native coordinate domain. Truncated save data, arbitrary memory corruption and allocation-exception paths have not been traced.

The scoped zone-save serializer at RVA `0x04E340`, reached from `0x1A5050`, serializes selected zone/entity/entry collections and does not serialize the `+0xA8` column array or its three scalar words in its inspected body. This supports no claim that every possible native transport or persistence format excludes those fields; corresponding load/overlay helpers and other formats were not exhaustively audited. [Source: `decompiled/client/functions/0044e340.c` and `005a5050.c`.]

The final guarantees are the explicit cache-read branches, direct scalar-producer associations, constructor initialization, scalar fill/write order, existing-zone early return, identified table publication, named writer preservation and named owner-removal/reset order. Complete absence of all indirect scalar writes, universal thread safety, freshness across arbitrary external mutation, and full final-zone contents after every large helper are unproved.

## Original-image confirmation

The image hash was checked directly for this audit. All exported instruction bytes inspected in the following key spans agree with `binary/Cube.exe`: cell/zone/region lookup; both wrappers; the builder's admission, initial construction and scalar fill; its final publication; the region/point/zone eviction interval; the seed and derived-seed writes; reset's table traversal; and the column-array construction interval. Original-image disassembly independently supplies cell constructor `0x050BF0–0x050C2A`, deletion continuations, and reset's missing `0x1A55C0–0x1A55CD` pointer-clear path. Virtual targets were read from original image table bytes, not inferred from an export's class label. The binary32 `0.5` failure constant was read from its actual original-image location, RVA `0x345DA0`. This confirmation is source evidence only; no native runtime observation or replacement-engine result is asserted.
