# Alpha column scalar cache publication

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD after a fresh source-only author's complete re-check and readback (2026-11-22). Scope: original client column ownership, scalar population, cache reads and the identified eviction/reset paths. This is a bounded static contract, not a claim that every helper reachable from world generation has been closed.

## Evidence and terminology

The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, image base `0x00400000`, 32-bit x86. Every source path below is relative to the original evidence root. Evidence consists of that image, the original `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` export and the individually named `decompiled/client/functions/` exports, which serve as cross-references only. No reconstruction or other contract supplies facts in this document. RVAs are image offsets from the base.

"Column cell" means the 32-byte object addressed through a zone's `+0xA8` array. "Cached scalar 04", "cached scalar 08" and "cached scalar 0C" name fields by offset and confirmed producer association. They carry no inferred environmental label. "Point record" means the separate object held in the world table at `+0x4000BC`; it is neither a column cell nor the region object held in the world table at `+0xBC`.

The main source anchors are the two cache readers at RVAs `0x1C8310` and `0x1C8350`; cell lookup `0x0347A0`, zone lookup `0x034D10` and region lookup `0x034A90`; zone builder `0x1E4850`; zone construction and teardown `0x2240F0`/`0x224390`; cell initialization `0x050BF0`; region provision `0x1DA280`; point-record provision `0x1D7A70`; single-zone eviction `0x1A4890`, region eviction `0x1A4800`, point-record eviction `0x1A4780` and world seed reset `0x1A52E0`. These anchors and the narrower instruction ranges cited below define the audit boundary.

## Addressing and cache admission

Each cache reader takes the world as its object context plus three 32-bit stack arguments: two signed integer column coordinates and a zone pointer that may be null. Its first operation relevant to the result is the cell lookup at RVA `0x0347A0`, given all three arguments. The coordinates are forwarded as their original 32-bit words; neither reader converts them to floating point, rescales them, clamps them or changes a rejected coordinate before the fallback call. The cache hit does not depend on the scalar's value. Positive zero, negative zero, `0.5`, nonfinite payloads and any other stored bit pattern are not tested as validity markers. The reader simply loads the chosen binary32 field if the returned cell address is nonzero. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1C8310–0x1C8341` and `0x1C8350–0x1C8381`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005c8310.c` and `005c8350.c`.]

Cell lookup admits each coordinate only when its signed value is at least zero and below `0x01000000` (16,777,216); otherwise it returns null. With a null zone hint it divides each coordinate by 256 and performs the zone lookup. With a nonnull hint it instead checks the coordinate rectangle represented by signed 32-bit zone fields `+0x60` (zone X) and `+0x64` (zone Y): the column must be at least the corresponding zone coordinate multiplied by 256 and below that product plus 256. A mismatched hint returns a null cell immediately; it does not retry the world table with a null hint. A matching hint bypasses the world region and zone tables entirely. No world identity, seed, generation number or pointer-lifetime test accompanies that rectangle check. For a live, normally formed matching zone, the cell lies at the array base plus 32 times the sum of local X and 256 times local Y, where both local values are the admitted nonnegative coordinates modulo 256. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x0347A0–0x03486F`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/004347a0.c`.]

The null-hint hierarchy has two further read-only boundaries. RVA `0x034D10` accepts signed zone coordinates from zero through 65,535, divides them by 64 and asks RVA `0x034A90` for a region. The latter accepts signed region coordinates from zero through 1,023 and returns the pointer at world `+0xBC` indexed by first coordinate times 1,024 plus second coordinate. A resident region's zone slot is at region `+0x10018`, indexed by local zone X times 64 plus local zone Y, each local value being the zone coordinate modulo 64. A null region or zone pointer produces a miss. None of these three lookup routines allocates, generates, inserts, reloads from disk, acquires a critical section or asks whether scalar construction has finished. Cell lookup does not separately reject a null `zone+0xA8` array pointer before adding the cell offset, so its usable-cell result presumes a live, normally initialized zone, not merely a nonnull zone word. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x034A90–0x034AC5`, `0x034D10–0x034D87` and `0x0347A0–0x03486F`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/00434a90.c`, `00434d10.c` and `004347a0.c`.]

The cell array's local ordering differs from the region and zone pointer-table order. Local X is the low index in the 256-by-256 cell array, while local zone Y is the low index in the region's 64-by-64 zone pointer table, and the second region coordinate is the low index in the 1,024-by-1,024 world region table. These are separate address calculations and must not be treated as one uniform table orientation.

| Reader | Cache-hit read | Cache-miss call with original coordinates | Cache mutation in reader |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RVA `0x1C8310` | binary32 cell `+0x04`, loaded onto the x87 stack as the return value | producer RVA `0x1C4800` | none |
| RVA `0x1C8350` | binary32 cell `+0x08`, loaded onto the x87 stack as the return value | producer RVA `0x1C4DD0` | none |

The miss is a computation-only fallback at this boundary. Each reader calls its producer with the world context and the two original coordinates only (the hint is not forwarded) and returns the producer's x87 result unchanged, without storing it in a cell, allocating a missing zone, calling the zone builder or substituting the hint's coordinates. Thus a wrong hint can force recomputation even when the world contains a suitable zone, and repeated misses do not become hits through these readers. Conversely, a valid matching hint can expose its own existing cell independently of whether the world table currently contains that zone. That statement is about admission with a live pointer; it does not make a destroyed or dangling hint safe. [Source: reader ranges above, especially RVAs `0x1C8328`, `0x1C8330–0x1C8341`, `0x1C8368` and `0x1C8370–0x1C8381`.]

## Initial cell contents and the three confirmed scalar fields

A normally constructed zone requests `0x200004` bytes for its cell array: a four-byte element count followed by 65,536 cells of 32 bytes. Zone construction records the count `0x10000` in the first word, initializes every element with RVA `0x050BF0` at stride `0x20`, and stores the address of the first element (four bytes past the allocation) in zone `+0xA8`. If that allocation returns null, zone `+0xA8` is set to null. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x22428B–0x2242D5`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/006240f0.c`.]

Cell initialization in the original image sets the cell's first word to the virtual-table address at RVA `0x2FFD90`, then sets each of `+0x04`, `+0x08`, `+0x0C`, `+0x10`, `+0x14`, `+0x18` and `+0x1C` to an all-zero 32-bit word. Read as these scalars, the zero words are binary32 positive zero. The initialization contains no separate scalar-validity bit or seed tag. Positive zero in a constructed cell therefore means its initialized numeric payload; the cache readers do not distinguish it from a producer result of zero. [Source: `binary/Cube.exe`, original instruction bytes at RVAs `0x050BF0–0x050C29`; the assembly export omits this body.]

The zone builder establishes these producer associations by direct calls followed immediately by binary32 stores of the x87 result:

| Cell field | Direct producer used during zone construction | Store RVA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `+0x04` | `0x1C4800` | `0x1E4B73` |
| `+0x08` | `0x1C4DD0` | `0x1E4B8D` |
| `+0x0C` | `0x1EF040` | `0x1E4BA7` |

Cached scalar 0C is independently confirmed by a consumer: the routine at RVA `0x1C56E0` performs the same cell lookup, loads binary32 cell `+0x0C` on a hit and calls producer `0x1EF040` on a miss, storing that fallback as a binary32 local before use. The consumer then applies a strict greater-than-zero test to the value before further work. This establishes the cache/producer correspondence without assigning an environmental interpretation to the scalar. Producer `0x1EF040` inspects point-record byte `+0x08` for numeric value 1 and has a positive-zero failure exit, taken when a required point-record slot is null, a required record index is outside the table, or none of the first inspected neighborhood of records has that byte equal to 1; a later accumulated-weight comparison can take the same exit. Its full spatial weighting is outside this publication contract. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1C5D0A–0x1C5D3F`, `0x1EF040–0x1EF152` and `0x1EF58B–0x1EF59F`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005ef040.c`.]

The neighboring cell words have different confirmed access patterns. RVA `0x0348B0` returns `+0x10`, RVA `0x19FF50` returns `+0x14` and RVA `0x034B70` returns `+0x1C`, each as a raw 32-bit word. In the block access paths at RVAs `0x02F7E0` and `0x02F730`, `+0x10` is the signed integer vertical origin, `+0x18` is the pointer to four-byte block entries and `+0x1C` is their signed integer count; together they delimit the represented vertical interval. This audit does not give `+0x14` an independently recovered semantic label or reinterpret those getters as further float caches. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` at those five RVAs; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/004348b0.c`, `0059ff50.c`, `00434b70.c`, `0042f7e0.c` and `0042f730.c`.]

## Construction, overwrite order and publication

The builder at RVA `0x1E4850` takes the world as its object context and accepts signed 32-bit zone coordinates from zero through 65,535. Inputs outside that range return null before any other work. For admitted coordinates it first asks RVA `0x1DA280` to provide the three-by-three neighborhood of regions around the requested zone's parent region (zone coordinates divided by 64). The first-coordinate offset runs from minus one through plus one in the outer loop; the second-coordinate offset follows the same order in the inner loop. It then reads the parent region's pointer from world `+0xBC` and keeps that value, and reads the requested zone with RVA `0x034D10`. A nonnull existing zone is returned immediately. This return occurs before allocating a new zone, before the zone-specific random reseed and before all three scalar-fill calls. Re-entering this builder for an existing zone is therefore not a scalar refresh operation, although its preceding region-neighborhood calls can have their own effects. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E489B–0x1E495E`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005e4850.c`.]

The region-provider boundary also matters when prerequisites are missing. RVA `0x1DA280` bounds its region coordinates to zero through 1,023 (returning null outside) and returns an already resident world `+0xBC` region without entering its construction branch. Only its missing-region branch requests the five-by-five neighborhood of point records through RVA `0x1D7A70`, offsets minus two through plus two with the first coordinate outer. That point-record routine has its own zero-through-1,023 bounds, returns an existing world `+0x4000BC` pointer unchanged, and otherwise constructs a record and stores it in that separate table. Consequently, requesting an existing region is not proof that a subsequently missing point-record slot has been repopulated. The detailed region and point-record numeric generation and file-loading paths are larger dependencies and are not specified here. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1DA2DF–0x1DA356`, resident return at `0x1DC693` and region insertion at `0x1DC628`; point-record entry at `0x1D7A88–0x1D7AC3` and insertions at `0x1D7E66` and `0x1D7F39`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005da280.c` and `005d7a70.c`.]

For a missing zone, the builder allocates a `0xC8`-byte zone and constructs it. It then reseeds the imported MSVCR110 `srand` generator with the 32-bit sum, wrapping modulo 2^32, of the word at world `+0x800188`, zone Y shifted left by 16, and zone X. It writes zone X and Y to zone `+0x60` and `+0x64`. The global column origins are those zone coordinates shifted left by eight. Other preparation work, including calls that use the zone-center coordinates (origin plus 128 on each axis), occurs before the scalar loop. This is not a claim about the complete random-draw schedule of the large builder or those preparation helpers. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E495F–0x1E4ADA`, especially `0x1E4996–0x1E49EE`.]

The scalar loop traverses global X from the zone's X origin through origin plus 255 in the outer loop and global Y from its Y origin through origin plus 255 in the inner loop, so each of the 65,536 cells is addressed exactly once. At each column it first calls producer `0x1C4800` with the world and the two global integer coordinates (X first) and stores the x87 result as binary32 in cell `+0x04`. It then calls `0x1C4DD0` with the same arguments and stores binary32 in `+0x08`. Finally it calls `0x1EF040` with the same arguments and stores binary32 in `+0x0C`. The next column begins only after the third store. The loop calls the direct producers, not the cache readers, and does not read the old initialized scalar words. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E4ADB–0x1E4BF3`, calls at `0x1E4B6E`, `0x1E4B88` and `0x1E4BA2`.]

These three stores overwrite the initialization zeros unconditionally with the returned scalar values. There is no test for a preferred old value, missing-input marker, finite value or success code and no conditional preservation of a previous scalar within the fill loop. Each result is stored before the next producer is called, so this is an ordered sequence of three separate writes, not an atomic triple. The stores are binary32 narrowing boundaries; a cache-hit reader subsequently loads that stored binary32 onto the x87 stack without further arithmetic. The reader itself adds no output clamp or corrective transform. This document does not prescribe a floating-point control environment beyond the instructions and memory widths observed here.

Two direct producer failure values are especially relevant to publication. Producers `0x1C4800` and `0x1C4DD0` each inspect point-record slots in world `+0x4000BC`; a required null slot or an out-of-table required record index branches to their failure exit rather than creating that record. Their final accumulated-weight comparison can take that same exit. Both exits load the original binary32 constant at RVA `0x345DA0`, whose bits are `0x3F000000`, exactly `0.5`. This is separate from the zero initialization and producer `0x1EF040`'s positive-zero failure exit. If either first producer reaches that exit during scalar filling, the loop stores `0.5` in its corresponding cache field and continues; there is no scalar validity tag that requests a later retry. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1C4996–0x1C49CD`, `0x1C4B30–0x1C4B69`, `0x1C4CA7–0x1C4CB0`, `0x1C4DB1–0x1C4DC9`, `0x1C4F53–0x1C4F90`, `0x1C50F0–0x1C512F` and `0x1C5262–0x1C529E`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x345DA0`.]

After the scalar fill the builder performs substantial block, object and other generation work. The identified direct write that makes the new zone reachable from its parent's ordinary zone-pointer table is much later, at RVA `0x1EE05A`. The slot is the requested local zone X times 64 plus local zone Y, and the stored value is the constructed zone pointer. The region into which it is stored is the parent-region pointer the builder read from world `+0xBC` immediately after its neighborhood requests and before allocating the zone; the builder does not re-read the world table or re-check residency before this store. All initial scalar-fill iterations precede that explicit publication store on the normal construction path. The builder then performs local cleanup and returns the zone pointer. The ordinary null-hint lookup requires a nonnull table slot; this identified publication path supplies it after the initial fill. Other exposure through unclosed helper branches is not universally excluded. This is an instruction-order statement, not a general synchronization or cross-thread visibility guarantee. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, parent-region read at RVA `0x1E4945`, slot-index calculation at `0x1E6B63–0x1E6B96`, publication and return at `0x1EE04E–0x1EE0BA`.]

The builder itself can use the new zone earlier by passing its pointer as the hint to cell lookup. Its later generation includes paired cache-hit reads of `+0x04` and `+0x08` at the zone-center column, each with the corresponding direct-producer fallback, before the final table insertion. These inline reads load the binary32 field (or the producer result narrowed to a binary32 local) into an SSE register rather than the x87 stack. They have the same rectangle and lifetime premises as other hinted lookups. Initial fill before ordinary table publication does not mean the pointer was unavailable to the builder's own helpers. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1E6BDD–0x1E6C69`; further hinted scalar reads in the same builder at `0x1EB094–0x1EB132`.]

## Preservation through the traced block writers

The cell-storage resize routine at RVA `0x050C50` takes a new entry count and an origin adjustment. When the new count equals the current count it returns without change. Otherwise it subtracts the adjustment from the signed origin `+0x10` and records the new count in `+0x1C`. For a positive count it allocates new four-byte entry storage, zero-fills it, copies existing entries as required, frees the old storage and stores the new pointer in `+0x18`. A zero or negative count frees the entry storage, records a count of zero and clears `+0x18`. No path in this routine writes cell `+0x04`, `+0x08` or `+0x0C`. The original image was used to resolve the normal continuation after free calls that the text export annotates as nonreturning. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` and `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x050C50–0x050D81`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/00450c50.c`.]

The block-entry writer at RVA `0x04E700` uses that resize routine when its index is below zero (growing at the low end and lowering the origin) or at or beyond the current count, then handles one four-byte block entry. After low-end growth the new first entry is written directly; otherwise an entry whose high flag bit is clear is overwritten. An entry whose high flag bit is set is kept when the low five bits of the new entry's fourth byte are zero; otherwise it is overwritten and its high flag bit is set again. It writes nothing outside the entry storage and leaves the three scalar fields alone. The world-coordinate block writer at RVA `0x04E7A0` obtains the existing column with RVA `0x0347A0` and delegates to this entry writer, including a fill of entries between the written index and the old origin; the bounded column mutation at RVA `0x071920` likewise delegates for a vertical index inside the represented interval. These traced block changes can alter a column's block storage without invalidating or recomputing its three scalar caches. The later direct cell accesses inspected in the zone builder use `+0x10`, `+0x18` and `+0x1C` for this separate storage. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x04E700–0x04E796`, `0x04E7A0–0x04E889` and `0x071920–0x0719EA`; builder cell/block instructions at `0x1E54F5–0x1E5504`, `0x1E554A–0x1E55BB` and `0x1E57F2–0x1E590E`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/0044e700.c`, `0044e7a0.c` and `00471920.c`.]

This preservation result is limited to the named writers and inspected direct accesses. It is not a proof that no other routine can ever write a cell through an alias. The large zone builder and all of its object-placement, loading and other helper branches have not been exhaustively closed for arbitrary indirect memory writes. No universal "immutable after fill" property is asserted.

## Eviction, prerequisite loss and seed transitions

The world's two locks at `+0x8000C0` and `+0x8000D8` are Win32 critical sections entered and left through the imported `EnterCriticalSection` and `LeaveCriticalSection`.

For valid zone coordinates, single-zone eviction at RVA `0x1A4890` locates the parent region and existing zone slot. If either is missing it does nothing. For a nonnull zone it enters world `+0x8000D8` then `+0x8000C0`, clears the region's zone slot, leaves `+0x8000C0` then `+0x8000D8`, invokes the zone-save path at RVA `0x1A5050` when world byte `+0xB4` is zero, and then calls the old zone's deleting destructor with flag 1. The save and destruction occur after the locks are released. There is no scalar refill or in-place scalar reset on this path. A following ordinary cache lookup misses because its zone slot is null; a following successful zone reconstruction creates new cells and runs the scalar fill again. The cache readers and the lookup routines acquire neither critical section. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A4890–0x1A4975`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005a4890.c`.]

The owner destruction is explicit. The zone's virtual-table address, written by zone construction, is RVA `0x31E280`; its first entry is RVA `0x224760`, which runs zone teardown `0x224390` and frees the zone when deletion flag bit 0 is set. Zone teardown handles zone `+0xA8` as a counted array: when the pointer is nonnull and the preceding count is nonzero it calls the first cell's first virtual entry, RVA `0x224700`, with flags 3. That entry runs cell teardown `0x050C30` over all cells using the recorded count and 32-byte stride, then frees the array allocation including its count word. Cell teardown restores the cell virtual-table address and frees its separate `+0x18` block storage when nonnull. This removes the scalar-owning objects rather than turning their scalar payloads into a reusable "invalid" state. A retained hint after this destruction is a dangling pointer, not a supported stale-cache read. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x224760–0x22477E`, `0x2243C3–0x2243EE` and `0x224700–0x224752`; `binary/Cube.exe`, virtual-table entries at RVAs `0x31E280` and `0x2FFD90` and cell-teardown continuation at `0x050C30–0x050C44`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/00624760.c`, `00624390.c`, `00624700.c` and `00450c30.c`.]

Whole-region eviction at RVA `0x1A4800` bounds region coordinates to zero through 1,023. For a resident region it first calls the region-save path at RVA `0x1A4AF0`, then enters world `+0x8000C0` then `+0x8000D8`, clears the world `+0xBC` region slot, leaves `+0x8000D8` then `+0x8000C0` and calls the region's deleting destructor with flag 1. This acquisition order is the reverse of the single-zone eviction order. The region virtual-table address is RVA `0x31DCCC`; its first entry is RVA `0x1C3FB0`, which runs region teardown `0x1C3CC0` and frees the region for flag bit 0. Region teardown visits all 64-by-64 zone pointer slots at region `+0x10018` and calls each nonnull zone's deleting destructor with flag 1. This invalidates ordinary column lookup by removing its owner hierarchy. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A4800–0x1A4882`, `0x1C3FB0–0x1C3FCE` and `0x1C3CF8–0x1C3D1C`; `binary/Cube.exe`, region virtual table at RVA `0x31DCCC`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005a4800.c`, `005c3fb0.c` and `005c3cc0.c`.]

Point-record eviction at RVA `0x1A4780` operates on the other table, world `+0x4000BC`, with the same zero-through-1,023 bounds. For a resident record it enters world `+0x8000C0` then `+0x8000D8`, clears that point-record slot, leaves them in reverse order and frees the record's memory directly, without a virtual destructor call. It does not visit the world `+0xBC` region table, zone slots, cell arrays or the three cached scalar words. Therefore, conditionally on a zone remaining alive and reachable, removal or later replacement of a point-record prerequisite alone does not force its existing scalar-cache hit to recompute. A cache miss instead takes the direct producer path, whose first two producers can return `0.5` as described above. This conditional result does not assert that the native maintenance scheduler normally leaves a retained zone with missing prerequisites. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A4780–0x1A47FC`; reader and producer ranges above; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005a4780.c`.]

One identified maintenance caller, RVA `0x06A8A0`, traverses the whole region table and, for each resident region, its zones before considering that region and its point record. In its normal retention branch it keeps a zone when the squared integer distance between its zone coordinates and some retained request's zone coordinates is below 16. It keeps a region when both absolute differences between its region coordinates and some retained request's parent-region coordinates are below 3, and keeps a point record under the wider per-axis condition below 5. For the same region-table slot its ordered calls are individual-zone evictions, then possible whole-region eviction, then possible point-record eviction. When its changed-selection flag is set, the retention exceptions are skipped, so each resident region, its resident zones and its point-record slot are passed to the eviction routines, and seed reset is invoked after the traversal. The handling of point-record slots whose region is not resident branches elsewhere and is not specified here. These observed ordering and padding differences are reasons not to turn the conditional stale-cache case into a claim that this caller routinely exposes it. Request-set construction, all caller scheduling, its elapsed-interval gate and inter-thread exclusion are outside this contract. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, eviction traversal at RVAs `0x06AD52–0x06AF6E` with calls at `0x06AE23`, `0x06AEBE` and `0x06AF25`, and reset call at `0x06AFAB`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/0046a8a0.c`.]

Seed reset at RVA `0x1A52E0` does more than change a key. It first stores the supplied 32-bit seed in world `+0x800164` and passes it to the imported `srand`. It then draws one `rand` value and stores its signed remainder modulo 100,000 in world `+0x800188`, and draws further values for additional seed-derived fields. Only afterward does it traverse all 1,024-by-1,024 region-table slots. For each resident region, when world byte `+0xB4` is zero it first passes every one of the region's 4,096 zone-slot values to the zone-save path `0x1A5050`; it then calls the region's deleting destructor with flag 1 and clears its world `+0xBC` slot. Independently of whether that region was resident, the corresponding point-record slot four megabytes (`0x400000` bytes) farther into the world is freed if nonnull and then cleared. Normal completion of this traversal leaves both pointer tables empty of their previous owners. The traversal's own instructions enter no world critical section; whether its save and destructor callees do was not traced. The original bytes confirm the point-record slot-clear instruction after its free call, which is absent from the assembly text export. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A5323–0x1A535E` and `0x1A5543–0x1A55E1`; `binary/Cube.exe`, omitted continuation at RVAs `0x1A55C0–0x1A55CC`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/005a52e0.c`.]

The reset's ordering establishes a bounded transition: seed and seed-derived state change before the traversal has destroyed every old zone. Before a particular old zone is destroyed, its scalar words have not been rewritten merely because the seed changed, and a permitted read of that still-live cell uses its stored payload without a seed-epoch check. This is not a promise that reading concurrently with reset is allowed, synchronized or safe. Region destruction precedes clearing its world pointer in this reset, so a pointer observed during destruction need not designate a usable owner. After a normally completed reset, null-hint lookup cannot recover the previous zone through those tables. A supplied pointer is not validated against the cleared hierarchy; after owner destruction its use remains invalid. There is no traced in-place scalar conversion from old seed to new seed.

Missing world region or zone pointers are thus misses, not hidden cell preservation locations. Missing point-record prerequisites are different: they can affect a direct producer while an existing independently owned column cache is still admitted. A completed reset removes both kinds of owner; single-zone and region eviction remove column owners; point-record eviction alone removes prerequisites. Those distinctions follow the actual pointer stores and destruction calls rather than a scalar-value heuristic.

## Precision, malformed state and limits

The contracted read and publication widths are binary32 at cell `+0x04`, `+0x08` and `+0x0C`, 32-bit pointers in the supported image and signed 32-bit coordinates for the stated admission tests. Coordinate products, shifts, slot indexing and address arithmetic are native 32-bit two's-complement operations; divisions by 256 and 64 and the matching remainders are signed and truncate toward zero, which equals floor division for the admitted nonnegative ranges. Within the normally admitted zone and column ranges the listed origins and local indices have the bounds described above. The cache readers themselves add no random draw, generation call, scalar store or seed comparison. Producer internals, noise arithmetic, file restoration and the complete region-generation random schedule remain separate dependencies; their identification here does not close their formulas.

This is not a malformed-memory parser contract. Invalid world or zone pointers, an invalid array count, allocation failure, null array storage inside a published zone, nonfinite scalar payloads and overflowed hint metadata have no recovered safe-recovery guarantee. A nonzero matching zone hint is dereferenced without ownership validation. The single-zone eviction helper does not repeat the complete signed zone-coordinate admission gate used by the ordinary zone lookup: it checks only the derived region coordinates, and its signed remainder arithmetic can produce a negative slot index for negative zone inputs. The eviction behavior above is for the valid native coordinate domain. Truncated save data, arbitrary memory corruption and allocation-exception paths have not been traced.

The scoped zone-save serializer at RVA `0x04E340`, reached from `0x1A5050`, writes selected zone, entity and entry collections and does not reference the `+0xA8` column array or its three scalar words in its inspected body. This supports no claim that every possible native transport or persistence format excludes those fields; corresponding load/overlay helpers and other formats were not exhaustively audited. [Source: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, bodies at RVAs `0x04E340` and `0x1A5050`; cross-reference `decompiled/client/functions/0044e340.c` and `005a5050.c`.]

The final guarantees are the explicit cache-read branches, direct scalar-producer associations, cell initialization, scalar fill and write order, existing-zone early return, identified table publication, named writer preservation and named owner-removal and reset order.

## Unresolved

- Region and point-record generation, file loading and overlay paths, and whether any load path restores or overwrites the scalar words.

## Re-certification (2026-11-22)

A fresh source-only author re-checked every statement in this document against the original client image and its original assembly export, including RVAs, the image hash, cell and table layout, initialization, fill and publication order, locks, offsets, widths and the `0.5` constant; exported bytes in the key spans were compared mechanically with the image. No behavioral claim was found to be wrong. One label was removed: the consumer at `0x1C56E0` is no longer described as doing "color/block-selection" work, because that interpretation was not confirmed, and the question moved to the unresolved list. Several statements were made more precise: the publication store reuses the parent-region pointer read before allocation, single-zone eviction takes its two locks in the opposite order to region and point-record eviction and saves and destroys only after releasing them, point records are freed directly, reset frees each point-record slot whether or not its region was resident and its traversal instructions take no world lock, the resize routine is a no-op for an unchanged count, and the random generator is identified as the imported `srand`/`rand` pair. Several inclusive range endpoints were corrected and the descriptive wording was rewritten as independent prose. What remains open is listed in the unresolved section: indirect-write closure, threading and lock coverage, scalar semantics and producer formulas, the random schedule, load paths, maintenance scheduling and the meaning of cell word `+0x14`.
