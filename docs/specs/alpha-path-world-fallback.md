# Original world fallback after a false route-consumer result

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD. This independent, code-free contract closes the composition writing actor `+0x160` after the false result of client helper RVA `0x1A7EB0`, inside world routine `0x20C510`. It also identifies bounded producers of the world `+0xB4` byte and the admission and placement of the later actor `+0x145C` addition. It does not repeat the route consumer's internal behavior or claim complete movement, transport, or server behavior.

The supported original is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, preferred image base `0x00400000`. At RVA `0x211BC7`, a nonzero world byte `+0xB4` skips the entire follower/fallback block and joins the subsequent actor timer work. ## Exact coordinate composition

The source coordinate is the **last** list node, selected through the header's previous link by RVA `0x2091A0`. This service reads the header pointer at list receiver `+0`, reads that header's `+4` link, and returns node `+8`. It neither searches for a nearest node nor copies, removes, or advances a list node. The three words at that returned address are read as signed 32-bit first, second, and third coordinates. The nonempty count test is the caller's only list-validity admission for this service. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x211CB8–0x211CCE`, `0x2091A0–0x2091A8`; `decompiled/client/functions/006091a0.c`, `005a6a70.c`.]

Before dereferencing the tail, the world constructs three identical signed 64-bit half-unit operands. Each of three calls to RVA `0x059C00` receives binary64 `0.5`, multiplies it by binary64 `65536`, stores that binary64 product, loads it into x87, and invokes native signed-64 conversion RVA `0x28D946`. The ordinary result of each conversion is raw integer 32768. The three scalar calls are made in eventual third-, second-, first-component order because of their stack placement; RVA `0x03B610` then copies the six words into a private triplet in first-, second-, third-component order. These are half units on **all three axes**; actor size and height do not supply these constants. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x211C57–0x211CB3`, `0x059C00–0x059C2B`, `0x03B610–0x03B63B`, `0x28D946–0x28D9BA`; `decompiled/client/functions/00459c00.c`, `0043b610.c`.]

The exact binary64 half is bits `0x3FE0000000000000` at RVA `0x345DE8`; the exact binary64 scale is bits `0x40F0000000000000` at `0x2FD588`. The conversion service's ordinary truncation is not a binary32 conversion of that product. The particular half-times-scale product is exactly representable and integral. The native conversion body supplies the reached signed-integer conversion; its exceptional or invalid-input behavior is not needed to turn these fixed finite constants into 32768.

After the last-node address has been obtained, RVA `0x1A6A70` reads its signed 32-bit coordinates in axis order and expands each into a signed 64-bit raw coordinate scaled by `65536`. Sign extension precedes the 16-bit left shift. The caller then invokes RVA `0x02C800` to add the private raw half-unit triplet, and RVA `0x02C7A0` to subtract the actor's current raw position at `+0x10/+0x18/+0x20`. Those actor position components are signed 64-bit values. The subtraction reads the actor position during this composition, after the route-consumer call and after list-tail selection. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x211CC7–0x211CE1`, `0x1A6A70–0x1A6AAE`, `0x02C800–0x02C854`, `0x02C7A0–0x02C7F4`; `decompiled/client/functions/005a6a70.c`, `0042c800.c`, `0042c7a0.c`.]

Each add/subtract service first copies its complete six-word receiver triplet into a private output, then performs first-, second-, third-component arithmetic. For each component it modifies the low 32-bit word followed by the high word with carry or borrow. Thus the subtraction is applied to the already formed tail-plus-half raw triplet; it is not a float subtraction of separately converted positions. Native arithmetic has 64-bit wrapping bit behavior and no clamp. The unwrapped signed-coordinate interpretation assumes the final difference is representable in signed 64 bits. The list-coordinate scaling and half addition themselves fit signed 64 bits for every signed 32-bit list coordinate. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, native bodies `0x02C800–0x02C854`, `0x02C7A0–0x02C7F4`.]

RVA `0x02C4A0` then writes binary32 actor `+0x160`, `+0x164`, and `+0x168`, in that order. For each axis it loads the complete signed 64-bit difference into x87, stores it as binary32, reloads and stores that binary32 once more, then multiplies it with scalar binary32 `1/65536` before the destination store. The inverse-scale bits are `0x37800000` at RVA `0x2FCD94`. The signed-64 difference is therefore narrowed **before** the power-of-two scaling. No binary64 division, direction normalization, magnitude cap, actor-height adjustment, or elapsed coefficient belongs to this composition. Its mathematical relation in the representable ordinary domain is the last grid coordinate plus half a unit on each axis, less the current raw actor position expressed in units, with the stated native narrowing before scaling. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x211CE1–0x211CEE`, `0x02C4A0–0x02C4FD`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042c4a0.c`.]

All three signed-64 differences have been constructed before the first destination float is stored. The destination triplet is separate from actor raw position, velocity `+0x34/+0x38/+0x3C`, and steering `+0x40/+0x44/+0x48`; this composition does not write those source/velocity/steering fields. It does not write actor `+0x145C`, alter a list or search map, allocate a container, append an event, query terrain or objects, access a random generator, or change a seed. The temporary coordinate services use caller stack storage. Floating-point conversion and multiplication inherit x87/MXCSR control; no private floating-point environment or protection scope is installed by this composition. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, call sequence `0x211C57–0x211CEE` and the service bodies cited above.]

## Bounded origin and lifecycle of the world gate

The byte read here is a real member of the world object, not merely an equal offset in another type. Client construction RVA `0x059C40` calls world construction RVA `0x18EB00` with receiver client `+0x2E4` and a literal-zero second stack argument. The world constructor copies that argument's low byte into its own `+0xB4` at `0x18EC0C`. The enclosing client member is consequently client `+0x398`. The original client-frame routine saves client `+0x2E4` as its world receiver and uses it for its calls to world RVA `0x20C510`. This independently connects the construction, client-relative alias, and world-update receiver. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x059F31–0x059F57`, `0x18EB2E–0x18EB36`, `0x18EC09–0x18EC12`, `0x089020–0x08902F`, `0x08CF1B–0x08CF49`; `decompiled/client/functions/0058eb00.c`.]

The byte is not immutable after construction. In client receiver routine RVA `0x06FC50`, a supplied string-like object's 32-bit length at `+0x10` is checked for zero. A nonzero length reaches a literal-one byte store to client `+0x398` at `0x06FCA7`, before the socket-creation call and before connection/handshake completion. Zero length exits without that store. Identified network-error/cleanup branches of the same routine clear the client socket word and then store byte zero to `+0x398` at `0x06FE47`, `0x06FEE9`, `0x07018C`, `0x070745`, and `0x070931`. The original network routine and its client-embedded-world service calls establish receiver identity; the byte's being one is not proof of a completed connection. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x06FC82–0x06FCB3` and the five clearing sites with their preceding socket-word stores; `decompiled/client/functions/0046fc50.c`.]

Client routine RVA `0x0719F0` supplies another confirmed clear boundary. It first requires client byte `+0x800585` nonzero. On that admitted route, a nonzero socket reaches close, socket-word zero, and a `+0x398` byte-zero store at `0x071A5B`. After its handle waits and a call with receiver client `+0x2E4` to RVA `0x201CB0`, it stores zero to `+0x398` again at `0x071AAD`, even when the earlier socket check had skipped the first clear. When the initial byte admission is false this routine bypasses these writes. Full connection failure classification, handle/synchronization policy, world-reset internals, other aliases, and exhaustive writers are not closed here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x071A1A–0x071AAD`; `decompiled/client/functions/004719f0.c`.]

The world fallback simply tests this raw byte against zero. It neither validates connection progress nor reads the client socket to derive its admission. Noncanonical nonzero byte values follow the same skip branch. The separately reached world checks described below read the byte again; no single immutable per-world-call gate snapshot or concurrent-writer exclusion is established.

## Limits

The contract assumes valid caller-owned actor/list storage and stable readable tail and position data. A nonzero count with a malformed or empty header chain has no established normalized fallback. No lock, map/list lifetime pin, ownership transfer, or atomic three-component publication is established in the composition. Invalid conversion control, unmasked exceptions, concurrent list or actor replacement, and exceptional service/allocator behavior in the intervening world work remain explicit boundaries.

The output has only the numeric identity established here: a binary32 triplet derived from the current last grid coordinate, three fixed half units, and current actor raw position. Broader consumers of actor `+0x160`, complete route/search production, the internals of the route consumer and physical services, network success invariants, every gate-byte alias/writer, frame scheduling, and any server counterpart remain outside this bounded original-client audit. The coordinate composition and counter store themselves introduce no random draw, seed access, event append, or protection operation.
