# Alpha client world-creature health admission

## Scope and evidence

This contract establishes the client world-update boundary at RVAs `0x211A96`–`0x211ADB` in routine `0x20C510`: where it occurs in the per-creature pass, how it queries and stores maximum health, and which values continue into the remaining creature work. It describes this client caller only. It makes no claim about a server counterpart or the complete maximum-health formula.

The supported original is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, image base `0x00400000`. Addresses below are RVAs. Evidence paths are relative to the original evidence root. Original binary instructions were checked directly where the exported assembly omits switch destinations. No prior contracts, reconstruction or replacement implementation material were used.

## Place in the creature pass

This is a particular pass over the world creature collection at world `+4`, not the first creature-related work performed by the whole world-update routine. The pass obtains a creature pointer from the current collection entry and caches it. A null pointer branches immediately to the common iterator-advance boundary at RVA `0x21FE21`. It does not inspect that creature's queued records, query its maximum health or enter the subsequent creature phases. The null case does not imply that earlier world-wide passes did nothing. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, pass setup at RVAs `0x210493`–`0x2104E6`; earlier maximum-health calls at `0x20C5C8`, `0x20C612` and `0x20C634`.]

For a non-null creature, the code first walks the creature's list at `+0x130C`, with count at `+0x1310`. Each record's selector is an unsigned byte at record `+0x128`; selectors `1` through `8` select native switch destinations, while other values take the record-advance path. All records reached by that iteration are handled before the maximum-health/admission boundary. The list can be empty; that case proceeds directly to the conditional list clear and then the health boundary. There is no general positive-health prerequisite before this record pass. Individual record handlers retain their own gates. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x2104EC`–`0x210546` and `0x211A44`–`0x211A80`; `binary/Cube.exe`, switch table at RVA `0x222C20` and switch destinations within `0x21054D`–`0x211A3E`.]

After the record iteration finishes, world byte `+0xB4` controls list consumption. When that byte is zero, the native clear helper empties the creature's `+0x130C` list, sets its count to zero and frees its nodes while retaining the list sentinel. When the byte is nonzero, this clear is skipped. Both paths then converge at RVA `0x211A96`. Thus a later nonpositive-health exit does not preserve consumed records for a retry and does not reverse record side effects. Conversely, nonzero world `+0xB4` does not bypass the maximum query or health admission. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211A58`–`0x211A96`; `binary/Cube.exe`, clear helper at RVA `0x06F870`–`0x06F8A3`.]

The record handlers preceding admission are substantive work. Two directly checked examples establish why admission cannot be moved ahead of that pass as a behavioral claim:

- The selector-1 handler, outside its special item-category/subtype branch and with world `+0xB4` zero, obtains an item-dependent scalar from helper RVA `0x0C6E10`, stores that return as binary32, adds it to current health using a binary32 addition and writes the result to creature `+0x16C`. Associated output construction follows before record advancement. The scalar formula and complete item-use eligibility remain dependencies outside this contract.
- At the start of the selector-3 handler, an action byte `0x4F` at creature `+0x68` is replaced with zero and the 32-bit field at `+0x1190` is set to zero. That occurs before the later health admission and before the handler's subsequent branches.

Other native record destinations also perform position/vector changes, world changes, output construction and random calls. Their full behavior and draw counts are not specified here. The established ordering is that any such work actually selected and completed precedes the health query; it is not rolled back when the later admission rejects the creature. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, selector-1 destination at RVAs `0x2110A4`–`0x2112F2`, health addition at `0x211212`–`0x21123B`; selector-3 beginning at `0x21094A`–`0x210964`; position/action/vector work at `0x210B95`–`0x210DD8`; random-call sites within `0x2108BA`–`0x21090C` and `0x210E80`–`0x210F50`.]

## Maximum query, narrowing and optional replacement

Every non-null creature that reaches RVA `0x211A96` invokes maximum-health helper RVA `0x044DB0` once, before testing whether its current health is positive. The caller supplies the cached creature pointer. The helper returns through the x87 floating-point register stack; the caller immediately stores and pops that result into a 32-bit floating-point temporary at RVA `0x211AA1`. This stored binary32 value is the comparison maximum. The helper's own checked return path loads a stored binary32 value onto x87 before returning. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211A96`–`0x211AA1`; `binary/Cube.exe`, maximum-helper return path at RVAs `0x044FD8`–`0x044FDE`.]

Only after that first call and store does the caller load the creature's current health from its 32-bit field at `+0x16C`. It compares that binary32 health as the first operand against the binary32 comparison maximum as the second operand. The comparison is the native scalar single-precision SSE comparison at RVA `0x211AB5`. No binary64 widening or extended-precision comparison replaces this boundary. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211AA7`–`0x211ABC`; `binary/Cube.exe`, the same instruction bytes at RVAs `0x211AAD`–`0x211ABC`.]

The first comparison has these outcomes when floating-point execution reaches the following branch:

| Relationship seen by the comparison | Maximum-helper calls in this boundary | Health write by the maximum-replacement step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current health strictly greater than the first stored maximum, with an ordered comparison | Two | A new result from the second call is stored directly into health as binary32. |
| Current health equal to or below the first stored maximum, with an ordered comparison | One | None. |
| Unordered comparison because either operand is NaN | One | None. |

On the strict-greater path the caller invokes RVA `0x044DB0` again at RVA `0x211AC0`. It does not store the first comparison temporary into health. Instead, it reloads the creature pointer and stores and pops the second x87 return directly into the binary32 health field at RVA `0x211ACB`. There is no comparison between the two helper results, no third maximum call in this boundary and no second maximum comparison after this replacement. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211AB5`–`0x211ACB`.]

The two calls must remain distinct observations of the helper. This bounded caller trace does not substitute an assumed identical second value for the native second call. In particular, it does not replace the sequence with an abstract minimum operation: equality and unordered values skip the replacement, and a replacement uses the fresh second return. Other parts of the world routine can make their own maximum-health calls; the one-or-two count above applies only to this boundary for this pass. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211A96`–`0x211ACB`, and independent earlier calls at `0x20C5C8`–`0x20C634`.]

## Admission after the maximum step

The caller next creates positive zero in the comparison register, with binary32 bits `0x00000000`, and compares that zero as the first operand against the health field as the second operand. It rereads the field, so admission sees either the unchanged health or the second helper return just stored there. The branch at RVA `0x211ADB` goes to the common creature-advance boundary when the comparison is ordered and zero is greater than or equal to health. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211AD1`–`0x211ADB`; `binary/Cube.exe`, the same instructions at RVAs `0x211AD1`–`0x211AE0`.]

| Health value seen at this final comparison | Result, if the comparison completes |
| --- | --- |
| Positive finite value or positive infinity | Continue at RVA `0x211AE1`. |
| Positive zero or negative zero | Advance to the next creature at RVA `0x21FE21`. |
| Negative finite value or negative infinity | Advance to the next creature at RVA `0x21FE21`. |
| NaN, producing an unordered comparison | Continue at RVA `0x211AE1`. |

The unordered result is material. The native branch tests the comparison's carry condition, so an unordered value follows the same continuation direction as a positive value. It does not perform an explicit NaN rejection. A NaN current-health value also skips the earlier maximum replacement because that first comparison is unordered. A NaN first maximum skips replacement of an otherwise ordered current-health value; the final decision then depends on the health field. These descriptions are conditional on native floating-point execution continuing normally. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, comparisons and conditional branches at RVAs `0x211AB5`–`0x211ABC` and `0x211AD4`–`0x211ADB`.]

The admission decision does not itself set zero or negative health to zero, destroy a creature, remove its collection entry, reset its action, emit a death record or return from the whole world routine. It branches to the iterator advance. Any optional maximum replacement and any earlier record effects have already occurred. The native advance updates the current iterator, checks the collection end and either returns to the next creature at RVA `0x2104D0` or proceeds into the later world phase at RVA `0x21FE61`. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211AD1`–`0x211ADB` and `0x21FE21`–`0x21FE67`; `decompiled/client/functions/00468d70.c`, RVA `0x068D70`; `decompiled/client/functions/005c3ea0.c`, RVA `0x1C3EA0`.]

“Living” at this boundary therefore means an admitted comparison result, not a separate persistent state classification. With ordinary positive finite maxima, a nonpositive health value reaches the maximum query and then exits this pass's remainder. Nevertheless, a queued action may have changed health before the query, and the optional replacement may have changed it before admission. The decision is made from the field at the final comparison, rather than from health at function entry or before the queued-record pass. No creature role, class, specialization or world `+0xB4` check is added to the maximum/admission block itself. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x2104D0`–`0x210546` and `0x211A86`–`0x211AE1`; `binary/Cube.exe`, preceding health addition at RVAs `0x211212`–`0x21123B`.]

## Work that follows admission

The per-creature behavior callback is after admission. The first continuation checks creature role byte `+0x60` and world byte `+0xB4`. With a nonzero role and zero world byte, the routine adds the update interval to the 32-bit accumulator at creature `+0x13C4`. Provided the signed field at creature `+0x12C` is not positive and the accumulator is at least `32`, it invokes the first virtual entry of the behavior object at `+0x13E4` when that pointer is non-null. The call receives the creature, world, accumulated interval and output aggregate. The accumulator is then reset to zero even when the behavior pointer was null. A positive `+0x12C` skips that callback path and leads to clearing flag `0x80` in the creature flags at `+0x124`. These effects do not occur on the health-admission exit. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211AE1`–`0x211B75`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042f160.c`, RVA `0x02F160`.]

The subsequent route/steering-related work also follows the health boundary. Under its own world and creature-state gates, it inspects the list at creature `+0x1460`, calls route helper RVA `0x1A7EB0`, updates movement-related flags and the vector at `+0x160`, and can call route-replacement helper RVA `0x1A7C90`. Their internal route semantics and success dependencies are not recovered here; the established fact is their caller-side placement after admission. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211BC7`–`0x211DFC`, particularly `0x211C30`, `0x211CEE` and `0x211DE0`; `decompiled/client/functions/005a7c90.c`, RVA `0x1A7C90`.]

Other later phases include the decrement/clamp work on creature timers at `+0x130`, `+0x134` and `+0x138`, the binary32 adjustment at `+0x13C`, and action-byte dispatch from `+0x68`. The common continuation following the action-specific paths performs cooldown work and then resident-list work at `+0x1178`: resident times are decremented and expired entries are collected for removal. Later action interruption/elapsed-time work, regeneration and movement/vector work remain within the admitted portion of this pass. The health exit goes directly to `0x21FE21`, beyond these portions, rather than executing them and merely suppressing a final movement store. This does not claim that every admitted creature executes every optional branch. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, timer work at RVAs `0x211D07`–`0x211DD0`, action dispatch at `0x211E04`–`0x211E1B`, common action continuation at `0x215EC0`–`0x215EC6` leading to `0x211F41`, cooldown/resident entry at `0x212048`–`0x212163`, resident continuation at `0x218402`–`0x2184C2`, action elapsed/interruption work from `0x2184C2`, health changes at `0x218A69`–`0x218A9E`, and later vector work before `0x21FDFC`.]

Admission is not a promise that health stays positive throughout those later phases. For example, the later creature-type `0x90` branch can reduce health and clamp a negative result to zero after the behavior portion has been reached. That code continues onward rather than returning to this admission check immediately. The health test specified here is an entry boundary for the remainder of this pass, not a check that precedes every individual later effect. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm`, RVAs `0x211B7B`–`0x211BC7`, followed by `0x211BC7`–`0x211D07`.]

## Unclosed dependencies and exceptional execution

The maximum-health helper's complete level/rank/item formula, imported power behavior and recursive side-effect properties are not closed by this caller contract. The item-dependent preceding health addition, the full queued-record switch, behavior-object implementations and route/movement helpers likewise remain independent dependencies. Their presence must not be replaced with inferred no-ops or with a different phase order. The boundary itself contains no explicit random draw, output-record append or world-ownership decision between its maximum calls and its admission branch.

The checked instructions do not establish a universal x87 control word or SSE control/status configuration. The 32-bit x87 stores are native conversion boundaries, including applicable rounding and exceptional-store behavior; the comparisons use the native SSE environment. NaNs can raise invalid-comparison exceptions, and an unmasked exception can prevent a branch from being reached. Denormal handling, exception flags, NaN payload/quieting details and unusual control-state effects are not generalized beyond the caller's actual operations. The ordered/unordered tables describe comparisons that complete, not a guarantee that every exceptional input returns normally.
