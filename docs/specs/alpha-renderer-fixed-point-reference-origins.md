# Alpha renderer fixed-point reference and origin inputs

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD. This original-only contract establishes the widths and numeric roles of client `+0x140`, `+0x1D8`, and `+0x1E0`, the identified client-update producer, and representative origin composition within renderer RVA `0x0AC260`. It distinguishes a render reference from the separate anchor used for horizontal shifts. It does not assign a player or camera identity to either triplet.

The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, under the evidence root `disassembled alpha cube world`. All addresses below are RVAs in that image; its preferred image base is `0x00400000`. Evidence paths are relative to that root. The original executable and scoped original client assembly/function exports establish the facts below. No reconstruction, replacement implementation, or prior contract was read for this scope.

## Fields, widths, and scale

The renderer receiver is called the client here, following the assigned scope. These offsets belong to that receiver and must not be confused with equally numbered fields in a creature or a stack temporary.

| Client field | Established representation and role |
| --- | --- |
| `+0x140`, `+0x148`, `+0x150` | Three signed 64-bit fixed-point components of the render reference, called R below |
| `+0x158`, `+0x160`, `+0x168` | A separate signed 64-bit fixed-point anchor triplet, called A below |
| `+0x1D8` | One signed 64-bit horizontal shift, called Sx |
| `+0x1E0` | One signed 64-bit horizontal shift, called Sy |

Each component uses **65536 fixed-point counts per coordinate unit**, with sixteen fractional bits. The full eight bytes matter. For example, the high halves of Sx and Sy are client `+0x1DC` and `+0x1E4`; they are not separate shift fields. The renderer constructs a shift triplet from Sx, Sy, and a signed 64-bit zero for its third component. There is no corresponding automatic vertical rebase in these observed paths. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, renderer RVA `0x0AD840–0x0AD8AC`, update stores `0x09CD02–0x09CD2C`; `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`; fixed-point helpers `decompiled/client/functions/00412080.c`, RVA `0x012080`, and `004120f0.c`, RVA `0x0120F0`.]

The fixed-point-to-binary32 conversion first loads a signed 64-bit integer through x87 and stores it as binary32. It then multiplies that binary32 result by exact binary32 `1/65536`, bits `0x37800000`, and supplies a binary32 result. The triplet converter repeats this per component. The reverse triplet conversion multiplies each binary32 input by exact binary32 `65536`, bits `0x47800000`, then uses runtime helper RVA `0x28D946` to convert to signed 64-bit integer, truncating toward zero for finite representable values. This boundary does not promise equivalence to converting an already divided integer or to retaining binary64 through the scale operation. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0120F0–0x012117` and `0x02C460–0x02C4FD`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042c460.c`, `0042c4a0.c`, and `0068d946.c`.]

Fixed-point triplet copies transfer six four-byte words. Triplet additions and subtractions perform three independent 64-bit operations with carry or borrow. They neither saturate nor discard the low sixteen fractional bits. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0042c5b0.c`, RVA `0x02C5B0`; `0042c7a0.c`, RVA `0x02C7A0`; `0042c800.c`, RVA `0x02C800`; `0042c9d0.c`, RVA `0x02C9D0`.]

## Identified update producer

The containing update routine begins at RVA `0x088EE0`. The bounded producer examined here is its reference/origin preparation around `0x09C2E6–0x09CEB1`. It is reached after substantial other update work. This contract specifies that executed preparation path; it does not assert that every entry to the containing routine reaches it, or that it is the only possible initialization/reset path over the client's lifetime. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`; `binary/Cube.exe`, the cited producer ranges.]

The update treats A as state distinct from the triplet at `+0x10` of the creature pointer held at client `+0x8006D0`. It subtracts that creature triplet from A in fixed point, converts the difference to binary32, and evaluates the sum of the three squared components. When that result is ordered above binary32 `2500`, bits `0x451C4000`, it copies the whole creature triplet into A. At equality, below the threshold, or unordered comparison if execution completes, this particular snap is skipped. This is a squared-distance threshold of 50 coordinate units for ordinary finite values; there is no square root in the test. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, RVA `0x09C367–0x09C3BF`; `decompiled/client/functions/00424860.c`, RVA `0x024860`; `binary/Cube.exe`, constant RVA `0x302ABC`.]

Subsequent branches can update A without simply copying that creature position. In the ordinary branch, its horizontal components are moved toward the selected creature's corresponding components through fixed-point arithmetic and a time/count-dependent coefficient. Its vertical component receives separate action/shape-dependent updates. Another branch obtains the binary32 triplet at embedded-world `+0x8000F0`, converts it to fixed point, and copies it into A; it can derive a replacement vertical value through cell helpers and then adds a fixed 50-coordinate-unit vertical amount. The alternate branch is selected when any of four client-owned objects at `+0x800874`, `+0x800888`, `+0x800894`, or `+0x80088C` satisfies the predicate at RVA `0x07FA10`. Those predicates are numeric/object-state gates here, without an inferred interface-mode name. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, RVA `0x09C3C4–0x09C76A`; `decompiled/client/functions/00487fe0.c`, RVA `0x087FE0`; `0047fa10.c`, RVA `0x07FA10`; `00468ca0.c`, RVA `0x068CA0`; `00412200.c`, RVA `0x012200`; `0042c9b0.c`, RVA `0x02C9B0`.]

These anchor-update observations close the distinction between A and a raw creature position. They do not close the entire anchor-following policy: the step-count producer, all action-dependent vertical targets, fixed-point interpolation helpers, and cell-height dependencies remain upstream inputs to the bounded reference/origin relation below. In particular, this contract supplies no rule that A always equals the creature position.

### Construction and later replacement of R

The update builds a temporary binary32 matrix from identity and the binary32 orientation-input triplet at client `+0x1A4`. It applies the component at `+0x1AC` through helper RVA `0x024610`, the component at `+0x1A8` through `0x0244F0`, and the component at `+0x1A4` through `0x0243D0`, in that order. Each rotation helper first multiplies its input in binary32 by the stored angular coefficient `0x3C8EFA35`, approximately `0.0174532923847`, then widens to binary64 for imported cosine and sine calls and narrows their results to binary32 for its matrix arithmetic. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, RVA `0x09C2E6–0x09C362`; helper exports `00423e70.c`, `00424610.c`, `004244f0.c`, and `004243d0.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, constant RVA `0x2FCDA4`.]

At RVA `0x09C76F–0x09C782`, R is initialized by copying A. The temporary matrix transforms the binary32 direction `(0, 0, 1)` through its three-by-three portion, using helper RVA `0x088E50`; its translation entries are not added by this helper. The resulting binary32 direction is multiplied component by component by the current binary32 distance input. The resulting triplet is converted to fixed point, including the binary32 multiplication by 65536 and signed-64-bit truncation described above. The update subtracts this fixed-point displacement from R. Thus this construction is A minus the converted direction/distance displacement. It is not a copy of A with an independently rounded vertical offset. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x09C76F–0x09C802`; `decompiled/client/functions/00488e50.c`, RVA `0x088E50`; `00451510.c`, RVA `0x051510`; `0042c460.c`, RVA `0x02C460`; `0042c9d0.c`, RVA `0x02C9D0`.]

The update then runs world-query/clipping work with multiple offset samples. After that work, at RVA `0x09CA16–0x09CA68`, it recomputes a converted direction/distance displacement and writes R again as A minus that displacement. The resolved binary32 distance is separately stored at client `+0x1CC`. A later conditional world-query branch can replace R once more with the selected creature's `+0x10` triplet plus another converted binary32 displacement, at `0x09CBBD–0x09CBDE`. These are actual replacements of the reference supplied to later matrices and rendering. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, RVA `0x09C807–0x09CBE3`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x09CA16–0x09CA8E` and `0x09CBBD–0x09CBDE`.]

The precise ray/cell admission, collision geometry, sample ordering, and distance-selection policy of those world-query dependencies are not audited here. Their unresolved behavior must not be replaced with an assumption that R equals A or the selected creature position. The closed result at this boundary is the triplet actually written by those copy/add/subtract paths.

### Shift production

After the reference preparation, the update negates A's first 64-bit component using helper RVA `0x012260` and copies all eight result bytes to client `+0x1D8`. It then negates A's second component and copies all eight bytes to client `+0x1E0`. The negation is integer two's-complement negation, including the low-word carry into the high word. There is no floating-point conversion, integer-coordinate division, chunk-index conversion, or fractional-bit clearing in these two stores. They occur in first-component, then second-component order. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x09CCF7–0x09CD2C`; `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, including the saved A-component addresses established at `0x09C44A–0x09C4C7` or `0x09C6D3–0x09C6F1`; `decompiled/client/functions/00412260.c`, RVA `0x012260`; `00411df0.c`, RVA `0x011DF0`.]

Consequently, on this completed update path Sx is negative A.x and Sy is negative A.y in native 64-bit arithmetic. The shifts are **not** established as negative R.x and negative R.y. They can retain fractional-coordinate precision, and are not rounded to a 32-unit chunk boundary. Their vertical companion in renderer composition is zero, rather than negative A.z or negative R.z.

## Renderer composition of the reference and other positions

At RVA `0x0AD840–0x0AD8AC`, the renderer constructs the three-component shift `(Sx, Sy, 0)` and adds it to R in fixed point through helper `0x02C800`. Only after this 64-bit composition does it convert each component to binary32 and multiply by `1/65536`. The result is passed to helper RVA `0x048010` on the object held at client `+0x800580`. The numeric input is therefore the horizontally shifted reference: R.x plus Sx, R.y plus Sy, and unchanged R.z, converted component by component. This establishes the submitted input, not the receiving helper's complete rendering meaning. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0AD840–0x0AD92C`; `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042c800.c`.]

Elsewhere, the same renderer subtracts other signed-64-bit positions from R before conversion. For example, the object-position difference at RVA `0x0AC93B–0x0AC9CC` reads all six words of R and performs three 64-bit subtractions with borrow, producing R minus the object-position triplet. This is a separate relation from applying the horizontal shifts. A distance or direction relative to R does not establish that the shift origin is R. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, RVA `0x0AC93B–0x0AC9DB`; representative subtractor `decompiled/client/functions/0042c7a0.c`, RVA `0x02C7A0`.]

The shifts are also assembled into fixed-point translation triplets and added to object positions before binary32 conversion, for example at `0x0B18BE–0x0B190B`. The third shift component is explicitly encoded as zero. The fixed-point add precedes the narrowing. Separately converting a large position and large negative shift to binary32 and then adding them is not the observed numeric order. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, RVA `0x0B18BE–0x0B190B`; `decompiled/client/functions/0043b610.c`, RVA `0x03B610`; `0042c800.c`; `0042c4a0.c`.]

## Horizontal chunk-origin composition

A representative chunk-key path in the renderer at RVA `0x0B1C8C–0x0B1D1D` selects the two four-byte key components from an object at `+0x18`, using helper RVA `0x028970`. The selected components are integer values. Each is first shifted left by five in **32 bits**, giving the 32-coordinate-unit chunk-origin component. Helper RVA `0x012080` then sign-extends that resulting signed 32-bit integer and encodes it as signed 64-bit fixed point by multiplying by 65536. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0B1C9D–0x0B1CC2` and `0x0B1CE8–0x0B1CFF`; `decompiled/client/functions/00412080.c`, RVA `0x012080`.]

The second key component is composed with Sy first, using a full 64-bit add through helper RVA `0x0122C0`, then converted to binary32 through helper `0x0120F0`. The first component is subsequently composed with Sx in the same way. The resulting two binary32 values become the first and second translation inputs to matrix helper RVA `0x024A60`. The third translation input in this observed draw path is separately loaded as a signed four-byte value from its draw record at `+0x1C` and converted to binary32. It is not supplied by a third client shift. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x0B1C8C–0x0B1D1D`; `decompiled/client/functions/004122c0.c`, RVA `0x0122C0`; `004120f0.c`; `00424a60.c`, RVA `0x024A60`.]

For ordinary representable coordinates, the horizontal translation is the 32-unit chunk origin minus the corresponding horizontal anchor component, expressed in coordinate units. The native precision contract is more specific: five-bit 32-bit shift, signed fixed-point encoding, 64-bit shift addition, signed-64-bit-to-binary32 narrowing, and binary32 multiplication by `1/65536`, in that order. In particular, the multiplication by 32 is not widened before the initial shift, and the fixed-point sum is not replaced with a binary32 subtraction. No saturation or overflow recovery is present in these helpers. This establishes the inspected chunk-key path; it is not a complete enumeration of every mesh, detail level, or draw branch in the renderer.

## Matrix reference frames and limits

The update constructs multiple matrices using these fields. At client `+0x22C`, it resets a binary32 4-by-4 matrix, applies the negated orientation inputs through helpers `0x0243D0`, `0x0244F0`, and `0x024610` in that order, then applies the converted negative-reference triplet. This construction does not include Sx or Sy. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x09CC59–0x09CCF2`; `decompiled/client/functions/00423e70.c`, `004243d0.c`, `004244f0.c`, `00424610.c`, `00468e70.c`, and `00424990.c`.]

At client `+0x26C`, after its separate orientation preparation, the update constructs the fixed-point negative-reference triplet and subtracts `(Sx, Sy, 0)` from it before converting the result to binary32 and applying matrix translation. For the ordinary nonoverflowing fixed-point-negation range, this translation input is negative of the horizontally shifted reference. Thus positions submitted with the horizontal shifts and this matrix's translation share the same rebased frame. This is a numeric frame relationship; no camera identity or complete matrix/GPU convention is inferred from it. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, RVA `0x09CD31–0x09CEB1`, particularly `0x09CE43–0x09CEB1`; `decompiled/client/functions/00468e70.c`, RVA `0x068E70`; `0042c7a0.c`; `0042c4a0.c`; `00424990.c`, RVA `0x024990`.]

The negative-reference helper used for these matrices is distinct from the exact scalar negator used for Sx and Sy. It copies a triplet, multiplies each fixed-point component by signed fixed-point negative one, `-65536`, using the low native 64-bit product, then divides by 65536 with signed division truncated toward zero. It acts as ordinary negation where that product is representable; the contract does not replace its overflow behavior with unlimited-precision negation. This does not weaken the separately established exact two-word negation that produces the horizontal shifts. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00468e70.c`, RVA `0x068E70`; `0042c900.c`, RVA `0x02C900`; scalar negator `00412260.c`, RVA `0x012260`.]

The matrix translation helpers operate on binary32 matrix entries and the already converted binary32 translation inputs. They update the final four entries using ordered products and additions with the existing basis columns. This contract establishes their coordinate inputs and operation boundary, without closing projection construction, all orientation modifiers, clipping, shaders, vertex formats, handedness conventions, or final screen-space behavior. Imported sine/cosine approximation and the ambient x87/SSE rounding and exception controls remain numeric dependencies. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00424990.c`, RVA `0x024990`; `00424a60.c`, RVA `0x024A60`; rotation helper exports cited above; original renderer assembly.]

Initial field values, every alternate writer/reset, the entire anchor-following and clipping policy, reference/anchor ownership, and frame synchronization are explicitly unclosed. The fixed-point copy, negate, add, subtract, and conversion helpers inspected here make no game random draws, but this is not a no-randomness claim for the containing client-update or renderer routines. The closed findings are field widths and scale, the identified update relations, the distinction between R and A, horizontal shift production, and the inspected fixed-point origin compositions before matrix processing.
