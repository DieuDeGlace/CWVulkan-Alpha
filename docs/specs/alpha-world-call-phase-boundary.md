# Alpha client world-call phase boundary

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD after complete author readback. This is an independent, original-only contract for the outer call boundary of client world routine RVA `0x20C510` and its enclosing call sequence in client routine RVA `0x088EE0`. It does not specify the complete internals of either large routine.

## Evidence and terminology

The supported image is original `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, preferred image base `0x00400000`. Evidence paths in this document are relative to the original evidence root, `Documents/disassembled alpha cube world`. Addresses below are RVAs unless explicitly described as image addresses. Original discovered assembly was checked against scoped disassembly of the supported binary at the world entry and the frame call sequence. The original world decompilation is incomplete; missing switch bodies are not treated as evidence that those bodies do nothing.

The principal evidence is `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/0060c510.asm` and `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/00488ee0.asm`, supplemented by the individually cited original helper exports. No reconstruction, replacement implementation, runtime observations or earlier project contracts were used.

“World” means the object supplied in ECX at `0x20C510`. The first stack argument is a signed 32-bit elapsed value. The second is a pointer to a caller-owned mutable aggregate, called the aggregate below. The third is a nullable pointer to a separate optional input aggregate. These are saved or read at `0x20C541–0x20C564` and `0x20CD1F`; normal completion removes twelve argument bytes at `0x221462`. Field offsets are relative to the object or aggregate explicitly named. Numeric byte selectors and ownership-related bytes are retained as offsets and values where their wider meaning is not closed.

## Enclosing client call sequence and aggregate lifetime

On the examined path in `0x088EE0`, the caller constructs one aggregate at `0x08CE2E–0x08CE35`, using helper `0x066880`. Its thirteen list headers occupy eight-byte intervals from aggregate `+0x00` through `+0x60`; each has a head pointer and a 32-bit count. This constructor runs outside the subsequent world-call loop. The caller loads a signed 32-bit value at `0x08CE3E` and stores it in client `+0x8006E8` at `0x08CE42`. That loaded value is the elapsed value discussed in the subdivision below. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00466880.c`; frame assembly, `0x08CE2E–0x08CE48`.]

If client `+0x8006C8` is nonnull, three helper calls occur in this order before world-call admission and subdivision: `0x0DD6A0` copies the backend list at `+0x34` into aggregate `+0x00`; `0x0DD5E0` copies backend `+0x3C` into aggregate `+0x58`; `0x0DD640` copies backend `+0x44` into aggregate `+0x20`. Each appends records in source traversal order, resets its own backend head links and count, and frees the old backend nodes. These are copies into destination nodes, not transfer of the same nodes. The three operations separately enter and leave the protection object addressed through backend `+0x04`; they do not establish an atomic snapshot across all three queues. [Evidence: frame assembly, `0x08CE48–0x08CE99`; `decompiled/client/functions/004dd6a0.c`, `004dd5e0.c`, `004dd640.c`, `004540c0.c`, `004544d0.c`, `00453ff0.c`; original binary, `0x0DD5E0–0x0DD6F5`.]

Two independent comparisons then admit the world-call sequence: a value obtained through helper `0x0787F0` must match client `+0x800A50`, and helper `0x051440` must accept the pair supplied at `0x08CEB4–0x08CEBF`. Failure jumps to later frame work at `0x08D5F0`. These comparisons are not elapsed-positive tests. The complete meaning and upstream maintenance of their compared values remain outside this contract. [Evidence: frame assembly, `0x08CE9D–0x08CEC9`.]

Once admitted, the signed elapsed value is compared with `20`. Values greater than `20` produce repeated calls with elapsed `20`, followed by one final call with a remainder from `1` through `20`. The repeated-call count is the integer quotient of elapsed minus one divided by twenty. An elapsed value of `20` produces only the final call with `20`; `40` produces two calls with `20`; `21` produces `20` followed by `1`. Values at most `20` bypass the repeated-call loop and are supplied unchanged to the final call, including zero and negative values. The final call is not conditional on a nonzero remainder. Both call sites pass the same aggregate address and a null optional input pointer. There is no aggregate reconstruction, backend queue drain or aggregate clearing between these calls. [Evidence: original binary and frame assembly, `0x08CEF4–0x08CF49`, world calls at `0x08CF2E` and `0x08CF49`.]

The elapsed value at this boundary is not an unconditional copy of an operating-system duration. Earlier, if client byte `+0x8006E5` is nonzero, the caller divides its then-current signed value by four with truncation toward zero, replaces any result below one with one, and makes an imported sleep call with argument five on that replacement path. This conditional adjustment is at `0x08C100–0x08C134`. Other upstream selection and frame branches are not closed here; the subdivision contract begins with the value actually loaded at `0x08CE3E`.

After the final world call, the enclosing routine continues substantial frame work. It later traverses the retained aggregate hit and buff lists at `0x09C044` and `0x09C0FC` on a connection-dependent path, and calls aggregate cleanup helper `0x067C70` at `0x09D365`. Those are enclosing-call activities, not activities repeated by the subdivision loop. The downstream filtering, packet construction and transport are outside this contract. [Evidence: frame assembly, `0x08CF4E`, `0x09C02F–0x09C124`, `0x09D35E–0x09D365`.]

## Outer order within each world invocation

The following order follows the observed branches and loop joins, rather than assuming ascending instruction addresses describe execution. In particular, the projectile collision body lies after the normal return address in the image and branches back into the projectile loop.

### Preliminary creature scan, health addition and clocks

Before the optional input lists or the main actor traversal, the routine scans the world creature collection at world `+0x04`. A qualifying entry is nonnull, has byte `+0x60` equal to zero, byte `+0x68` equal to `0x54`, and binary32 health at `+0x16C` ordered greater than zero. Any qualifying entry sets a local flag. Only a qualifying entry equal to world `+0xB8` receives the health addition in this preliminary scan. Null and nonqualifying entries are advanced past. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20C555–0x20C66C`.]

For that designated entry, maximum-health helper `0x044DB0` is called and its x87 result is stored to binary32. The elapsed signed integer is converted to binary32, multiplied first by binary32 `0.0010000000474974513` (bits `0x3A83126F`), then by binary32 `0.05000000074505806` (bits `0x3D4CCCCD`). The rounded maximum is multiplied by that rounded elapsed factor, added to current health, and stored to binary32 health. A second maximum-health query is narrowed to binary32 for comparison; ordered health greater than that value causes a third query whose x87 result is stored directly to binary32 health. This is before later input processing and the main actor health admission. The helper’s internal stat arithmetic is not specified here. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20C5C0–0x20C63F`; original binary constants at RVAs `0x2FD870` and `0x2FCDB4`.]

The world then adds elapsed to the 32-bit counter at `+0x8000BC`. It adds elapsed times `100` to `+0x80015C` when the preliminary flag is set, otherwise elapsed times `10`. These integer operations retain their low 32 bits. If world `+0xA4` is zero, the latter field is overwritten with `32,400,000` (`0x01EE6280`). While its signed value is strictly greater than `86,400,000` (`0x05265C00`), the routine increments world `+0x800160`, subtracts `86,400,000`, and calls `0x1F0C80` with the same aggregate. Equality does not enter this wrap loop. No corresponding negative wrap is present in this clock block. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20C672–0x20C6E2`.]

Clock publication is also near the beginning of the invocation. Using the already incremented counter at `+0x8000BC`, the routine compares its signed quotient by `10,000`, truncated toward zero, with the corresponding quotient after adding elapsed once more with 32-bit arithmetic. A difference, nonzero world `+0xB0` as tested through `0x0497A0` on world `+0xAC`, and nonzero world `+0xA4` admit publication. The constructed eight-byte payload contains the four bytes at `+0x800160` first, then the four bytes at `+0x80015C`; it is supplied under the original string `time` to helper `0x0499C0` on world `+0xAC`. This establishes the local call and payload order, not remote delivery or a transport schedule. The comparison is not described as an old-counter/new-counter comparison: the first counter addition has already happened. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20C6E4–0x20C838`; `decompiled/client/functions/004497a0.c`, `004499c0.c`; original binary string at RVA `0x315B40`.]

### Preparation before queued inputs

Next, elapsed is converted once to a saved binary32 value, and multiplied by the same binary32 `0.0010000000474974513` to form another saved time factor. Invocation-local containers are constructed. A separate creature-collection traversal follows at `0x20C8E1–0x20CD05`, before either optional input list. It performs conditional item, state, relationship and spatial preparation, can call `0x1D9AD0` with the aggregate, and can append an aggregate `+0x60` record. This traversal is distinct from the later main actor traversal and has its own null skips and selector gates. Its large item/spatial dependencies remain bounded; it is not characterized as a universal actor update or a universal dead-entry deletion pass. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20C83D–0x20CD1F`, especially `0x20CBB7` and `0x20CCDD–0x20CCE0`.]

### Optional queued hit inputs, then optional buff inputs

The third argument is tested for null at `0x20CD26`. A null pointer skips both optional input traversals. This is the value passed by both enclosing client call sites; the earlier backend copies enter the mutable aggregate, not this optional input argument.

For nonnull optional input, its list at `+0x00` is considered first. If nonempty and world byte `+0xB4` is nonzero, aggregate `+0x00` is cleared before processing the input records. The input list is then traversed in its native forward order, with each record supplied to hit receiver `0x195A60` together with the aggregate and an invocation-local collection. The outer traversal advances its iterator without erasing the optional input nodes. Hit-receiver admission, payload mutation, target effects and additional records are not re-audited here. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20CD2E–0x20CDD0`; `decompiled/client/functions/00477230.c`, `0046f870.c`, `00468d30.c`.]

Optional input `+0x58` is considered second. Its forty-byte records carry an eight-byte source at record `+0x00`, eight-byte target at `+0x08`, and a resident record beginning at `+0x10`. The outer loop looks up the target and, when present, applies the resident record through creature helper `0x046AF0`. A missing target skips local application. This outer loop does not test the embedded source or an elapsed argument before that call. It advances without erasing input nodes. After the nonempty buff-list traversal, world byte `+0xB4` nonzero causes aggregate `+0x58` to be cleared. Thus the hit clear precedes its local processing, whereas the buff clear follows local processing. Empty input lists do not trigger their associated clear; null optional input triggers neither. These observations do not establish safe aliasing between optional input and aggregate pointers. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20CDD2–0x20CEA5`; `decompiled/client/functions/00451c70.c` for the forty-byte event copy, `00446af0.c` for local resident application.]

### Intervening world maintenance and main actor admission

Before the main actor traversal, the routine processes a locally prepared collection at `0x20CEAA–0x20DDE5`, including a path calling generic-hit helper `0x196D30` at `0x20DD18`. It then branches on world byte `+0xB4`: the nonzero branch scans creature entries and can copy a type-5 entry’s identity into its resolved linked creature at `+0x11C8`; the zero branch performs additional local-collection and relationship work. After these branches join at `0x20F942`, a further maintenance region is entered only with world `+0xB4` zero and world `+0x94` nonempty. These regions precede main actor admission; their complete spawning, item, spatial and relationship semantics are unresolved here. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x20CEAA–0x210499`, especially `0x20DDEB–0x20DEB6` and `0x20F942–0x20F962`.]

The main creature traversal begins at `0x210499`. It uses the world `+0x04` collection’s native iterator order. It does not first build a complete immutable actor snapshot. A null creature pointer jumps directly to iterator advance at `0x21FE21`. Each nonnull creature first traverses its own record list at `+0x130C`, dispatching on the byte at each record `+0x128`; values one through eight have switch destinations. After that traversal, creature `+0x130C` is cleared when world byte `+0xB4` is zero and retained by this clear site otherwise. The full dispatch bodies and their mutations are not closed. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x210499–0x210546`, `0x211A44–0x211A91`, `0x21FE21–0x21FE5B`.]

Maximum-health admission follows the per-creature record work. One call to `0x044DB0` is narrowed from x87 to a binary32 temporary. Current binary32 health is compared with it. Only ordered greater health triggers another maximum query and an x87-to-binary32 store to health. Equal, lower or unordered comparison skips that store. A separate comparison of binary32 zero with current health then skips the remaining actor body for ordered health at most zero. Positive or unordered health falls through, assuming floating-point execution completes. Consequently, record dispatch, optional record clearing and maximum-health querying can precede a dead actor’s skip. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x211A96–0x211ADB`.]

### Admitted actor body and common tail

For admitted creatures with byte `+0x60` nonzero and world byte `+0xB4` zero, elapsed is added to signed 32-bit creature `+0x13C4`. A positive creature `+0x12C` bypasses the callback threshold path. Otherwise an accumulated value at least `32` allows the callback pointer at `+0x13E4` to be called when nonnull, with creature, world, accumulated elapsed and aggregate; the accumulator is then reset to zero even when that pointer was null. This is a thresholded per-invocation opportunity, not proof of exactly one callback per frame or per fixed duration. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x211AE1–0x211B7B`.]

Subsequent conditional steering/relationship work precedes timer updates and the action-byte dispatch. The integer fields at `+0x130`, `+0x134` and `+0x138` are reduced using elapsed before action selection; the latter two are clamped to zero when the 32-bit subtraction result is negative. Action selection reads byte `+0x68` at `0x211E04` and dispatches values one through `0x6E`, with other values taking the common continuation. The large selected bodies are not reconstructed here. The continuation includes further timer/list work, traversal of resident records at creature `+0x1178`, deferred removal of selected resident entries, later action/timer state and movement/pose-related work before the actor iterator advances. The resident traversal subtracts elapsed from each record’s signed 32-bit remaining-time field at record `+0x08` before its positive-time decision. This contract closes that ordering boundary without specifying all action bodies, effects, movement arithmetic or pose dependencies. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x211B7B–0x211E1B`, `0x211F41–0x212163`, `0x218310–0x218541`, `0x21FDD0–0x21FE5B`.]

### Other object collection, projectiles and final publication

After the main actor loop finishes, the routine traverses world `+0x0C`. The first loop at `0x21FEA0` only advances its iterator; a second traversal at `0x21FF02–0x220A6A` performs object work. The exact object category and complete arithmetic are not assigned a name here. This phase precedes iteration of the separate collection at world `+0x14`. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x21FE61–0x220A70`.]

The world `+0x14` projectile-record iteration starts at `0x220A70`. For its elapsed-dependent step, a velocity triplet beginning at record `+0x3C` is supplied to helper `0x051510` with the saved binary32 time factor, and signed 32-bit age at `+0x68` is incremented by elapsed. Subsequent decisions depend on kind at `+0x60` and the updated age, including kind two or age at most `5,000` entering the collision body at `0x221465`. That body can call generic-hit helper `0x196D30` and append effects and buff events to the same aggregate. Selected projectile iterators are recorded locally for removal; their nodes are removed from world `+0x14` after the traversal, at `0x220DB9–0x220E42`. Detailed targeting, movement, collision and retirement decisions remain outside this boundary contract. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x220A70–0x220E4C`, `0x221465–0x222B92`; `decompiled/client/functions/00477c60.c`; generic-hit call `0x222AB8`, aggregate event/effect append sites `0x222A0E` and `0x222A4D`.]

Another creature-collection relationship pass at `0x220E51–0x221136` follows projectile processing. Final publication then has four ordered parts. First, each entry in an invocation-local creature collection has byte `+0x75` set to one, is supplied to snapshot helper `0x0DC820`, and produces an appended aggregate `+0x28` record. Second, all currently present aggregate `+0x30` coordinate-pair records are traversed; successful world lookup through `0x034D10` has byte `+0x76` set to one. Third, a separate invocation-local object collection is converted through helper `0x208F60` and appended to aggregate `+0x38`. Fourth, all currently present aggregate `+0x18` records are traversed, with each signed coordinate divided by `256` using truncation toward zero; successful `0x034D10` lookup receives the record in its collection at `+0x68`. Invocation-local containers are then cleaned up before normal return. [Evidence: world assembly, `0x220E51–0x221462`, especially `0x22118D–0x2211A6`, `0x221230–0x221264`, `0x2212ED–0x221301`, `0x221384–0x2213E1`.]

## Repetition, mutation and elapsed limits

Each actual world call has its own preliminary scan, clock block, local-container lifetime, optional-input opportunity, main actor traversal, later object/projectile passes and final publication opportunity, subject to the per-phase branches above. Aggregate construction and backend queue copying belong to the enclosing caller and are not repeated between its subdivided world calls. The mutable aggregate is not a freshly empty result object at every world entry. Existing records can survive and new records can be appended; in particular, the final traversals of aggregate `+0x30` and `+0x18` start from the aggregate’s current beginnings, without a saved “new records only” boundary. Records retained from an earlier world invocation can therefore be visited again by those traversals. The optional-input clear sites described above are specific conditional mutations, not an unconditional whole-aggregate reset. Other large helper bodies are not certified free of aggregate mutations.

Zero elapsed is admitted by the observed caller once its independent comparisons succeed, and world entry has no positive-elapsed guard before the preliminary scan and clocks. Even with zero, that scan can make maximum-health calls and cap an already excessive qualifying health value; a zero world `+0xA4` still forces the clock field to `32,400,000`; preparation and applicable input/list work have no elapsed-positive admission at their documented boundaries. Zero therefore does not establish a mutation-free call. Negative elapsed is likewise passed unchanged by the admitted final-call path and reaches the initial world scan and integer clock additions; the visible signed subtraction/addition and binary32 conversion sites use that value. This is not a claim that all nested helpers implement meaningful backward simulation, nor proof that every data-dependent branch completes for arbitrary state.

The large action switches, behavior callback bodies, world-maintenance helpers, scalar/stat dependencies, collision internals and publication consumers remain unresolved. Their random draws, reentrant mutations, allocations, failures and exceptional exits are not enumerated. Native iterator order is preserved as an observable boundary; no universal cross-container sort order, duplicate suppression, frozen creature membership or whole-frame freshness guarantee is established. No all-frame protection scope is inferred from the separately confirmed backend queue locks. Floating-point rounding depends on the native SSE/x87 controls; this contract identifies the observed conversion and store boundaries without fixing untraced ambient control state. It supplies no server counterpart, transport delivery guarantee or equivalence between one combined elapsed call and several subdivided calls.
