# Alpha creature attachment and rendered-coordinate producers

This independent original-only contract covers creature fields `+0x1A04`, `+0x1B84`, `+0x1D18`, `+0x1D20`, `+0x1350` and `+0x137C`, their identified producers, and relevant read/refresh order in client renderer RVA `0x0AC260`. It closes the scalar conversions, the identified model-point transform, the reference copies and adjustments, and the ordinary rendered-coordinate refresh described below. The large preceding pose construction and the complete surrounding action and scheduling policies remain bounded dependencies. Field names here describe storage and arithmetic, without assigning a player, camera, hand or anatomical role.

The supported original image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, preferred base `0x00400000`. Addresses below are RVAs. Evidence paths are relative to the original `disassembled alpha cube world` directory; bare helper filenames in grouped citations are under `decompiled/client/functions/`. This contract uses original image instructions, constants and game decompiler exports only. No other project contract, reconstruction or runtime observation supplies evidence.

## Storage, owner and initialization

| Creature offset | Established representation and relationship |
|---|---|
| `+0x1350`, `+0x1358`, `+0x1360` | Three signed 64-bit fixed-point coordinates, sixteen fractional bits; called P here |
| `+0x1374`, `+0x1378`, `+0x137C` | Three binary32 angular values; the requested `+0x137C` is component 2 |
| `+0x1498` | Start of an embedded pose/render subobject, called C here; its first 32-bit word points to its owning creature |
| `+0x1A04`, `+0x1A08`, `+0x1A0C` | Binary32 attachment triplet A; subobject C `+0x56C` |
| `+0x1B84`, `+0x1B88`, `+0x1B8C` | Binary32 attachment triplet B; subobject C `+0x6EC` |
| `+0x1D18`, `+0x1D20` | Two signed 64-bit horizontal reference values; subobject C `+0x880` and `+0x888` |

The creature constructor at RVA `0x03B690` explicitly writes all six 32-bit words of P as zero, and all three angular words at `+0x1374` as positive binary32 zero. It constructs C through RVA `0x011E50`, supplying the creature pointer. That subobject constructor stores the owner pointer and explicitly zeroes all four 32-bit words of the two horizontal references. These are actual stores, not an assumption about allocation. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0043b690.c` and `00411e50.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x03B836–0x03B886` and `0x03BB42–0x03BB4D`.]

The inspected constructor bodies do not establish initial values for attachment triplets A and B. The many explicit zero stores elsewhere in C do not cover C `+0x56C` or `+0x6EC`. Therefore these attachments have no zero-default guarantee from this evidence. This does not claim that every allocation path leaves arbitrary bytes; allocation-wide initialization and other construction paths are unclosed. C's byte `+0x878`, creature `+0x1D10`, is initialized to zero, but the refresh routine sets it on entry before either attachment is conditionally produced. It cannot by itself certify that a particular attachment was refreshed. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00411e50.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA `0x01293B`; model gates at RVAs `0x021438–0x02143F` and `0x021B23–0x021B2A`.]

## Refresh entry and copied references

Renderer calls to RVA `0x0128F0` pass the creature's C as the implicit object. The refresh reads its owner through C's first word. On entry it copies the full signed 64-bit value addressed by its seventh stack argument into C `+0x880`, then the full value addressed by its eighth stack argument into C `+0x888`. Each copy is low 32-bit word followed by high 32-bit word. These are snapshots of supplied values; they are not recomputed from P, an attachment, a chunk index or the owner identifier. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x012910–0x012968`; renderer call setup at `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, RVAs `0x0B5D5A–0x0B5DB2`.]

At the ordinary renderer call at RVA `0x0B5DB2`, those two pointer arguments address client `+0x1D8` and client `+0x1E0`, in X then Y order. Thus creature `+0x1D18/+0x1D20` retain the horizontal reference values supplied to that particular pose refresh. They are separate storage from the client's current references and may be read later without being refreshed again. Other calls can supply different pointers. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, RVAs `0x0B5D73–0x0B5DB2`; `binary/Cube.exe`, entry copies at RVAs `0x012938–0x012968`.]

The routine obtains model pointers for owner records at `+0xAA8` and `+0x990` through helper RVA `0x0EC400` and the supplied model collection. It stores the results in C `+0xDC` and `+0xE0`, respectively, which are creature `+0x1574` and `+0x1578`. A supplied mask with bit `0x40` clears both of these pointers before their later use. The lookup's complete resource-selection, construction, lifetime and cache policy remains outside this contract. The established relationship is a pointer used by this creature's embedded subobject, rather than independently owned model data proven exclusive to that creature. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x0129E4–0x012A08` and `0x01F12F–0x01F145`; original lookup lead `decompiled/client/functions/004ec400.c`.]

## Conditional model-point production

The pose routine has an identified initial position-matrix input before the attachment matrices are built. After resetting a local matrix to identity, it forms horizontal translation coordinates from the native 64-bit sums P.X plus supplied reference X, and P.Y plus supplied reference Y. For Z it first subtracts a fixed-point conversion of owner binary32 `+0x1180` from P.Z, then adds a separately converted binary32 component at C `+0xB4`. Each of those two float-to-fixed conversions multiplies by binary32 `65536` before truncating to signed 64-bit. Each final integer translation coordinate is separately narrowed to binary32 and multiplied by `2^-16`, then supplied to the translation helper. The subsequent angular calls include owner `+0x137C`, `+0x1378`, `+0x1374`, in that order, with additional pose translations and rotations interleaved. This establishes direct P/reference/angle dependencies; it does not identify either final attachment matrix with this initial matrix or close the intervening pose construction. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x01F154–0x01F220` and `0x01F439–0x01F55A`; `binary/Cube.exe`, scalar conversion/subtraction/addition helpers RVAs `0x012220–0x012255`, `0x0122C0–0x012311`; helper `004120f0.c`.]

When C `+0xDC` is nonnull and execution reaches its model block, the routine uses the binary32 4-by-4 matrix at C `+0x2AC`, creature `+0x1744`, to produce A. When C `+0xE0` is nonnull and execution reaches the corresponding block, it uses the matrix at C `+0x2EC`, creature `+0x1784`, to produce B. The A block precedes the B block. A null pointer skips that block's initial attachment write; it does not store a replacement zero attachment. The preceding matrix-building branches depend on pose state, owner state and model information and are not fully closed here. The matrices at the transform calls are explicit bounded inputs. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x021438–0x02145E`, `0x021AA5–0x021B2A` and `0x0223BF–0x02243D`; transform helper `decompiled/client/functions/004248a0.c`.]

For each of these two models, the point supplied to the attachment transform has X equal to one half of the signed 32-bit word at model `+0x44`, Y equal to one half of the signed 32-bit word at model `+0x48`, and Z equal to the full signed 32-bit word at model `+0x4C`. Each integer is separately converted to binary32. X and Y are then separately multiplied by binary32 `0.5`, bits `0x3F000000`; Z has no half multiplier. Native read/call order for this point is Z, Y, X. There is no nonnegative clamp or checked dimension range in these calls. This states the scalar-field relation without assigning a semantic origin or unit to the model fields. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x021AA5–0x021AFE` and `0x0223BF–0x022418`; scalar getters `decompiled/client/functions/00424810.c`, `00424820.c`, `0047b5f0.c`; image constant at RVA `0x345DA0`.]

The transform at RVA `0x0248A0` uses float element index row plus four times column. It forms a homogeneous denominator from row 3: the rounded X product plus the rounded Y product, then the rounded Z product, then the fourth-column constant. It computes binary32 one divided by that denominator. For the X numerator, the first sum has Y's product as first operand and X's product as second; it then adds the Z product and constant. For Y and Z numerators it adds X's product, Y's product, Z's product and constant in that order. All these products, additions and the reciprocal are scalar binary32. Each output is the reciprocal multiplied by its corresponding rounded numerator, followed by a binary32 store, in X, Y, Z order. There is no zero-denominator or finite-value guard, no fused operation, and no normalizing-length calculation. The returned triplet is copied into the attachment field with three 32-bit word copies. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x0248A0–0x024984`; attachment copies at RVAs `0x021B18–0x021B1E` and `0x022432–0x022438`; `decompiled/client/functions/00411e30.c`.]

Those initial model-point writes are not the final complete attachment contract. Later in the same refresh, the routine constructs a binary32 adjustment from P and the copied references. Its X component comes from the native 64-bit negation of P.X, followed by 64-bit subtraction of C `+0x880`; its Y component follows the same sequence with P.Y and C `+0x888`. Each resulting signed 64-bit integer is narrowed to binary32 and multiplied in binary32 by exactly `2^-16`, bits `0x37800000`. The Z adjustment is explicitly zero. It adds this adjustment component by component to B, then adds it to A, with other related triplets interleaved between those two calls. Each addition is binary32 with the adjustment as the first operand. This sequence runs after the model blocks' null checks; the initial model-point write and this later adjustment must not be conflated. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x0225FD–0x0226F1`; scalar negation/subtraction helpers `00412260.c`, `00412200.c`, `004120f0.c`; vector addition `00412850.c`.]

Further branches can modify these series after the adjustment. A concrete replacement of A's first triplet occurs for C's 32-bit selector `+0x8` equal to `0x32`, within a bounded owner-timer window: the routine transforms binary32 point `(20, 0, -12)` through its then-current local matrix and overwrites A. Selector `0x45` has a different bounded timer branch that generates a sequence beginning at A using trigonometric points and its local matrix, replacing the first triplet as well. These are additional producers, not model-size transforms. The complete timer helpers, pose matrices and other action/history branches at RVAs `0x0226F6–0x022FE1` are not closed here. Consequently this contract does not claim that every final A or B equals the initial model point plus the stated adjustment, or that a missing model prevents later writes. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x0226F6–0x0229EE` and `0x022D18–0x022E3D`.]

## Rendered fixed triplet and angular refresh

The client world routine RVA `0x20C510` contains a common refresh region for P and the angular triplet. On its direct-copy branch, it copies the current owner's 24-byte triplet at `+0x10` into P, then copies the 12-byte triplet at `+0x28` into `+0x1374`. Thus `+0x137C` receives component 2 of that source. These are bit-preserving field copies, not float conversions of P. The direct branch is selected when the signed 32-bit field at `+0x1398` is nonpositive, after a preceding linked-object case has been excluded or failed. The linked case can instead copy another creature's P and angular triplet, with a constructed vertical offset on P. Its identity lookup and full selection policy remain bounded. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x21F968–0x21FA64`, `0x21FB80–0x21FB87` and `0x21FD36–0x21FD4F`.]

When that `+0x1398` field is positive on the ordinary branch, the routine obtains a binary32 blend amount by starting at zero and repeatedly approaching binary32 one with rate bits `0x3C75C28F`, approximately `0.015`. The repeat count is the signed 32-bit first stack argument of world routine RVA `0x20C510`; no elapsed-seconds meaning is established here. Each repeat subtracts the current value from one in binary32, multiplies by the rate in binary32, then adds the rounded product to the current value in binary32. Nonpositive repeat counts leave the initial zero. The native helper unrolls four repeats at a time without replacing the recurrence by a power expression. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x20C549–0x20C54F` and `0x21FB8D–0x21FBBD`; `decompiled/client/functions/00411d40.c`, RVA `0x011D40`.]

The blend amount is first multiplied in binary32 by `65536`, bits `0x47800000`, and truncated to a signed 64-bit fixed-point factor through RVA `0x02C580` and runtime conversion RVA `0x28D946`. The routine subtracts current P from the supplied target triplet, multiplies each raw 64-bit difference by that fixed-point factor retaining the native low 64-bit product, signed-divides each product by `65536` with truncation toward zero, and adds the resulting triplet to P in X, Y, Z order. There is no overflow clamp. This is a fixed-point blend with a quantized factor, not three direct float blends. The ordinary target is the retained current-state position at owner `+0x10`; upstream state changes and alternate identity branches remain outside this region's closure. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x21FBC2–0x21FC04`, with target pointer established at RVA `0x21B43A–0x21B44B`; helpers `0042c580.c`, `0042c7a0.c`, `0042c6e0.c`, `0042c900.c`, `0042c980.c`; native scalar conversion at `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02C580–0x02C5A9`.]

Z then receives a second correction. The routine rereads the now-updated P.Z, subtracts it from target Z in 64-bit arithmetic, narrows that raw difference to binary32, multiplies by the original binary32 blend amount, and truncates the rounded product to signed 64-bit raw counts. It adds those counts to P.Z. No additional `65536` multiplier or divisor is used in this second correction. X and Y do not receive this extra step in the audited branch. Combining the two Z corrections into one blend would lose the intermediate P.Z, factor quantization and raw-count float conversion boundaries. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x21FC09–0x21FC4F`; `binary/Cube.exe`, scalar helper RVAs `0x068CF0–0x068D23`; addition helper `decompiled/client/functions/0042c9b0.c`.]

After updating P, the branch updates angular components 0, 1 and 2 sequentially through RVA `0x023F70`, using the same binary32 blend amount. The ordinary input pointers address the retained state angles beginning at owner `+0x28`; the final component is written back at `+0x137C`. This is not ordinary subtraction-and-linear-interpolation of angle values. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x21FC54–0x21FD1F`; angle-source pointer sites RVAs `0x2191C0–0x219204` and `0x21D7B7–0x21D7CC`; `decompiled/client/functions/00423f70.c`.]

For each supplied current angle and target angle, RVA `0x023F70` divides the angle by binary32 `180`, widens that rounded quotient to binary64, multiplies by binary64 pi, then narrows to binary32. It calls imported cosine then imported sine for the current converted angle, followed by cosine then sine for the target converted angle. Each call receives the converted binary32 angle widened to binary64; each result is immediately narrowed to binary32 and then widened again for the following difference arithmetic. It forms, in binary64, target sine times current cosine minus target cosine times current sine, then narrows that difference to binary32. Ordered values beyond positive or negative one are clamped to the corresponding endpoint; an unordered value is not changed by the clamp branches. Imported arcsine follows, with its result immediately narrowed to binary32. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x023F70–0x02404E`; constants at image RVAs `0x2FCE5C`, `0x2FCE08`, `0x345DC0` and `0x345E8C`.]

The helper widens the blend amount to binary64, multiplies by binary64 `180`, divides by binary64 pi, multiplies that result by the widened, already narrowed arcsine result, and narrows the product to binary32. It adds that rounded increment to the original current angle in binary32. There is no final modulo, angle-range wrap, quadrant selection or atan2 call in this helper. Pi is the binary64 value with bits `0x400921FB54442D18`; binary64 `180` has bits `0x4066800000000000`; binary32 `180` has bits `0x43340000`. Imported trigonometric algorithms and their exceptional-result policies remain callee boundaries. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02404E–0x024089`; constants at image RVAs `0x2FCE08`, `0x2FCE18` and `0x2FCE5C`.]

This common region is not an exclusive writer proof. Later world-routine branches can transform and replace P and add to the final angular component; one such visible sequence is at RVAs `0x220838–0x2208BE`. Linked-object copies also occur later at RVAs `0x220FBE–0x220FF3`. Other identified original routines contain direct P copies, including RVAs `0x06B63C–0x06B669`, `0x08088F–0x0808BC` and `0x082407–0x082434`; a separate construction/display path copies P and zeros `+0x137C` at RVAs `0x207107–0x20713A`. Their complete admission and scheduling policies are not closed here. These observations prevent treating the ordinary refresh as a universal final value for every creature. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, the cited ranges.]

## Renderer recovery and observable order

At RVA `0x0B0200`, the renderer reads A and converts it to a fixed-point triplet through RVA `0x02C460`. Each float component is independently multiplied in binary32 by `65536`, then converted through RVA `0x28D946`; for finite representable inputs the integer result truncates toward zero. The X, Y and Z conversions occur in that order. It then obtains the native 64-bit negations of creature reference Y and reference X, in that read order, constructs the fixed-point triplet negative reference X, negative reference Y, zero, and adds the converted A triplet. The addition occurs in 64-bit integer arithmetic after the float-to-fixed conversion. B has the corresponding separate sequence beginning at RVA `0x0B037D`. There is no added P term in either of these two recovery sequences. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, RVAs `0x0B01FE–0x0B0281` and `0x0B037B–0x0B03FE`; `binary/Cube.exe`, conversion helper RVA `0x02C460`; helpers `00412260.c`, `0043b610.c`, `0042c800.c`.]

These attachment reads are conditional on surrounding item/scalar gates. Those gates do not check C's corresponding model pointer, a per-triplet validity bit, a pose timestamp, or equality of the stored references to current client references. Their presence therefore does not prove an attachment was freshly produced or initialized. The coordinate-recovery arithmetic uses whichever attachment and copied reference words are present when read. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, RVAs `0x0B0184–0x0B020D` and `0x0B033E–0x0B038A`.]

Other source construction in the renderer reads P and the final angular component. For example, RVAs `0x0B000B–0x0B00A1` reset a local matrix, apply a rotation from `+0x137C`, transform binary32 direction `(0,1,0)`, convert that direction to fixed point and add it to P. The rotation helper first multiplies the angle by binary32 bits `0x3C8EFA35`, approximately pi divided by 180, then widens for imported cosine and sine and narrows their results before matrix arithmetic. Direction helper RVA `0x088E50` uses only the first three columns, with binary32 products and additions, and does not add matrix translation. Thus this path consumes the rendered P/angle values directly, independently of A and B. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, the cited range; `decompiled/client/functions/00424610.c` and `00488e50.c`.]

The identified A/B reads at RVAs `0x0B0200/0x0B037D` precede the renderer's visible calls to refresh RVA `0x0128F0` at RVAs `0x0B5CF3/0x0B5DB2`. Conditional additional refresh calls occur at RVAs `0x0BA8FE/0x0BA9A5`. Later geometry loops read series beginning at A and B, at RVAs `0x0B9DF0` and `0x0B9F82`, respectively; these loops use 12-byte triplet strides. A refresh can be skipped or invoked repeatedly, and different calls can use different matrix or reference arguments. This establishes local program order between these identified sites, not that an early light read necessarily uses a previous frame's pose or that every later read sees one common pose generation. Earlier indirect calls, separate rendering paths and wider update/render scheduling remain unclosed. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, the cited call/read sites and RVAs `0x0B5C00–0x0B5D04`, `0x0B5D32–0x0B5DB7`, `0x0BA871–0x0BA9BD`.]

The renderer's containing native critical section is entered at RVA `0x0AC37E` using client `+0x2E4`, through wrapper RVA `0x201CB0`, and is released on the normal path at RVA `0x0BBB31` through RVA `0x201E90`. The wrappers address the critical section at the supplied object's `+0x8000C0`. That proves the renderer's containing protection scope, not a lock acquired by the arithmetic helpers or a universal protection rule for all pose, construction and world-update writers. Individual reference and attachment stores are not a combined atomic publication. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly-functions/004ac260.asm`, the cited sites; `decompiled/client/functions/00601cb0.c`, `00601e90.c`.]

The closed copies, conversion helpers, model-point arithmetic and ordinary rendered-coordinate arithmetic contain no explicit game-owned random draw. No claim is made about RNG effects in the full pose/action computation, resource lookup/construction, linked identity helpers, constructor dependencies or unexamined callbacks. Native 64-bit overflow follows the observed low-word/high-word arithmetic without added saturation. Ambient x87 and MXCSR controls, denormal handling, enabled exceptions, nonfinite or out-of-range float-to-integer results, invalid pointers and imported math remain explicit limits. Initial attachment validity, complete pose matrices, every special action/history branch, exclusive writer ownership and whole-frame freshness are not closed by this bounded contract.
