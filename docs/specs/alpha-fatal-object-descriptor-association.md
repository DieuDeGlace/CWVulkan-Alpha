# Fatal selector-5 object and descriptor association

Scope: the selector-5 spatial record chosen by the client fatal path at RVA 0x196193–0x196219, the 32-bit association value that record holds at +0x178, and the separate descriptor-pointer collection indexed by the creature's retained descriptor ordinal. Supported image: `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, image base 0x00400000. All addresses are RVAs; evidence paths are relative to the original Alpha evidence root. Evidence is the original image, its listing `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` (listing bytes for every cited instruction range compared with the image file and matched), the image's symbol and import data, and individually named original function exports. No other contract, reconstruction or replacement implementation was used.

What is closed: the consumer's selection rule, the record defaults, whole-record copying, prefix-only replacement, descriptor publication and several invalidation boundaries. What is **not closed**: any concrete producer that gives a newly generated selector-5 record a nonnegative descriptor ordinal at +0x178. The default and copy writers are not evidence of such a producer. Nothing here implies that the fatal selector-5 branch is unreachable.

## Two collections and the value that joins them

Notation: C is the creature already resolved by the hit applier; Z is the spatial object located from C+0x1B0/+0x1B4; O is a record in Z's contiguous record collection, whose begin and end pointers are Z+0x0C and Z+0x10 and whose records are 0x188 bytes apart.

Selection walks that collection from the beginning in order, counting a record ordinal as it goes. It accepts the first record whose 32-bit word at O+0x00 equals 5, whose 32-bit word at O+0x178 is bit-for-bit equal to C+0x1B8, and whose byte at O+0x30 is nonzero. No record or creature position takes part in the choice. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` and `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA 0x196193–0x1961DA.]

The counted record ordinal locates O inside the contiguous collection and is unrelated to the descriptor ordinal held in O+0x178 and C+0x1B8. Once O is accepted, the object-state helper RVA 0x1A58E0 is applied to O with requested state zero. An output entry is then assembled from C's two retained spatial coordinates, the counted record ordinal and a prefix copy of O, and appended to an output collection. O+0x178 is never used in place of the record ordinal. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1961DA–0x196219.]

The descriptor collection is independent: it begins at Z+0x18 with its end at Z+0x1C, and each element is a 32-bit pointer to a separately allocated descriptor D. The later fatal backing-record access re-derives Z from C+0x1B0/+0x1B4 with the same range and table arithmetic as the spatial lookup, then treats C+0x1B8 as a signed 32-bit ordinal that must be nonnegative and below the pointer count. It then writes the constant 0x00124F80 (1,200,000) to D+0x38 and copies a world 32-bit word at world+0x800160 into D+0x3C. The contiguous record ordinal plays no part, and D is not tested for null after the bounds check. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x196B65–0x196C49.]

So the actual link is integer equality between O+0x178 and C+0x1B8. A record's position in the contiguous collection does not itself identify a descriptor. The selection does not reject a negative association merely for being negative; equality is all it tests. The later descriptor access applies its own nonnegative test. These are separate gates. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1961B9–0x1961CB and 0x196C13–0x196C28.]

## Record construction, copying and retained metadata

Full-record initializer RVA 0x18EAA0 sets byte O+0x30 to 1; the 32-bit words O+0x34, O+0x38, O+0x40 and O+0x44 to zero; initialises the nested region at O+0x48 through RVA 0x03C020; then stores 0xFFFFFFFF separately to O+0x178, O+0x17C and O+0x180 and zero to O+0x184. It does not set the selector, a position or a descriptor ordinal, and bytes it does not write cannot be assumed zero. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x18EAA0–0x18EAF6; `decompiled/client/functions/0058eaa0.c`, `0043c020.c`.]

That default only means the association holds the all-ones pattern at this point. It is not a valid descriptor, and a copied default must never be read as ordinal zero. O+0x17C and O+0x180 also start at −1, but no producer assigning spatial coordinates to those two words has been found, so equal defaults do not justify calling them cached coordinates. The cached spatial coordinates that are established below belong to the creature and to Z, not to these record words.

Whole-record copy RVA 0x18EA10 first copies the common prefix through RVA 0x068670, then copies the nested state at +0x48 and the word at +0x54 and the nested state at +0x58 through separate helpers, then +0x170 and +0x174, and finally the four 32-bit words +0x178, +0x17C, +0x180 and +0x184. Whatever association the source holds, positive, negative or otherwise, is carried over unchanged. No spatial lookup, descriptor bounds check or recalculation occurs. This is a preservation writer, not the missing producer of a valid association. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x18EA10–0x18EA92; `decompiled/client/functions/0058ea10.c`.]

Prefix copier RVA 0x068670 writes the 32-bit word +0x00, the six 32-bit words +0x08 through +0x1C (three 64-bit fixed-point coordinates, as read by the state helper below), the four words +0x20 through +0x2C, the byte +0x30, the words +0x34 and +0x38, and the words +0x40 and +0x44. It does not write +0x04, +0x3C, anything from +0x48 on, or the padding bytes +0x31–+0x33. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x068670–0x0686D9; `decompiled/client/functions/00468670.c`.]

In the client routine containing RVA 0x08F481–0x08F4F6, a queued object-state update carrying a two-coordinate spatial identifier and a signed record ordinal is applied as follows: Z is resolved, a negative ordinal is rejected, the ordinal is checked against the contiguous record count (span divided by 0x188), the supplied prefix is copied over that record through RVA 0x068670, and the byte at Z+0x74 is cleared. This can replace the selector, position and state of an existing record while keeping its old +0x178 association. It neither requires the new selector to be 5 nor rebuilds any descriptor link from the new position. How that queued update is produced and admitted upstream is outside scope. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x08F481–0x08F4F6; helpers RVA 0x04B560 and 0x04E890.]

State helper RVA 0x1A58E0 is narrower than a reset. If O+0x30 already equals the requested state, it does nothing. Otherwise it replaces O+0x34 with 1000 minus its old value, clamped below at zero, stores the requested state in O+0x30, and may append an effect positioned from O's three 64-bit fixed-point coordinates at +0x08/+0x10/+0x18. The effect selector is 0x33 for record selector 5 (unconditionally), 0x35 for selector 6 and 0x34 for selector 7 (each only when the requested state is nonzero), and 0x36 for every other selector. O+0x178 is not touched, so clearing the state byte leaves the descriptor association in place. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1A58E0–0x1A5A03, and the call at 0x1961DA–0x1961E9; `decompiled/client/functions/005a58e0.c`.]

## Record and descriptor publication

Record append RVA 0x1A0E00 works on a collection of 0x188-byte records. When storage is full it first grows the collection; it then constructs a whole-record copy at the old end through RVA 0x18EA10 and advances the end by 0x188. When the source record lies inside the same collection, the routine converts it to an ordinal before growing and re-locates it afterwards. The source association is preserved; no new link is derived from the destination position. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1A0E00–0x1A0EA0; `decompiled/client/functions/005a0e00.c`.]

Growth check RVA 0x194B40 tail-jumps to reallocation RVA 0x1949D0, which obtains new storage, copies the existing records in order through RVA 0x18E8E0 (itself using the whole-record copy), destroys and frees the old sequence, and installs new begin, end and capacity pointers. On success values and order, including +0x178, survive; raw pointers into the old storage do not. Behaviour on allocation failure or exceptions is not promised. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x194B40–0x194BDF, 0x1949D0–0x194AB4, 0x18E8E0–0x18E985; `decompiled/client/functions/00594b40.c`, `005949d0.c`, `0058e8e0.c`, `0058ea10.c`.]

Spatial producer RVA 0x1E4850 appends records to the new spatial object's +0x0C collection through RVA 0x1A0E00; examples are the calls at 0x1E9E15, 0x1EA9B6 and 0x1EBEFB. These tie the initializer, copy and append routines to the same collection that fatal selection reads. The temporary records built at these sites (for example, the one at 0x1E9BC9 starts from the full-record initializer) carry their own selector, fixed-point position, orientation and scale values. They do not show that every generated selector or association has been traced, and no valid selector-5 ordinal assignment is inferred from them. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1E9BC9–0x1E9E15, 0x1EA88B–0x1EA9B6, 0x1EBDFC–0x1EBEFB.]

Descriptors are a different kind of object. Constructor RVA 0x1ADB20 stores the virtual table at VA 0x0071DBEC, which the original symbol data names as the `cube::Spawn` virtual table, into an object allocated with 0x10F0 bytes, and sets initial fields including D+0x28 = 1. The generator at RVA 0x1AF170 fills the descriptor and appends its pointer to Z+0x18 through RVA 0x26ADD0; one concrete instance is RVA 0x1BA3ED–0x1BA50C, reached from the spatial producer's call at RVA 0x1E9672 with Z as its first argument. D+0x28 is the descriptor's own field and is not O's selector at +0x00. The value 5 written to D+0x28 on that path does not create a selector-5 spatial record or an O+0x178 association. Pointer origin, allocation size, destination collection and element stride keep the two kinds apart. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1ADB20–0x1ADB44, 0x1BA3ED–0x1BA50C, 0x1E9655–0x1E9677; `decompiled/client/symbols.csv` entry for VA 0x0071DBEC; `decompiled/client/functions/005adb20.c`, `0066add0.c`.]

Pointer append RVA 0x26ADD0 adds one 32-bit pointer and advances the end by four bytes, growing storage when needed while preserving the pointer sequence. It never clones descriptors and never alters any record's +0x178. The nested descriptor generation (appearance, equipment, behaviour, random choices and all descriptor classes) is not part of the closed association producer. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x26ADD0–0x26AE32; `decompiled/client/functions/0066add0.c`.]

## Coordinate and creature-ordinal sources

Spatial lookup RVA 0x034D10 accepts two signed coordinates each in 0 through 65535 inclusive. Their quotients by 64 select a region through RVA 0x034A90 (region table at world+0xBC, 1024 by 1024, the first coordinate as the more significant index); their remainders modulo 64 select a spatial pointer in that region's table at +0x10018, again with the first coordinate more significant. A missing region or pointer makes the lookup return null. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x034A90–0x034AC3, 0x034D10–0x034D85; `decompiled/client/functions/00434d10.c`, `00434a90.c`.]

Spatial producer RVA 0x1E4850 admits the same coordinate range, returns early when the lookup already finds an object, otherwise allocates and constructs a new 0xC8-byte object Z, stores the input coordinates as 32-bit words at Z+0x60 and Z+0x64, and later publishes Z into the region slot chosen by the input remainders. These two fields are therefore Z's retained spatial identifiers, independent of the same offsets in other object types. The producer seeds the C runtime random generator from a value formed from the two coordinates and a world word at world+0x800188, and draws from it during generation. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1E4889–0x1E48BC, 0x1E4930–0x1E49D2, 0x1E6B63–0x1E6B96, 0x1EE04E–0x1EE05A; import slots for `srand` and `rand`; `decompiled/client/functions/006240f0.c`.]

The ordinary descriptor-driven actor creation in world routine RVA 0x20C510 walks Z's +0x18 pointer collection from ordinal zero. For a descriptor D that passes its gates (including a lookup confirming that no actor is already stored under D's identifier), D's eight-byte identifier at +0x48/+0x4C becomes both the new creature's own identifier and its map key. D's fixed-point triplet at +0x10 is copied to the creature's current position and, separately, to the triplet at C+0x1C0. The routine then builds the three words Z+0x60, Z+0x64 and the descriptor ordinal and copies them into C+0x1B0, C+0x1B4 and C+0x1B8. This is a concrete producer of the creature side of the link used by fatal selection. It does not write O+0x178 and does not show that a matching selector-5 record exists. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x20FB48–0x20FC02, 0x20FFB4–0x210020, 0x21009C–0x2100FA, 0x210433–0x210453; RVA 0x011E30 and 0x02C500.]

The creature's C+0x1B0/+0x1B4 coordinates are thus cached descriptor provenance, separate from its changeable current position. The creature constructor and the inspected creature reset initialise them as −1, −1 and 0 (through a sub-object at creature+0x10 whose +0x1A0/+0x1A4/+0x1A8 fields they are), and the creature state copy at RVA 0x04B040 can later overwrite all three without recreating provenance. The creature's own identifier, its map key, the contiguous record ordinal and the descriptor ordinal are four separate values. Own identifier and key agree on the ordinary creation route cited above; the object association is an independent integer field. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x03B6CB–0x03B6D1, 0x046338–0x04633D, 0x03C2B5–0x03C2C5, 0x046701–0x046715, 0x04B2CA–0x04B2E8; `decompiled/client/functions/0043b690.c`, `0043c100.c`, `00446330.c`, `004465c0.c`, `0044b040.c`.]

## Replacement boundaries and unresolved generation

Regional refresh RVA 0x1D8750 reaches the same region table and Z+0x18 collection. Starting at the signed index stored in Z+0xA0 and continuing while the index is below the current pointer count, it destroys each non-null descriptor through its virtual destructor. It then resizes the pointer collection to the Z+0xA0 value through RVA 0x00D9D0. That resize truncates when the value is below the count, does nothing when equal, and, because it compares unsigned, would extend the collection with null pointers when the value is larger. For a boundary within the current count this keeps the prefix's ordinals and removes the tail's descriptors. The destroy-and-resize sequence does not update any surviving record's +0x178. Later appends can occupy ordinals whose former descriptors were destroyed; no generation counter or stable descriptor identity follows from an ordinal alone. The full surrounding refresh and subsequent record regeneration remain open. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1D89C3–0x1D8A0A, 0x00D9D0–0x00DA5F; `decompiled/client/functions/005d8750.c`, `0040d9d0.c`.]

In short: successful record growth keeps values and order; prefix-only updates keep the association; descriptor tail replacement can change what a retained ordinal refers to. Destruction, unloading, concurrent replacement and callbacks of spatial objects need separate lifetime proof. Neither the fatal consumer nor these copy routines guarantee that every selector-5 record has a live, correct descriptor association.

No random number is drawn by the association default, the final +0x178 copy, the prefix copier or the creature's three-word provenance copy. The spatial and descriptor generators around them do seed and draw random numbers and invoke nested scalar, terrain, item and behaviour code; their branch coverage and draw order are not recovered here. Allocation failure, imported runtime behaviour and publication synchronisation are bounded dependencies and give no grounds for inferring missing association values.

The remaining gap is specific: this contract does not identify the original instructions that first give a newly generated selector-5 record a valid descriptor ordinal in +0x178, nor whether such an assignment exists on every ordinary path. It assigns no coordinate meaning to O+0x17C/+0x180. The consumer link and the preservation writers may be relied on only with these limits. There is no server equivalence, inferred actor role, guessed positive association or assumed all-zero record initialisation.

## Re-certification (2026-11-14)

Re-checked by a source-side author against the original client image, its listing (bytes compared with the file for all cited ranges), its symbol data and import table, and the named exports. The Infinite-Cubes reconstruction was not consulted.

Confirmed:
- Selection rule (32-bit selector 5 at +0x00, 32-bit equality of +0x178 with C+0x1B8, nonzero byte +0x30, stride 0x188, counted ordinal) and output assembly.
- Descriptor collection at Z+0x18/+0x1C; signed nonnegative and upper-bound test on C+0x1B8; no null test of D.
- Initializer, whole-record copy and prefix copier field sets; −1 defaults at +0x178/+0x17C/+0x180 and zero at +0x184.
- Queued prefix update at 0x08F481–0x08F4F6.
- State helper's 1000-minus clamp and effect selector 0x33 for selector 5.
- Append, growth and reallocation behaviour; spatial producer append sites; Z+0x60/+0x64 coordinates and region-slot publication.
- Descriptor constructor vtable (`cube::Spawn`), 0x10F0 allocation, D+0x28 = 5 path, pointer append to Z+0x18 with Z confirmed as the generator's first argument.
- Spatial lookup ranges and index arithmetic; ordinary creation's identifier, position and three-word provenance copy; creature defaults −1, −1, 0.

Corrected:
- Regional refresh: the final call is a resize to Z+0xA0, not an unconditional shrink; a larger boundary would extend with null pointers (unsigned comparison), and the destroy loop uses a signed comparison.
- State helper: all of its writes (state, +0x34, effect) occur only when the requested state differs; effect selectors for selectors 6, 7 and others are now listed.
- Prefix copier: now states that +0x04, +0x3C and padding are not written.
- Whole-record copy: now records the separate copy of the word at +0x54.
- Z+0x74 clear is a byte write.
- Fatal descriptor access: now records the written values (0x124F80 to D+0x38, world+0x800160 to D+0x3C) and that Z is re-derived inline rather than through RVA 0x034D10.
- Prose rewritten in independent wording.

Unresolved:
- The original producer, if any, that assigns a valid descriptor ordinal to a newly generated selector-5 record's +0x178.
- Meaning of O+0x17C/+0x180; meaning of world+0x800160 and of the D+0x38/D+0x3C fields.
- Full regional refresh procedure, behaviour for negative or over-count Z+0xA0 values, spatial-object lifetime, allocation failure, concurrency, nested generator draw order, server counterpart.
