# Fatal selector-5 object and descriptor association

This independent contract covers the selector-5 spatial record selected at original client RVA 0x196193–0x196219, its 32-bit association at record+0x178, and the separate descriptor pointer collection addressed by the creature's retained ordinal. The supported original image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, image base 0x00400000. All routine addresses below are RVAs. Evidence paths are relative to `Documents/disassembled alpha cube world`.

The consumer identity, explicit record defaults, full-record copying, prefix-only state replacement, descriptor publication and several invalidation boundaries are closed below. A concrete producer that assigns a nonnegative descriptor ordinal to a newly generated selector-5 record's +0x178 is **not closed**. In particular, the default and copy writers must not be represented as proof of that missing assignment. No claim that the fatal selector-5 branch is universally unreachable follows from this remaining gap. Original image instructions and individually identified original exports supply the evidence; no other contract, reconstruction or replacement implementation was used.

## The two collections and their joining value

Let C be the creature already resolved by native hit application, Z the spatial object found using C+0x1B0/+0x1B4, and O a record in Z's contiguous record collection. Z+0x0C and Z+0x10 hold that collection's beginning and end. Its records are 0x188 bytes apart. Fatal selection scans from its beginning in record order, maintains a separate record ordinal, and admits the first record with 32-bit selector 5 at O+0x00, a 32-bit +0x178 value bitwise equal to C+0x1B8, and a nonzero byte at O+0x30. No record-position or creature-position comparison is part of this selection. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x196193–0x1961DA; supported `binary/Cube.exe`, the same range.]

The record ordinal maintained by this scan identifies O in the contiguous collection. It is different from the descriptor ordinal stored in O+0x178 and C+0x1B8. On admission, the object-state helper is called on O; the output construction then supplies the two retained spatial coordinates, the contiguous record ordinal, and a copy of O's common state prefix. It does not substitute O+0x178 for the output's record ordinal. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1961DA–0x196219.]

The independently indexed descriptor collection begins at Z+0x18, with its end at Z+0x1C. Each element is a 32-bit pointer to a separately allocated descriptor D. The later fatal backing-record access checks C+0x1B8 as a signed 32-bit ordinal: it must be nonnegative and less than the current pointer-collection length. It then selects D from that collection and writes D+0x38 and D+0x3C. This access does not use the contiguous record ordinal. It does not separately null-check D after the bounds check. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x196B65–0x196C49.]

Thus the actual joining condition is the stored integer equality between O+0x178 and the creature's retained descriptor ordinal. The existence of O at a particular contiguous-record position does not itself establish any descriptor identity. The fatal scan does not reject a negative association merely because it is negative: it uses equality. The later descriptor lookup has its own signed nonnegative test. These are distinct gates. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1961B9–0x1961CB and 0x196C13–0x196C28.]

## Record construction, copied state and retained metadata

The ordinary full-record initializer at RVA 0x18EAA0 explicitly sets O+0x30 to byte 1; O+0x34 and O+0x38 to 32-bit zero; and O+0x40/+0x44 to zero. It initializes the nested region beginning at O+0x48 through RVA 0x03C020. It then writes 0xFFFFFFFF to O+0x178, O+0x17C and O+0x180, and writes zero to O+0x184. The three −1 values are separate 32-bit stores. The initializer does not assign selector 5, a position or a descriptor ordinal. Unwritten bytes cannot be inferred to be zero from the allocation or these field defaults. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0058eaa0.c`, `0043c020.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x18EAA0–0x18EAF6.]

The association default therefore means only that the field receives the all-ones bit pattern at this boundary. It is not proof of a valid descriptor, and a copied default must not become ordinal zero by implication. Although O+0x17C/+0x180 also receive −1, this audit has not established a producer assigning spatial coordinates to those two object-record fields. Their equal defaults are insufficient to name them cached coordinates. The concrete cached spatial coordinates established below belong to the creature's association group and the spatial object, not to an assumed reinterpretation of these object-record words.

The complete-record copy at RVA 0x18EA10 first copies the common prefix through RVA 0x068670. It copies or constructs the nested state beginning at +0x48/+0x58 through separate helpers, copies +0x170/+0x174, then explicitly copies the four 32-bit words at +0x178, +0x17C, +0x180 and +0x184. A positive, negative or otherwise arbitrary association is preserved unchanged by that final copy. No spatial lookup, descriptor count check or ordinal recalculation occurs there. This is a preservation writer, not the missing original producer of a valid positive association. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0058ea10.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x18EA10–0x18EA92.]

The common prefix copier at RVA 0x068670 writes selector +0x00; the six 32-bit position words at +0x08 through +0x1C; the words at +0x20 through +0x2C; byte +0x30; words +0x34/+0x38; and words +0x40/+0x44. Its last written byte is below +0x48. It does not write +0x178 or the other retained metadata. It also does not establish an all-byte copy of prefix padding. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00468670.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x068670–0x0686D9.]

In the enclosing client-frame routine, a queued object-state update supplies a two-coordinate spatial identifier and a signed record ordinal. The routine resolves Z, rejects a negative ordinal, checks it against the contiguous-record count, and copies the supplied prefix to that current record through RVA 0x068670. It then clears Z+0x74. This route can replace the selector, position and state of an existing record while preserving its old +0x178 association. It does not require the replacement selector to be 5 or reconstruct a descriptor relationship from the replacement position. The upstream production and transport admission of that queued update are outside this contract. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x08F481–0x08F4F6.]

The state change used by the fatal branch is also narrower than a full-record reset. Helper RVA 0x1A58E0 changes O+0x30 only when it differs from the requested state; it replaces O+0x34 by 1000 minus its old value, clamped below at zero, and can append an effect using O's stored fixed-point position. For selector 5, the effect selector is 0x33. These writes do not alter O+0x178. Consequently clearing the object's state byte does not clear its stored descriptor association. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005a58e0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1961DA–0x1961E9 and routine RVA 0x1A58E0.]

## Ordinary record and descriptor publication

The record append boundary RVA 0x1A0E00 operates on a collection of 0x188-byte records. It constructs a complete copy at the old end through RVA 0x18EA10, then advances the end by 0x188. If its input record already lies in the same collection and growth is needed, it retains the input's record ordinal and re-finds its source after growth. That behavior preserves the source association; it does not create a new relationship based on the appended record's destination ordinal. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005a0e00.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, routine RVA 0x1A0E00.]

Growth checks at RVA 0x194B40 lead to the allocation boundary RVA 0x1949D0. The latter obtains new storage, copies the existing record sequence through RVA 0x18E8E0 and the full-record copier, destroys the old sequence and replaces the collection's begin/end/capacity pointers. Record values and order are preserved on the successful path, including +0x178; raw pointers into the old contiguous storage are not preserved. The allocation and exception boundaries are not a promise of successful publication under failure. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00594b40.c`, `005949d0.c`, `0058e8e0.c`, `0058ea10.c`.]

The spatial producer at RVA 0x1E4850 uses the full-record append on the spatial object's +0x0C collection; concrete calls include RVAs 0x1E9E15, 0x1EA9B6 and 0x1EBEFB. These independently establish the collection's native identity and link the constructor/copy helpers to the spatial records consumed by fatal selection. The inspected temporary records at these boundaries have their own selector, fixed-point position, orientation and scale construction. They are not evidence that every generated selector or every association has been traced. No assignment of a valid selector-5 descriptor ordinal is inferred from these ordinary append calls. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1E9BC9–0x1E9E15, 0x1EA88B–0x1EA9B6, 0x1EBDFC–0x1EBEFB.]

The descriptor pointer collection is a different native object. Descriptor construction at RVA 0x1ADB20 installs the original Spawn virtual table and initializes a separately allocated 0x10F0-byte object. The generator can fill its fields, then append its pointer to Z+0x18 through RVA 0x26ADD0. One concrete path does this at RVA 0x1BA502–0x1BA50C. The descriptor's own selector-like field at D+0x28 is not O's selector at +0x00; a value 5 written to D+0x28 on that path does not establish a selector-5 spatial record or an O+0x178 association. These identities are distinguished by pointer origin, allocation, destination collection and record stride. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005adb20.c`, `0066add0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1BA3ED–0x1BA50C; supported `binary/Cube.exe`, RVA 0x1ADB20–0x1ADB44.]

The pointer append at RVA 0x26ADD0 adds one 32-bit pointer and advances its end by four bytes. Its storage growth can replace the pointer array while retaining the descriptor pointers and sequence. It does not clone the descriptors and does not reassign any spatial record's +0x178. Nested descriptor generation, including appearance, equipment, behavior, random choices and all admitted descriptor classes, remains outside the closed association producer. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0066add0.c`; descriptor constructor and generator addresses above.]

## Concrete coordinate and creature-ordinal sources

The spatial lookup used by fatal selection, RVA 0x034D10, admits signed coordinates in the range 0 through 65535. Each coordinate's quotient by 64 selects a larger region; the two remainders modulo 64 select a spatial pointer from the region's +0x10018 table. The first coordinate is the more significant table component. Missing regions or spatial pointers fail the lookup. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00434d10.c`, `00434a90.c`.]

In the corresponding spatial producer RVA 0x1E4850, the admitted input coordinate pair is written into Z+0x60/+0x64 as two 32-bit words. The routine later publishes Z in the region table slot selected by the input remainders. This establishes those fields as retained spatial identifiers of this native object, independently of repeated offsets in other object types. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1E4889–0x1E48BC, 0x1E4930–0x1E49D2, 0x1E6B63–0x1E6B96, 0x1EE04E–0x1EE05A; `decompiled/client/functions/006240f0.c`.]

The ordinary descriptor-based actor creation in world RVA 0x20C510 iterates Z's +0x18 pointer collection from ordinal zero. A selected descriptor D supplies its eight-byte identifier at +0x48/+0x4C to both the creature constructor and the creature-map insertion key. Its fixed-point triplet at D+0x10 is copied separately to the creature's current position and the triplet at C+0x1C0. Later, the routine supplies Z+0x60, Z+0x64 and the descriptor ordinal to the three-word copy into C+0x1B0/+0x1B4/+0x1B8. This is a concrete producer of the creature-side association consumed by fatal selection. It does not assign O+0x178 and does not prove that a matching selector-5 record already exists. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x20FB48–0x20FC02, 0x20FFB4–0x210020, 0x21009C–0x2100FA, 0x210433–0x210453; `decompiled/client/functions/0042c500.c`, `00411e30.c`.]

The creature-side coordinates are therefore cached descriptor provenance, distinct from its mutable current position. At construction or the inspected creature reset they instead begin as −1, −1, 0; ordinary state copying can later replace them without recreating descriptor provenance. This audit does not treat the creature's own ID, its map entry key, the contiguous spatial-record ordinal, and the descriptor ordinal as one identity. Key/own-ID agreement is established for the cited ordinary actor creation route, while the object association is an independent integer field. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0043b690.c`, `0043c100.c`, `00446330.c`, `004465c0.c`, `0044b040.c`; original ordinary creation instructions above.]

## Replacement boundaries and unresolved generation

A regional refresh route at RVA 0x1D8750 independently accesses the same spatial pointer table and Z+0x18 descriptor collection. It destroys non-null descriptor pointers from the retained boundary index at Z+0xA0 through the current end, then shrinks the pointer collection to that boundary. For valid collection bounds this preserves the prefix's ordinal positions in that operation, but removes the tail's descriptors. The inspected destruction/shrink sequence does not update a surviving spatial record's +0x178. Later appends can therefore occupy ordinal positions whose previous descriptors have been removed; no generation counter or stable descriptor identity is proved by an ordinal alone. The complete surrounding refresh procedure and all subsequent record regeneration remain unclosed. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005d8750.c`, `0040d9d0.c`; `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA 0x1D89E0–0x1D8A0A.]

Successful record growth preserves values and sequence; prefix-only updates preserve the extended association; descriptor tail replacement can change what a retained ordinal denotes. Spatial object destruction, unloading, concurrent replacement and callback effects require their own lifetime proof. Neither the fatal consumer nor these copy helpers establish a whole-world invariant that every selector-5 record has a live, correct descriptor association.

No random draw is made by the explicit association default, the final +0x178 copy, the prefix copier or the creature's three-word provenance assignment. The surrounding spatial and descriptor generators do seed or consume randomness and invoke nested scalar, terrain, item and behavior dependencies. Their complete branch coverage and draw order are not recovered here. Allocation failure, imported conversion behavior and synchronization of publication are also bounded dependencies, rather than reasons to infer missing association values.

The remaining producer gap is specific: this contract does not identify the original instruction sequence that first gives a newly generated selector-5 O a valid descriptor ordinal in +0x178, nor prove whether that assignment exists on every ordinary path. It also does not establish coordinate meanings for O+0x17C/+0x180 from their defaults or copies. The verified consumer bridge and preservation writers are usable only with those limits retained. There is no server equivalence, inferred actor role, guessed positive association, or inferred all-zero record initialization.
