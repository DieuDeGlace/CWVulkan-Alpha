# Alpha world-hit target admission

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD (re-certified 2026-11-22).

## Image and scope

Supported original client: `binary/Cube.exe` in the relative evidence root `disassembled alpha cube world`, 3,885,568 bytes, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, preferred base `0x400000`. All addresses below are RVAs relative to that base. Evidence consists of that original image, its disassembly listing `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` and its symbol table `decompiled/client/symbols.csv` (used only to identify an imported runtime routine). Constants were read directly from the image. No reconstruction source, other contract, runtime observation or implementation material was used.

The audited world application starts at `0x195A60`. Its receiver is the world object. Its three explicit arguments are, in order, a pointer to a 72-byte hit record, a pointer to an output aggregate, and a further four-byte value that is held unchanged until fatal processing. The type and ownership of that third value remain unresolved. This contract covers admission and ordinary local effects up to the final health comparison at `0x196133–0x196143`, and identifies where fatal processing begins. It does not describe fatal rewards, removal, linked-actor cleanup or downstream transport.

Direct calls to this routine occur at eight sites in the client image: `0x19700F`, `0x197229`, `0x1974DD` and `0x1986D0` inside routine `0x196D30`; `0x20CD9B` and `0x221E14` inside routine `0x20C510`; and `0x214BAC` and `0x214F72`, which the listing does not attribute to a named routine start. The enclosing routes, their gates and their purpose are separate caller dependencies; no caller is given a role name here, and no server counterpart is inferred. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, those call instructions.

Field layout used below. Hit record: source identifier, eight bytes at `+0`; target identifier, eight bytes at `+8`; binary32 amount at `+0x10`; signed four-byte values at `+0x18` and `+0x1C` (called the first and second record timers); binary32 response components at `+0x38`, `+0x3C` and `+0x40`; category byte at `+0x45`. Creature: own eight-byte identifier at `+8`; signed 64-bit fixed-point position at `+0x10/+0x18/+0x20`; role byte `+0x60`; four-byte kind `+0x64`; action byte `+0x68`; 16-bit flag word `+0x7E`; binary32 health `+0x16C`. Further creature fields are introduced where used. Numeric roles, kinds and categories are kept as numbers; descriptive labels such as "timer", "resource" or "class" are reading aids, not proven meanings.

## Target lookup and delivery-only admission

The routine first looks up the record's target identifier in the world's creature table at world `+4`, using helper `0x077D90`. It returns immediately, before any other work, when the lookup reaches the table's end marker, when the matching entry stores a null creature pointer, or when target health compared with zero is less than or equal to zero. Positive and negative zero health are both rejected. A NaN health value is not rejected by this comparison and is admitted. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195AAB–0x195AF7`.

The creature table is an ordered tree keyed by the eight-byte identifier; each entry holds its key at entry `+0x10` and the creature pointer at entry `+0x18`. The ordering compares the high four bytes as signed and, when those are equal, the low four bytes as unsigned. A found entry must match both halves exactly. A zero identifier is an ordinary key: an actual entry with key zero can be found. The end marker and a null stored pointer are distinct rejection cases. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, helper `0x077D90–0x077E03`.

For an admitted target, if the world's four-byte pointer field `+0xB8` is null and the target's role byte is zero, the routine takes a delivery-only route: it appends a copy of the incoming hit record to the end of the list that begins at the output aggregate's offset zero, using helper `0x086290`, and returns. It performs no source lookup and none of the local effects described below. World byte `+0xB4` is not consulted before this route. A role-zero target that reaches this routine therefore need not be locally changed. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195AFD–0x195B18`.

The appended copy carries the record's meaningful fields but not necessarily its padding. The copy transfers four-byte units at `+0` through `+0x10`, the byte at `+0x14`, four-byte units `+0x18` through `+0x40`, and the bytes `+0x44`, `+0x45` and `+0x46`. Bytes `+0x15–+0x17` and `+0x47` are not copied. The list's element-count limit raises a length failure through the runtime; allocation and length failure are outside ordinary completion. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, helpers `0x086290`, `0x051EE0`, `0x0599A0`.

Every other admitted target takes the local route. In this document only, **designated target** means a target whose pointer is identical to world `+0xB8`; this avoids assigning that field an unproved meaning. When `+0xB8` is nonnull the delivery-only route is unavailable to every target, but later steps still compare the target pointer with it. How world `+0xB4` and `+0xB8` are initialised and change over time is not established here.

## Source identity and effects before protection

The local route next looks up the record's source identifier in the same table. A miss gives a null source; a found entry with a null stored pointer also gives a null source. Source health is not examined. No substitution occurs: neither source nor target is replaced by an owner identifier, by world `+0xB8`, by any linked identifier, or by any other field. Source and target may be the same creature. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195B1D–0x195B43`.

When the amount is strictly greater than zero (a NaN or zero amount does not qualify), the target's binary32 field `+0x1190` is reduced by a level-scaled amount:

1. The target's signed four-byte `+0x190`, minus one, is added to the target's byte `+0x1A8` read as an unsigned value. The integer sum is converted to binary32 and multiplied by binary32 0.25.
2. That product is widened to binary64 and used as the exponent in the runtime's precise power routine with binary64 base 2 (the imported routine is identified by the symbol table as the precise SSE2 power function). The binary64 result is narrowed to binary32.
3. Separately, the record amount is multiplied by binary32 0.25, and that product is divided by the narrowed power result.
4. The quotient is subtracted from target `+0x1190` and the result stored. Every step rounds to binary32.
5. If the stored result compares less than zero it is replaced by positive zero. Zero, negative zero and NaN results are kept as stored.

Still within the strictly positive case, if the target's action byte `+0x68` equals 84 it is set to zero and the target's signed four-byte field `+0x12C` is set to 3000. These effects happen before the contributor protection gate and before any health admission, and do not depend on a later health reduction. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195B3E–0x195BD5`; power import reached through `0x28D8D4`; constants at RVAs `0x345E18` (binary64 2) and `0x345D98` (binary32 0.25).

When the amount compares greater than or equal to zero (so positive zero and negative zero qualify, and NaN does not) and the source is nonnull, the following effects occur in this order:

1. The source's eight-byte field `+0x11D0` receives the record's target identifier; then the target's `+0x11D0` receives the record's source identifier.
2. In the source's map at `+0x13AC`, the binary32 value keyed by the record's target identifier is increased by binary32 1.
3. In the target's map at `+0x13A4`, the binary32 value keyed by the record's source identifier is increased. The increment is the amount times a weight, plus binary32 `0x3C23D70A` (about 0.01). The weighted amount is formed first, the constant is added to it next, and the stored value is added last; each step rounds to binary32.
4. Helper `0x1A0970` runs with the world as receiver and three arguments: source, target and the output aggregate.

The maps at `+0x13A4` and `+0x13AC` are ordered trees with the same key ordering as the creature table, each holding a binary32 value at entry `+0x18`. Map helper `0x068AD0` returns the value for a key, creating an entry with value zero when the key is absent. The weight is chosen as follows: if source predicate `0x044520` is true, the amount is used unscaled (weight 1); otherwise, if the source's kind is 25, the weight is 10; otherwise, if the source's byte `+0xAA8` equals 3, item predicate `0x044820` holds for the two bytes at source `+0xAA8`, and the source's byte `+0x141` equals 1, the weight is 20; otherwise the weight is 2. Equal source and target identifiers do not skip these writes, so a nonnull self-source can create a positive self-keyed value even for a zero amount. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195BD5–0x195CE4`; helpers `0x068AD0`, `0x044520`, `0x044820`; constants at RVAs `0x2FCDA0` (0.01), `0x345DC0` (1), `0x345E74` (10), `0x2FCD74` (20), `0x345E10` (2).

Predicate `0x044520` is true when the creature's byte `+0x140` equals 3, when its kind is 117, 86 or 104, or when its byte `+0xAA8` equals 3 and byte `+0xAA9` is one of 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 or 12. Otherwise it is false. Item predicate `0x044820` is true when the first of its two bytes is 3 and the second is one of 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 15, 16, 17 or 18. These sets choose tracking weights only; they are not health-admission sets. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x044520–0x044582`, `0x044820–0x044855`.

Helper `0x1A0970`, given a first creature A, a second creature B and the aggregate, behaves as follows. It first appends one record to the list at aggregate `+0x50`, holding B's own identifier, then A's own identifier, then the binary32 value in B's `+0x13A4` map keyed by A's identifier (created as zero if absent). In the call above A is the source and B is the target. It then visits the world creature table in ascending key order. Entries with a null creature pointer, and creatures identical to A or to B, are skipped. For each remaining candidate C, relationship predicate `0x196CA0` is evaluated with C as its first argument and A as its second. If that is true, C must lie strictly within squared distance 64 of B. Each of the three coordinate differences, C minus B, is taken in signed 64-bit arithmetic, rounded once to binary32, and multiplied by binary32 1/65536. Each difference is squared with its own rounding; the squares for `+0x10` and `+0x18` are added, and the square for `+0x20` is added to that sum. A squared distance equal to 64, or NaN, does not pass. For a passing candidate, C's `+0x13A4` value keyed by A's identifier is fetched (created as zero if absent). If it compares equal to zero (positive or negative zero, not NaN), it is set to binary32 0.5 and another record holding C's identifier, A's identifier and that value (0.5) is appended to aggregate `+0x50`. No candidate's health is checked. These are relationship records, not further 72-byte hits. The helper reads A's identifier before anything else, so it requires a valid A. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x1A0970–0x1A0BE0`; constants at RVAs `0x2FD330` (64), `0x2FCD94` (1/65536).

## Contributor protection and its established producers

After the effects above, bit mask `0x2000` of the target's flag word `+0x7E` decides whether an extra gate applies. With the bit clear, processing continues. With it set, the routine walks the target's `+0x13A4` map in ascending key order and looks for an entry satisfying all of the following: its value compares strictly greater than zero (zero, negative and NaN values fail); its key is found in the world creature table; the found entry's creature pointer is nonnull; and that creature's role byte is zero. The first such entry lets processing continue. An empty map, or a map with no such entry, returns. The contributor is not compared with the current source, any owner, or world `+0xB8`, and its health is not checked. Because only existence matters, the walk order has no other visible effect. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195CE7–0x195D6A`.

This gate does not undo earlier effects. The current hit can already have created or increased its source's entry in the target's map, and helper `0x1A0970` can already have appended records. When the source itself has role zero and is in the table, its newly positive entry can satisfy the gate. Conversely, a hit that is rejected here can leave tracking state, the `+0x1190` reduction and the action/`+0x12C` change in place.

Producers known so far are limited. The map updates and helper fanout above produce map values. Routine `0x059C40` writes zero into the role byte of the creature referenced through its object's field `+0x8006D0` at `0x06216C`; routine `0x04BE40` writes zero into the role byte of the creature it is processing at `0x04BF98`. These show that role zero is actually written, not that role zero uniquely means local ownership. Complete producers of role values, kind values, the flag word and specifically mask `0x2000` are not established. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x062166–0x062176`, `0x04BF90–0x04BF98`.

## Response, timers and category exits

Category byte 3 returns immediately after the contributor gate (or after the flag test when the gate does not apply). It therefore skips all of the following response, timer, resource and health work, but keeps any earlier tracking and positive-amount effects. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195D70–0x195D74`.

For any other category, the response vector is processed only when the amount compares greater than or equal to zero; negative and NaN amounts skip directly to the second record timer step below. When the target's signed `+0x12C` is greater than zero and the record's first timer `+0x18` is less than or equal to zero, each of the three incoming components is multiplied by binary32 `0x3DCCCCCD` (about 0.1); otherwise they are used unchanged. The `+0x12C` value read here includes any 3000 written earlier in the same call. The target's binary32 response storage `+0x4C/+0x50/+0x54` may be replaced only when the target's role is not 6 and either world byte `+0xB4` is zero and the target's role is nonzero, or the target is the designated target. Replacement additionally requires the incoming squared magnitude to compare greater than or equal to the stored squared magnitude; equality replaces, and a NaN on either side prevents replacement. Each squared magnitude squares its three components separately, adds the first two, then adds the third, rounding to binary32 each time. The three components are stored in increasing offset order. Independently of whether replacement happened (including for role 6), a strictly positive first record timer `+0x18` sets the target's binary32 fields `+0x34`, `+0x38` and `+0x3C` to zero. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195D7A–0x195E51`; constant at RVA `0x2FCB7C` (0.1).

Next, for every amount, a strictly positive second record timer `+0x1C` raises the target's signed four-byte `+0x130` to the larger of its previous value and the record value (signed comparison); a zero or negative record value leaves it unchanged. Category 1 then adds binary32 0.25 to the target's binary32 field `+0x170`, stores the sum, replaces it with 1 if it compares strictly greater than 1 (a NaN sum is kept), and returns. Category 4 calls target helper `0x03CF20` with the output aggregate and then returns whatever the helper did. Neither category reaches the later stages. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195E51–0x195EB5`; constants at RVAs `0x345D98` (0.25), `0x345DC0` (1).

Helper `0x03CF20` acts only when the creature's four-byte field `+0x128` is nonzero, its byte `+0x140` equals 4 and its byte `+0x141` equals 1. It then adds 0.25 to `+0x170` with the same cap at 1; builds a 24-byte resident-effect record with type byte 11, binary32 amount zero, four-byte duration 30000 and eight-byte source identifier zero; applies it to the creature through helper `0x046AF0`; and appends to the list at aggregate `+0x58` a 40-byte event holding the creature's own identifier twice followed by that 24-byte record. Bytes of the 24-byte record that this helper does not write are not established, and the resident application policy of `0x046AF0` is a separate dependency. When the admission fails, none of these effects occur, but the caller still returns. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x03CF20–0x03CFE1`.

For all remaining categories, a strictly positive first record timer `+0x18` raises the target's signed `+0x12C` to the larger of its current value and that timer. This happens before the health ownership test. Then, if world byte `+0xB4` is nonzero and the target is not the designated target, the routine returns. Non-designated targets can therefore receive all earlier local effects and still be denied health processing. When world byte `+0xB4` is zero, every target that reaches this point proceeds. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195EBA–0x195EE7`.

## Amount and health admission

The health phase separates amounts that compare strictly greater than zero, strictly less than zero, and neither (zero of either sign, or NaN).

For a strictly positive amount with category 5, the routine walks the target's list at `+0x1178` from front to back. For each element whose type byte (element `+8`) is 6, the element's binary32 amount at `+0xC` is reduced by the full hit amount and stored; if the result compares less than or equal to zero, both that amount and the four-byte duration at element `+0x10` become zero (a NaN result is kept). For every type-6 element, whether or not it was zeroed, a 40-byte event is appended to the list at aggregate `+0x58`: the record's source identifier, the record's target identifier, then the element's 24 bytes starting at element `+8`. No element is removed, and target health is not reduced on this category-5 path. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195EED–0x195FC7`.

For a strictly positive amount with any other category reaching this phase, health-admission helper `0x03E310` is evaluated first on the target. Only if that is true and the source is nonnull is it evaluated on the source. A null source skips the second evaluation and does not by itself block the reduction. When admitted, target health becomes its stored value minus the record amount, with one binary32 subtraction. If either required evaluation is false, health is unchanged. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x195FCC–0x196003`.

Helper `0x03E310` is true for role bytes 0, 1, 3 and 5. For role 6 it is true unless the four-byte kind is 140, 141 or 142, in which case it is false. It is false for every other role byte. It examines nothing else: not health, world state, maps, identity, amount or resident effects. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x03E310–0x03E344`.

An amount that is zero (either sign) or NaN changes no health, though earlier steps may already have run. A strictly negative amount, in any category reaching this phase, skips both admission evaluations: target health becomes its stored value minus the amount, one binary32 subtraction, which raises health. Maximum-health helper `0x044DB0` is then called once and its result rounded to binary32. If the new health compares strictly greater than that value, the helper is called a second time and its result, rounded to binary32, becomes the stored health. Equality or NaN does not cause the second call. The maximum-health calculation itself is outside this contract. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x196008–0x196052`.

For a strictly negative amount only, and only when world byte `+0xB4` is zero, the routine then visits the world creature table in ascending key order. No creature is excluded except entries with a null pointer; the target and source themselves are visited. For each candidate C, relationship predicate `0x196CA0` is evaluated with the source as its first argument and C as its second, the reverse of the order used inside helper `0x1A0970`. If true, C's `+0x13A4` map must already contain an entry keyed by the target creature's own identifier; presence is enough and its value is not examined. C's entry keyed by the source creature's own identifier is then fetched (created as zero if absent) and reduced by the amount times binary32 0.1, which increases it because the amount is negative. Helper `0x1A0970` is then called with the source first, C second and the aggregate third. All of this precedes the final health comparison. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x196058–0x19612D`; constant at RVA `0x2FCB7C` (0.1).

That traversal has no guard against a null source. If the source is null, world byte `+0xB4` is zero and the table holds at least one nonnull creature, the predicate is reached with a null first argument and reads through it. The health increase and clamp will already have happened. No successful return is established for this combination, so it must not be treated as a supported source-free heal. With world byte `+0xB4` nonzero the traversal is skipped. Positive source-free hits follow the separate admission described above.

The byte at record `+0x44` is not read by any of these admission or amount steps; none of them doubles or otherwise rescales the amount. They use the amount already in the record. How a producer computed that amount is not specified here.

## Relationship predicate

For predicate `0x196CA0`, the order of its two creature arguments matters. Its world receiver is not used. It decides as follows:

1. False when the first creature's role is 5 and the second's is 0.
2. False when either creature's role is 6.
3. True when exactly one of the two has role 1.
4. When both have role 1, helper `0x044680` is evaluated on the first creature and then on the second. Different results give true. Equal results lead to a second evaluation of the same pair in the same order, again giving true on a difference, and otherwise continuing to step 5.
5. True when bit mask `0x20` is set in byte `+0x124` of the first creature, or else of the second creature; false when neither has it.

No identity, identifier, owner, health or distance test occurs in this predicate. It reads both arguments directly, so both must be valid creatures. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x196CA0–0x196D2A`.

Helper `0x044680` is true only when the creature's flag word `+0x7E` has no bit from mask `0x1A00` set and its four-byte kind is one of these decimal values:

`19, 20, 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 53, 55, 56, 57, 58, 67, 68, 74, 87, 89, 92, 93, 98, 100, 106, 107, 145, 146, 147`.

Otherwise it is false. Complete producers of these kind and flag values and of mask `0x20` in byte `+0x124` are not established, and the predicate's result is not given a faction or ownership meaning. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x044680–0x044759`.

## Fatal boundary and output distinction

Every path that reaches `0x196133` reads target health again. Health that compares strictly greater than zero returns normally. Health that is less than or equal to zero, including negative zero, takes the fatal path starting at `0x196149`, and so does a NaN health. Exactly zero is fatal here although it was rejected at admission; a NaN health is admitted at entry and fatal here. The first visible fatal effects are: health is set to positive zero; a record is appended to the list at aggregate `+8` holding the target's position (each signed 64-bit coordinate at `+0x10/+0x18/+0x20` rounded once to binary32 and multiplied by 1/65536), a four-byte value 27 and two binary32 values of 1; and the target's four-byte field `+0x6C` is set to zero. Further object and region work follows, and at `0x19622E` the world calls routine `0x19B180` with the target and the held third argument. Everything from the fatal boundary onward, including `0x19B180`, removal, rewards, linked actors and the eventual return, is outside this contract. Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x196133–0x196193`, `0x196221–0x19622E`; helpers `0x02C4A0`, `0x0864C0`.

The category-3, category-1 and category-4 exits, a failed contributor gate and a failed ownership test all leave before the final comparison. Their side effects must be kept separate from this routine's own fatal path. Called resident and maximum-health helpers, and runtime failures, are not covered by any promise of harmless or all-or-nothing failure.

The local route never appends another 72-byte hit just because local changes were made. Relationship records, resident-effect events and the fatal record each go to their own lists in their own order. The delivery-only route, by contrast, appends the incoming hit without local effects. The routine returns no value that could stand in for these separate outcomes. A caller can append a hit and then call this routine under its own mode test: inside `0x196D30`, the hit is appended at `0x196FEE` and the call at `0x19700F` happens only when world byte `+0xB4` is zero (`0x196FF3–0x19700F`). Within `0x20C510`, the call at `0x20CD9B` occurs while walking a separate input collection. Neither observation establishes socket delivery, remote replay or equivalence with any other health consumer.

## Unresolved

- Type, ownership and use of the third argument beyond being passed to `0x19B180`.
- Purpose, gates and complete routes of the eight callers, including the two call sites at `0x214BAC` and `0x214F72` whose enclosing routine the listing does not identify.
- Initialisation and lifecycle of world `+0xB4` and `+0xB8`; meaning of the designated target.
- Complete producers of role bytes, kinds, flag word `+0x7E` (in particular masks `0x2000` and `0x1A00`), byte `+0x124` mask `0x20`, and the maps at `+0x13A4/+0x13AC`.
- Meanings of the labelled fields (`+0x1190`, `+0x190`, `+0x1A8`, `+0x12C`, `+0x130`, `+0x170`, `+0x34–+0x3C`, `+0x4C–+0x54`, `+0x6C`, `+0x128`, `+0x140`, `+0x141`, `+0xAA8/+0xAA9`) beyond the behaviour stated.
- Internal behaviour of `0x044DB0`, `0x046AF0` and the event constructor `0x03B660`, and the unwritten bytes of the 24-byte record built by `0x03CF20`.
- Behaviour with infinite amounts or health, power overflow or underflow, malformed identifiers or containers, concurrent modification, allocation or length failure, and the null-source negative-amount combination.
- No random draw appears in the pre-fatal body, helper `0x1A0970`, predicate `0x196CA0`, or helpers `0x03E310`, `0x044520`, `0x044820`, `0x044680` and `0x077D90`; this is not extended to fatal processing or to helpers whose bodies were not examined.

## Re-certification (2026-11-22)

A fresh source-only author re-checked every statement in this document against the original client image and its disassembly listing, without using any reconstruction or implementation material. Image size, hash and base, every cited RVA range, every constant and every comparison direction were confirmed, with these changes: the kind set for helper `0x044680` listed 59 where the image has 89; the direct call-site list was incomplete and now includes `0x221E14`, `0x214BAC` and `0x214F72`; NaN and signed-zero outcomes are now stated for each comparison (NaN target health is admitted at entry and fatal at the final comparison, negative-zero health is rejected at entry, a zero map value in `0x1A0970` includes a newly created or negative-zero entry); and caller role names ("world-step", "client construction", "load routine") and field role names were reduced to neutral labels. Evidence citations to decompiled function files were replaced by citations to the image. What remains open is listed in the unresolved section above.
