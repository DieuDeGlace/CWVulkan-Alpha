# Alpha world-hit fatal effects

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD after complete author readback; re-certified against the original client image on 2026-11-22. This independent contract covers the fatal continuation of client world-hit application, its immediate state and output effects, and bounded loot, numeric adjustment and credit dependencies. It does not specify later corpse disposal or respawn processing.

## Evidence and application boundary

The supported original image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, preferred image base `0x00400000`. Addresses below are RVAs. Evidence paths are relative to `Documents/disassembled alpha cube world`. The main evidence is the native instruction listing `decompiled/client/assembly.txt` for the routines at `0x195A60`, `0x196D30`, `0x19AEB0`, `0x19B180`, `0x1A0BF0`, `0x0C7E80`, `0x047B00`, `0x03CA60` and `0x0462F0`, read together with the matching per-routine decompiled listings under `decompiled/client/functions/` and constant bytes read directly from the image. Where the decompiled listings omit or misstate a branch, the native instructions govern. No reconstruction, implementation material, runtime finding or earlier contract was used as evidence.

World application routine `0x195A60` receives the world in ECX and three stack arguments: a hit-record pointer, a mutable output aggregate, and a caller-owned collection used by nested drop publication. It returns by removing twelve argument bytes. Denote the record by R, the aggregate by A and the collection by C. The routine looks up R's eight-byte target key at `+0x08` in the world creature map at world `+0x04`; the mapped pointer is the target T used by the continuation. R's eight-byte source key is at `+0x00`, its binary32 amount at `+0x10`, and its effect selector byte at `+0x45`. [Evidence: native `0x195A8E–0x195AC7`.]

The target lookup must find an entry with a nonnull pointer. An initial comparison exits when T health at `+0x16C` is ordered at most zero; an unordered health value passes this first comparison. When world `+0xB8` is null and T byte `+0x60` is zero, the routine appends the hit record to the list at A `+0x00` and returns without further work. Otherwise the source key is looked up separately, giving a source pointer that may be null. A missing source does not by itself exclude every later branch. [Evidence: native `0x195ACC–0x195B3E`.]

Several stages precede fatal admission and are bounded here rather than replaced by a universal rule that positive hits kill. They include relationship and tracking updates; a gate when T word flags at `+0x7E` contain `0x2000`, which requires an entry in T's relationship map `+0x13A4` with an ordered-positive binary32 value whose key resolves to a nonnull creature with byte `+0x60` zero, and otherwise returns; and effect-specific work. Selector three returns before any health change. Selector one adjusts a binary32 field at `+0x170` and returns. Selector four calls `0x03CF20` and returns. Next, when world byte `+0xB4` is nonzero, T must equal world `+0xB8` or the routine returns. A positive amount then either takes the selector-five path, which reduces values held by T's resident list entries of type six instead of health, or subtracts R's amount from T health, provided predicate `0x03E310` is true for T and for the source when one was resolved. A negative amount takes a healing path capped by maximum-health helper `0x044DB0`. The fatal comparison is at `0x19613C`: binary32 zero is compared with current T health and the routine returns when the comparison sets carry. The fatal body is therefore entered only for ordered health at most zero; unordered health does not enter it. [Evidence: native `0x195B3E–0x196143`.]

The target pointer, the target's own ID and the map entry key are separate inputs. Application uses the pointer obtained by looking up R's target key and never checks it against T's own ID at `+0x08/+0x0C`. Generic hit construction at `0x196D30` normally copies the supplied target creature's own ID into R `+0x08` and the source creature's own ID into R `+0x00`, using an all-zero source key when no source is supplied. It appends the record to the list at A `+0x00` and, when world `+0xB4` is zero, calls `0x195A60` synchronously from `0x1986D0`. Queued records can also be applied by the world routine at `0x20CD9B`. That producer does not remove the distinction at the later lookup boundary. [Evidence: native `0x1986B4–0x1986D5`; `00596d30.c`; native `0x20CD80–0x20CDA0`.]

## Immediate fatal order

The following order is synchronous inside `0x195A60`, subject to the stated nested calls completing. Allocation failure or a fault in a nested call is not rolled back.

1. T health `+0x16C` is written as four zero bytes. A position is formed from T's three signed 64-bit fixed coordinates beginning at `+0x10`, each converted to binary32 and multiplied by binary32 `1/65536` (bits `0x37800000`) through `0x02C4A0`. An effect record is appended to the list at A `+0x08`, carrying that position, 32-bit selector `0x1B`, and two binary32 values equal to one. T's 32-bit field `+0x6C` is then set to zero; the append precedes this store. This sequence writes no value to T byte `+0x68`. [Evidence: native `0x196149–0x19618C`; `0042c4a0.c`.]

2. World object lookup `0x034D10` receives T's signed 32-bit fields `+0x1B0` and `+0x1B4`, in that argument order. If it returns an object, the object's vector of `0x188`-byte records (begin at `+0x0C`, end at `+0x10`) is scanned in order. The first record whose 32-bit kind at `+0x00` is five, whose 32-bit field `+0x178` equals T `+0x1B8`, and whose byte `+0x30` is nonzero is handled: routine `0x1A58E0` is called with that record, A and requested byte state zero, and a snapshot is then appended to the list at A `+0x30`. The snapshot carries T `+0x1B0`, T `+0x1B4`, the record's index in the vector, and then a field copy of the record. The scan stops at that match. [Evidence: native `0x196193–0x196219`; `00468670.c`.]

3. Loot routine `0x19B180` is called with T and C. Its admitted drop construction and random draws happen before the later relationship and credit work. The call is made even when that routine's own entry checks cause an immediate return. [Evidence: native `0x196221–0x19622E`.]

4. The world creature map is traversed. For each entry with a nonnull pointer whose byte `+0x60` is zero, a 64-bit fixed-coordinate difference (that creature's coordinates minus T's) is written into a temporary through `0x02C7A0`. The temporary is not used afterwards, so this loop applies no distance-based recipient filter. Next, T's eight-byte link at `+0x11C8` is examined. If it is nonzero and resolves through the world map to a nonnull creature, that creature's health `+0x16C` is set to zero. This is a direct store, with no second fatal sequence, loot or effect for that creature in this routine. [Evidence: native `0x196233–0x1962DC`; `0042c7a0.c`.]

5. A common credit amount is calculated, then optional regional progress and completion work runs. The world creature map is then traversed once for relationship cleanup and per-recipient credit and grants. The final backing-record writes occur after that traversal. These stages are detailed below. [Evidence: native `0x1962E6–0x196C49`.]

Routine `0x1A58E0` changes the matching record only when its byte `+0x30` differs from the requested state. Its signed 32-bit field `+0x34` becomes `1000` minus the old value, replaced by zero when negative, and byte `+0x30` then receives the requested state. For a kind-five record this change also appends an effect to the list at A `+0x08` with selector `0x33`, a position converted from the record's 64-bit fixed triplet beginning at `+0x08` (each scaled by `1/65536`), and two binary32 ones. The snapshot that follows therefore sees the changed fields. The snapshot field copy covers selected fields only, and this contract does not claim that padding or skipped words are initialized. [Evidence: `005a58e0.c`; native call at `0x1961E9`; `00468670.c`.]

## Regional progress, relationships and final backing record

Before the regional stage, a local completion flag is cleared and a local forty-byte regional descriptor copy is zero-filled. Regional lookup is admitted only when world byte `+0xB4` is zero. T's signed fields `+0x1B0` and `+0x1B4` are each divided by eight with truncation toward zero and passed, in that order, to `0x087DA0`. The returned object G must be nonnull and its byte `+0x41` must differ from two. T word flags `+0x7E` must contain `0x2000`, or G's 32-bit selector `+0x34` must be five and T byte `+0x1D38` nonzero. T's relationship map `+0x13A4` is then searched in map order for an entry whose binary32 value at entry `+0x18` is ordered greater than zero and whose key resolves through the world map to a nonnull creature with byte `+0x60` zero. Only the first such entry admits the selector decision below; a key with a non-positive value does not. If no entry qualifies, the regional stage ends with no event. [Evidence: native `0x19638D–0x1964E6`; constant at `0x345D84` is binary32 zero.]

G's selector `+0x34` chooses the action through a table at `0x196C78/0x196C84`. Selectors one through five and thirteen set G byte `+0x41` to two and set the local completion flag. Selectors seven through twelve first increment G's 32-bit progress at `+0x44` and store one into G byte `+0x41`; when the updated signed progress is at least G's signed goal at `+0x48`, they set G byte `+0x41` to two and set the local completion flag. Selector six and any value outside one through thirteen produce no progress change and no event. For every selector that acts, the forty-byte descriptor beginning at G `+0x2C`, as changed, is copied into the local descriptor and then into an event appended to the list at A `+0x60`. The event's first two 32-bit fields are T's two signed coordinates divided by eight toward zero, and the descriptor copy begins at event `+0x10`. The event is zero-initialized first and the descriptor is copied by fields, so no claim is made about byte-exact padding. [Evidence: native `0x1964E6–0x196579`; jump table bytes in the image; `00468620.c`, `00465c70.c`.]

When the local completion flag is set, the routine visits an eight-by-eight block of spatial coordinates, X outermost and Y innermost. Each axis starts at the signed coordinate divided by eight (toward zero) and multiplied by eight, and covers eight consecutive values. For each found spatial object, its pointer vector at `+0x18` is processed from the signed index stored at object `+0xA0` up to the current end. Each nonnull element receives a call through the first slot of its virtual table with argument one, and the vector is then resized to the stored index. This happens inside fatal application, before the relationship traversal. Spatial lookup, the called virtual function and vector failure behavior are bounded dependencies. [Evidence: native `0x19658B–0x19667D`.]

The traversal that follows uses each world-map entry's current pointer P and its map entry key independently. For nonnull P, P's eight-byte link at `+0x11D0/+0x11D4` is cleared, lower 32-bit word first, when it equals either R's target key or T's link at `+0x11C8`. These are 32-bit integer word comparisons. Entries in P's relationship map `+0x13A4` whose key equals either of those two values are collected into a temporary list during the scan and erased through `0x03EDE0` after it. These are relationship-map erasures; the loop does not erase any world creature-map entry. T's link remains the comparison source, and a zero link value is not excluded from these equality tests. [Evidence: native `0x196685–0x196821`.]

After every map entry has been visited, T's coordinates `+0x1B0/+0x1B4` must each be nonnegative and below `65,536`. Each is divided by sixty-four to give region coordinates, which must be below `1024`, and the region pointer at world `+0xBC` indexed by X times `1024` plus Y must be nonnull. The remainders modulo sixty-four select the spatial object pointer at region `+0x10018` indexed by local X times `64` plus local Y, which must be nonnull. T's signed index `+0x1B8` must be nonnegative and below the element count of that object's pointer vector at `+0x18`. The selected element's record then receives 32-bit `1,200,000` at `+0x38`, followed by the world's 32-bit value at `+0x800160` at `+0x3C`. The element pointer itself is not tested for null at this store. Any failed condition skips both stores. These are immediate backing-record writes; their later consumers and respawn meaning are not specified here. [Evidence: native `0x196B5F–0x196C49`.]

## Credit calculation and synchronous consumers

Scalar routine `0x03CA60` takes a binary32 input, subtracts one, multiplies by binary32 `0.05000000074505806` (bits `0x3D4CCCCD`), adds one, divides one by that result, and subtracts the quotient from one. Each operation is a scalar binary32 operation. The stored binary32 result is returned on the x87 stack. Denote that rounded result by F of the input; this notation is descriptive only. [Evidence: native `0x03CA60–0x03CA94`.]

For fatal credit, T's signed 32-bit field `+0x190` is added to its zero-extended byte `+0x1A8` with 32-bit arithmetic. The sum is converted to binary32 and passed to `0x03CA60`. The result is stored as binary32, multiplied by `20` (bits `0x41A00000`), and replaced by binary32 one only when it is ordered below one. When predicate `0x0445D0` is true, the value is multiplied by twenty again. That predicate is true exactly when T's 32-bit kind at `+0x64` is one of `0x65`, `0x6B`, `0x6C`, `0x6D`, `0x72`, `0x73`, `0x74`, `0x75`, `0x76`, `0x77`. When T word flags `+0x7E` contain `0x200`, the value is then multiplied by ten (bits `0x41200000`). Finally, flag `0x800` forces binary32 zero. There is no second lower bound after these adjustments. [Evidence: native `0x1962E6–0x196385`; `004445d0.c`; constants at `0x2FCD74`, `0x345E74`, `0x345DC0`, `0x2FCDB4`.]

During the map traversal, ordinary experience credit is considered only when T byte `+0x60` is one and P byte `+0x60` is zero. T's relationship map must contain P's map entry key; this test does not recheck the value's sign or size. A 24-byte record is appended to the list at A `+0x48`. It holds the recipient's map entry key at `+0x00`, R's target key at `+0x08`, T's 32-bit kind from `+0x64` at `+0x10`, and the common credit at `+0x14`, converted from binary32 to signed 32-bit with truncation toward zero. The record is then passed at once to world consumer `0x1A0BF0`. A computed credit of zero does not suppress the record. [Evidence: native `0x196824–0x1968E8`; `00451d00.c`.]

Consumer `0x1A0BF0` looks up both record keys in the world map, the recipient key at `+0x00` and the second key at `+0x08`, and returns unless both resolve to nonnull pointers. It then performs three independent stages in order:

- If the second creature equals world `+0xB8`, that creature's link at `+0x11C8` is looked up. If it resolves to a nonnull creature, that creature's health `+0x16C` is set to zero and its relationship map `+0x13A4` is cleared. The world `+0xB8` creature's eight-byte link at `+0x11D0` is then cleared in either case.
- If world `+0xB8` is nonnull, equals the recipient, and the second creature's byte `+0x60` is one, the record's signed 32-bit credit is added to the recipient's `+0x194` and level routine `0x047B00` runs on the recipient.
- If world byte `+0xB4` is zero, the recipient's link at `+0x11C8` is looked up, whether or not the recipient is the world `+0xB8` creature. If it resolves to a nonnull creature with ordered-positive health, that creature receives the same credit at `+0x194` and the same level routine. Its resulting `+0x194` is then copied to recipient `+0x1024`, the low sixteen bits of its `+0x190` to recipient `+0x1030`, and the item-shaped block at recipient `+0x1020` is copied to recipient `+0x1D48` through `0x02C5E0`.

All three stages complete before the consumer returns. [Evidence: native `0x1A0BF0–0x1A0D78`.]

Level routine `0x047B00` does nothing unless the creature's signed level at `+0x190` is positive. For level n, its threshold is F(n) multiplied by binary32 `1000` (bits `0x447A0000`), plus binary32 `50` (bits `0x42480000`), truncated to signed 32-bit. All of this arithmetic is scalar binary32. While the signed accumulated value at `+0x194` is at least the threshold for the current level, the threshold is subtracted, the level is incremented, and health `+0x16C` is set to the x87 result of maximum-health routine `0x044DB0`, stored as binary32. The threshold is recomputed for the new level before each further test. Credit application can therefore change level and health before the fatal caller returns, so the queued credit record is not the only effect. [Evidence: native `0x047B00–0x047C1C`; calls at `0x1A0CF2`, `0x1A0D41`; constant at `0x2FD8F4`.]

## Completion grants and their order

When T byte `+0x60` is one, P byte `+0x60` is zero and the local completion flag is set, two grant records are appended to the list at A `+0x40` for that recipient. This gate is reached even when the preceding experience relationship-key test failed. Each grant record holds the recipient's map entry key at `+0x00` and an item beginning at `+0x08`. The first item has category byte thirteen and a quantity in the 16-bit item field `+0x10`. The quantity is formed from the local descriptor copy: its signed 32-bit field `+0x10` plus its zero-extended byte `+0x14`, converted to binary32 and passed to `0x03CA60`. The result is stored as binary32, multiplied by fifty (bits `0x42480000`), increased by binary32 one, truncated to signed 32-bit, and stored as its low sixteen bits. There is no explicit quantity clamp. [Evidence: native `0x1968ED–0x1969F7`; `00451cc0.c`.]

If P equals world `+0xB8`, that first item is then passed to `0x06EBE0` on P's inventory object at `+0x11DC`, with slot argument minus one. With minus one, that routine first derives a slot from the item and may enlarge its slot list. For category thirteen it then adds the item's signed 16-bit quantity to the inventory object's 32-bit counter at `+0x12C` and returns. A recipient other than world `+0xB8` gets the grant record only. [Evidence: native `0x1969F9–0x196A19`; `0046ebe0.c`.]

Next, one draw from the imported C runtime `rand` is reduced by signed remainder modulo two. Remainder zero selects item generator `0x1F51E0`; a nonzero remainder selects `0x1F8AD0`. The generator receives the descriptor copy's signed field `+0x10`, its zero-extended byte `+0x14`, and P's byte `+0x140`. The generated item replaces the temporary item, a second grant record is appended for the same recipient key, and the same local inventory call follows when P equals world `+0xB8`. For each recipient the order is: first grant appended, optional first local grant, parity draw and generation, second grant appended, optional second local grant. The complete generated item, any draws inside the generators, and general inventory stacking and slot policy remain open. [Evidence: native `0x196A1E–0x196AD3`.]

## Loot routine, item adjustment and random order

Loot routine `0x19B180` takes T and C on the stack and the world in ECX. It returns before any draw when T byte `+0x60` is zero or three, or when T word flags `+0x7E` contain `0x800`. Otherwise it first consumes one `rand` result whose value is discarded. It then builds a temporary list of pointers to nonempty item slots in this order: T `+0x990`, `+0xAA8`, `+0x530`, `+0x878`, `+0x760`, `+0x648`, `+0x418`. An item slot is nonempty when its category byte is nonzero. The slots are referenced for copying and are not cleared. [Evidence: native `0x19B1C0–0x19B326`.]

All drops from this routine go through drop emitter `0x19AEB0`. Its inputs are an item pointer, a fixed position, a binary32 heading, a binary32 scale, and C. Every placement below uses the same pattern unless stated otherwise:

- Offsets: two draws are made in order. Each gives binary32 one minus (the draw converted to binary32, multiplied by two and divided by `32767`, bits `0x46FFFE00`). They become X and Y of a displacement whose third component is zero.
- Conversion: each component is multiplied by binary32 `65536` and converted to signed 64-bit by the runtime routine at `0x28D946`.
- Position: the displacement is added to T's fixed coordinates at `+0x10` with 64-bit arithmetic.
- Heading: a further draw, converted to binary32, multiplied by `360` (bits `0x43B40000`) and divided by `32767`.

If the pointer list is nonempty, a draw is reduced by signed remainder modulo four. Remainder zero, or T flag `0x200`, admits an equipped-item drop; the draw is consumed even when the flag admits it. The admitted branch, in order:

1. makes the two offset draws;
2. draws a list index by unsigned remainder over the list size;
3. draws a heading;
4. calls the emitter with the selected original slot pointer and scale one.

The decompiled listing omits this branch, but the native instructions in the supported image contain it. [Evidence: native `0x19B326–0x19B422`.]

After that, the routine runs the following stages in this order:

- Generated drop. This stage runs when T byte `+0x60` is one or T kind `+0x64` is `0x8F`, and a draw gives signed remainder zero modulo ten. The generated level is T's signed `+0x190` minus one plus a draw's signed remainder modulo two, raised to one if lower. A parity draw follows. Material routine `0x1F8530` is called with T byte `+0x1A8`, and then remainder zero selects generator `0x1F51E0` while a nonzero remainder selects `0x1F8AD0`. The generated item is adjusted by `0x0C7E80` with binary32 radius bits `0x3D4CCCCD` and byte argument one. Placement uses the offsets, heading and emitter with scale one. Next, a draw is reduced modulo five; on remainder zero a second item is produced. For it, `0x1F8530` is called again with T byte `+0x1A8`, generator `0x1F79E0` receives the same level, and the same adjustment and a placement with scale one follow. This second gate exists only inside the admitted first branch.
- Kind-specific material drops. One draw gives a repeat count of its signed remainder modulo three plus one. Each repetition clears a temporary item and fills it according to T's kind at `+0x64` through a table covering kinds `0x3A` to `0x8B`. Kinds `0x78`, `0x79`, `0x7C` and `0x7E` make an extra parity draw inside the repetition. A repetition that leaves a nonzero category is placed with the offsets, heading and emitter, using a kind-specific scale of `0.75`, `3.0` or `0.8`. A repetition that leaves category zero, including every kind outside the table's listed entries, ends the whole stage with no placement draws. The complete kind-to-item table is a bounded dependency here.
- Bonus and currency drops. This stage runs only when T byte `+0x60` is one or T kind is `0x8F`. A draw is reduced modulo fifty. On remainder zero the routine makes the two offset draws and the heading draw first. It then calls material routine `0x1F8530` with T byte `+0x1A8` plus one, calls generator `0x1F6D50` with T level `+0x190`, and finally calls the emitter with scale `0.75`. In this branch the placement draws come before the item generation. Next, T word flags `+0x7E` are tested against `0x18`:
  - Flags clear (currency). Routine `0x045F60` receives T level as binary32 and T byte `+0x1A8`. Its x87 result is stored as binary32 and multiplied by ten. A draw then multiplies the value by binary32 one plus (the draw times two divided by `32767`). Flag `0x200` multiplies by ten again. The result is truncated to signed 32-bit and split by signed division into the remainder modulo one hundred, the next remainder modulo one hundred, and the quotient by ten thousand. Each nonzero part is emitted in that order as a category-twelve item: subtype byte `+0x0D` is `10`, `12` or `11` respectively, and the part is stored in item `+0x10`. Each emitted part gets its own offsets, heading and emitter call with scale `0.75`.
  - Either flag set (category fourteen). A draw's signed remainder modulo one thousand, plus twenty times T byte `+0x1A8`, chooses a tier: zero below `700`, one below `950`, two below `999`, otherwise three. Flag `0x200` raises the tier by one, capped at three. A draw modulo twenty is always made next. Only when its remainder is zero and the tier is nonzero is an item built: category byte fourteen, a further raw draw stored at item `+0x04`, the low sixteen bits of T level at item `+0x10`, and the tier at item byte `+0x0C`. That item is placed with scale `0.75`.
- Inventory drops. This stage runs only when T byte `+0x60` is one. T's list of inventory vectors at `+0x11DC` (12-byte vector headers) is walked in order, and each vector's entries of `0x11C` bytes are walked in order. An entry is used only when its signed 32-bit count at entry `+0x00` is positive and its item category byte at entry `+0x04` is nonzero. Items with category one and subcategory byte `+0x05` one are skipped without draws. Every other admitted entry makes one emitter call per unit of the count, passing the entry's item directly, with the offsets, heading and scale `0.75` before each call. The loop does not remove anything from the source inventory.

These statements fix draw-order boundaries, not probabilities or total draw counts: the generators, the material routine, the currency amount routine and the emitter can consume more draws. [Evidence: native `0x19B427–0x19C3B8`; kind table bytes at `0x19C3D8/0x19C424`; constants at `0x2FCD70`, `0x2FCDC4`, `0x2FCDC8`, `0x2FCE6C`, `0x2FCE7C`, `0x2FD338`.]

Drop emitter `0x19AEB0` returns by removing twenty argument bytes. It first initializes a 328-byte (`0x148`) dropped-item record. The record's default binary32 size value `0x3D924925` is multiplied by the supplied scale. The supplied fixed position and heading are stored. One draw is then made, and its signed remainder modulo `300` plus `500` is stored as a lifetime, together with the world's 32-bit value at `+0x800160`. The fixed X and Y are divided by `65536` and then by `256` toward zero, and the result selects a spatial object through `0x034D10`. If none is found, the emitter returns with nothing stored; only the lifetime draw has been consumed. Otherwise it copies the item into the record and makes another heading draw, which replaces the supplied heading; the caller's earlier heading draw is still consumed. It then searches downward from the fixed Z divided by `65536` (with an extra decrement when the 64-bit Z is negative) for a cell whose type, the low five bits of cell byte `+0x03`, is neither zero nor two. Out-of-range or missing columns and cells use fixed default cells from the image. The stored fixed Z becomes the found cell index plus one, times `65536`. The record is appended at once to the spatial object's record vector at `+0x30`, and the spatial object pointer is inserted into C through `0x18E7D0`. World drop state is therefore already changed, and C names the object for a later publication step owned by the caller. [Evidence: native `0x19AEB0–0x19B178`; `0044a970.c`, `0044d590.c`, `0058e7d0.c`.]

Item adjustment `0x0C7E80` acts on the item's signed 16-bit field `+0x10`. It is not a victim price or damage scalar. It takes a binary32 radius and a byte argument, and returns by removing eight argument bytes. Values below two are left unchanged and consume no draw. For a larger value L, it evaluates F(L) and subtracts the radius. If the difference is ordered at most zero, it stores one without a draw. Otherwise the difference goes to inverse routine `0x0462F0`. That result is stored as binary32, truncated to signed 32-bit, and raised to at least one to give the lower endpoint. With a nonzero byte argument, F(L) plus the radius is capped at binary32 bits `0x3F7FF972` when ordered above it; the inverse of that value, stored as binary32 and truncated, gives the upper endpoint. With a zero byte argument, the upper endpoint is L itself. One draw then gives its signed remainder over (upper minus lower plus one), plus the lower endpoint, and the low sixteen bits replace item `+0x10`. The inverse divides one by (one minus its input), subtracts one, multiplies by twenty, and adds one, all in scalar binary32, returning on the x87 stack. This closes the immediate adjustment and its conditional draw without giving it any economic or policy meaning. [Evidence: native `0x0C7E80–0x0C7F5C`, `0x0462F0–0x046323`; constant at `0x302A3C`.]

## Completion and explicit limits

All of the following happen before `0x195A60` returns: the health store, the `+0x6C` reset, object state changes, stored drop records, linked-health stores, regional progress and spatial-object processing, relationship cleanup, local credit and level-routine calls, local grants, and backing-record stores. Appended aggregate effects, object snapshots, regional events, experience records, grant records and C's object entries are outputs for callers and later consumers. Creating them does not prove rendering, packet transmission, acknowledgement, durable storage or exactly-once remote application. The fatal continuation does not erase T's world-map entry and does not by itself establish a corpse or respawn lifecycle.

Numeric operations keep native binary32 narrowing, 32-bit wraparound and low-word stores; no clamps are added beyond those stated. No server counterpart and no server/client equivalence is inferred.

## Unresolved

- Upstream hit producers, the scalar inputs feeding R, predicate `0x03E310`, the selector-one, selector-four and selector-five paths, and the healing path's later side effects are bounded and not specified in full.
- The producers and full layouts of world objects, regional descriptors and spatial objects, and the virtual function called during completion processing.
- Default terrain cells, the termination of the emitter's downward search when no supporting cell exists, and the snapshot or transport consumer of C.
- Item generators `0x1F51E0`, `0x1F8AD0`, `0x1F79E0`, `0x1F6D50`, material routine `0x1F8530`, the kind-specific material table contents, and currency amount routine `0x045F60`.
- Maximum-health routine `0x044DB0`, and overflow or exceptional behavior of the level loop.
- General inventory slot derivation, growth and stacking in `0x06EBE0` for categories other than thirteen.
- The imported `rand` algorithm, its state and its sharing with other callers, and the exact rounding and control-word behavior of runtime conversion routine `0x28D946` and the 64-bit division routine used by the emitter.
- Exceptional floating-point inputs, invalid pointers or indices, integer division by zero or by a non-positive range in the adjustment draw, ambient SSE and x87 control state, allocation failure and length-limit aborts, and synchronization outside the documented calls are not certified safe.

## Re-certification (2026-11-22)

A fresh source-only author, with no access to implementation material, re-checked every statement in this document against the original `Cube.exe` image: its native instructions, decompiled listings and constant bytes. The image identity, the RVAs, the health and death gates, the fatal order, the regional, relationship, credit and grant stages, the backing-record writes, the loot gates, the random-draw order and the adjustment arithmetic were confirmed. Several points were corrected. The synchronous call in hit construction is at `0x1986D0`, not `0x1986D2`. Runtime routine `0x28D946` is statically linked, not imported. The linked-creature credit in `0x1A0BF0` runs whenever world `+0xB4` is zero, not only for a local recipient. In the bonus branch the placement draws come before item generation. The currency and category-fourteen branches sit inside the byte-one-or-kind-`0x8F` gate. The material stage repeats its draw count, and a zero item ends it. Some details were added: the emitter's scale argument and record defaults, the selector-six exclusion, the level-threshold formula, and the 64-bit difference direction. Decompiler-oriented wording was rewritten as plain prose, and the limits were moved into an explicit unresolved section. What remains open is listed in that section: nested item and material generators, terrain search termination, maximum health, inventory policy, random and conversion runtime behavior, and exceptional-input safety.
