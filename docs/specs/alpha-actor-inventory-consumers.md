# Original actor inventory consumers

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD. This independent, code-free contract covers the health-item branch preceding target selection in client RVA `0x02CB20`, the talk/vendor inventory copies in `0x0889E0` and `0x0A2300`, and the bounded fatal inventory enumeration in `0x19B180`. It identifies the distinct descriptor-to-actor copy boundary. It does not claim a complete inventory, vendor, target-selection, loot, or player-interface system.

The supported original is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, preferred image base `0x00400000`. Paths below are relative to `Documents/disassembled alpha cube world`. Evidence is the original image, `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, and the individually cited original function exports. Native instructions were checked for admissions, item-copy coverage, quantity mutations, and publication order. Earlier held prose, reconstruction, replacement code, runtime observations, and implementation history are not evidence.

## Storage identity and copy coverage

Actor `+0x11DC/+0x11E0/+0x11E4` are the three 32-bit pointers of an outer contiguous group collection: begin, logical end, and capacity end. Each group descriptor is 12 bytes and similarly describes an inner contiguous entry collection. Each entry is `0x11C` bytes: a 32-bit quantity followed by an item occupying `0x118` bytes. The consumers below choose groups by traversal or copy their existing order; they do not establish symbolic names for individual group indices. Actor `+0x11E8` is a separate 32-bit value, actor `+0x11EC` a separate item, and `+0x1304/+0x1308` two further 32-bit values. The talk copies handle those members separately from the group collection. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02CC06–0x02CC40`, `0x088AD2–0x088B4D`, `0x0A2365–0x0A23AB`; `decompiled/client/functions/0044ad30.c`, `0044aa20.c`, `0044aff0.c`.]

Group assignment RVA `0x04AD30` preserves the source's logical group count and group order. Reached inner assignment/construction services preserve each group's logical entry count and entry order. They copy every entry quantity as its raw 32-bit word, without a positive-count, nonzero-count, category, or item-equality filter. Empty groups remain part of the logical group sequence. A source with no groups empties the destination's logical group collection; self-assignment is a no-op. Destination capacity and allocation addresses can be reused or replaced and are not promised equal to the source's capacity or addresses. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x04AD30–0x04AEE2`, `0x04AA20–0x04AB8F`, `0x04A700–0x04A79C`; `decompiled/client/functions/00449cb0.c`, `0044a410.c`, `0044a700.c`.]

The item assignment at RVA `0x02C5E0` and item copy construction at `0x00EE70` do **not** copy all `0x118` item bytes indiscriminately. Both write item-relative bytes `+0/+1`, bytes `+4` through `+0x0E`, the 16-bit word `+0x10`, bytes `+0x14` through `+0x113`, and the 32-bit word `+0x114`. Bytes `+2/+3/+0x0F/+0x12/+0x13` are not written by these item-copy bodies. Their meaning is not established here. Existing destination contents at those five bytes are retained by assignment; new allocation does not establish zero defaults for them. Entry range copying first stores the quantity, then invokes the item copy for entry `+4`, then advances by `0x11C`. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02C5E0–0x02C672`, `0x00EE70–0x00EF10`, `0x04A3C0–0x04A400`, `0x04A4B0–0x04A4F6`; `decompiled/client/functions/0044a600.c`.]

## Health-item consumption before target selection

RVA `0x02CB20` receives a supplied actor as its first stack argument, world as its second, elapsed as its third, and an output aggregate as its fourth; its receiver is a separate behavior object. At entry it clears actor's 16-bit `+0x124` mask 4. Before considering inventory it can normalize actor action byte `+0x68` to zero: this occurs when that byte is none of `0x53/0x52/0x54/0x4F` and signed actor counter `+0x6C` is strictly greater than 10000. It then bypasses inventory/target work when 32-bit actor `+0x128` is nonzero, or when the resulting action is `0x50` and signed `+0x6C` is below 3000. These ordered entry effects are not a health-item-specific eligibility service. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02CB60–0x02CBCC`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042cb20.c`.]

The inventory scan additionally requires actor binary32 health `+0x16C` to be ordered strictly below one quarter of the maximum-health result. Health is read and saved before the maximum helper call. The maximum helper `0x044DB0` is called once for this test; its return is narrowed to binary32 before multiplication by binary32 `0.25`, bits `0x3E800000` at RVA `0x345D98`. Unordered comparison skips this scan if execution continues. The actor's signed `+0x6C` must also be strictly greater than the signed result of action-duration helper `0x03D1A0`. The maximum and duration helper internals are unclosed dependencies of this contract. No actor role-byte filter occurs inside this health-item scan. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02CBCC–0x02CC06`.]

The scan visits **all existing groups** from outer begin to end in increasing 12-byte address order, and each group's entries from begin to end in increasing `0x11C` address order. Empty groups are skipped by pointer equality. It captures the outer end pointer at scan entry and each inner end pointer when entering that group. It does not select a fixed group index or derive a group from item category. Within an entry it requires only quantity **not equal to zero**, item byte `+0` exactly 1, and item byte `+1` exactly 1. The first such entry in this nested order wins. There is no call to full item equality RVA `0x02F4A0`, no seed/material/rank comparison, and no earlier signed-positive quantity requirement. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02CC06–0x02CC40`, `0x02CC8D–0x02CC97`.]

On that first match, the synchronous order is actor action byte `+0x68` set to `0x50`, actor 32-bit counter `+0x6C` set to zero, selected item assigned to actor item `+0x1E8` through `0x02C5E0`, then selected entry quantity decremented by one. The snapshot is therefore made before decrement or exhaustion clearing, and contains the item fields covered by the assignment service, not the entry's quantity word. If the post-decrement quantity is signed at or below zero, the branch stores quantity zero and clears only item bytes `+0/+1`. It does not erase or compact the entry and does not clear the remaining source item bytes. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02CC8D–0x02CCBB`.]

The decrement is raw 32-bit arithmetic followed by a signed comparison. Consequently, nonzero negative quantities are admitted by the match test; they do not receive a preliminary clamp. A post-decrement nonpositive result is normalized as above, while the raw minimum signed value wraps to the maximum signed value and passes the post-decrement positive test. No broader malformed-count policy is inferred from this particular branch.

After mutation, the branch makes two successive calls through RNG import IAT RVA `0x2FC468`. Each returned signed 32-bit integer is converted to binary32, multiplied by binary32 four, divided by binary32 32767, then reduced by binary32 two. Those constants are bits `0x40800000`, `0x46FFFE00`, and `0x40000000` at RVAs `0x345E60`, `0x2FD338`, and `0x345E10`. The first calculation is retained across the second draw. Stores occur to steering `+0x40` from the first result, `+0x48` zero, then `+0x44` from the second result. The branch jumps to an AL-true return and does not proceed to the target-record lookup at `0x02CC42`. It does not itself increase health. Imported RNG behavior, preceding helper effects, and the later action/healing consumer are outside this bounded branch. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02CCBB–0x02CD20`, `0x02EEBE–0x02EEE3`.]

One independently confirmed world caller is indirect through actor `+0x13E4`. After the world's null/health admission, actor role byte `+0x60` must be nonzero and world byte `+0xB4` zero to reach its behavior accumulator work. The world adds the original elapsed word to actor signed 32-bit `+0x13C4`. Actor `+0x12C` must be signed at or below zero and the accumulated result at least 32 to enter the call opportunity. With `+0x13E4` nonnull, it supplies actor, world, that accumulated elapsed value, and the aggregate to the object's first virtual method. At that threshold the accumulator is then reset to zero, including when the pointer was null and no call occurred; a below-threshold value is retained. A positive `+0x12C` bypasses that call opportunity and reset. This is not a promise of a call on every world invocation or a positive-elapsed check. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x211AD1–0x211B57`.]

The original sequential behavior service RVA `0x0DC390` traverses its child list through next links and invokes each child's first virtual method until one returns true. The original behavior constructor `0x02C550` installs a table whose first method is `0x02CB20`; native table RVA `0x2FD310` establishes that link. The reached world creation route adds such an object to a sequential behavior collection. Thus a preceding child can prevent this consumer from being reached. Selection, ownership, replacement, and freshness of every behavior collection are not closed here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x0DC390–0x0DC3FA`, `0x02C550–0x02C57D`, `0x210223–0x2102A7`; sequential table RVA `0x303A00`; `decompiled/client/functions/004dc390.c`.]

## Talk/vendor actor-to-client copies

Client helper RVA `0x0889E0` looks up an eight-byte key at client `+0x800A70` in the embedded world's creature map at client `+0x2E8`, through lookup service `0x077D90`. An absent entry produces a null selected actor; a present entry supplies its mapped actor pointer from map node `+0x18`. The helper stores that pointer into client `+0x8008D8` before examining it. The map key and mapped actor pointer are distinct inputs; this helper does not compare the key with the actor's own identifier. A null selected actor takes another branch and performs no actor-inventory export here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x0889E8–0x088A24`; `decompiled/client/functions/004889e0.c`, `00477d90.c`.]

A nonnull selected actor reaches inventory export only when actor byte `+0x140` is exactly `0x80`, `0x81`, or `0x82`. The inventory export branch does not additionally test the selected actor's role byte `+0x60`, health, quantity totals, position, or distance. Kind byte `0x83` takes a separate early-return interface branch, and byte `0x89` reaches a separate later interface activation without satisfying this three-value inventory gate. Symbolic profession or group names are not assigned from those bytes. Upstream production and validation of the selected key remain unclosed. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x088A61–0x088B76`.]

For the admitted three values, the helper first calls refresh service `0x0A2300`, then writes two interface activation words to one, and sets the 32-bit mode field of the object at client `+0x80095C`, offset `+0x1B4`, to zero. It next assigns actor group collection `+0x11DC` to client buffer `+0x800C0C` through `0x04AD30`. After that assignment it copies actor `+0x11E8` to client `+0x800C18`, assigns actor item `+0x11EC` to client `+0x800C1C` through `0x02C5E0`, then copies actor `+0x1304` and `+0x1308` to client `+0x800D34` and `+0x800D38`. The source is the selected mapped actor, with its current existing number of groups; there is no forced four-group layout or entry-count filtering in this path. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x088AC4–0x088B53`.]

The original frame call at RVA `0x0973E6` has its own immediate gates: client byte `+0x800A40` zero, client byte `+0x0F` nonzero, global byte at RVA `0x36B138` zero, and the comparison of zero with the local player actor's health must take the below branch. Under masked exceptions that last condition admits ordered positive health and unordered health. Ordered nonpositive health takes a different branch. The player pointer is read from client `+0x8006D0`; these are caller-side local-player gates, not a health or ownership test of the selected vendor actor. Earlier frame admission, input meaning of the bytes, and interaction-distance production are outside this contract. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x09735F–0x0973EB`, within frame routine `0x088EE0`.]

Refresh RVA `0x0A2300` separately tests the interface mode field against exactly 1. When it is not 1 and cached actor pointer client `+0x8008D8` is nonnull, it performs the same group assignment and separate scalar/item/scalar copies, in the same order described above. It does not repeat the three-value actor `+0x140` admission or re-look up the map key. A null cached pointer leaves these buffer values unchanged by that branch. Both the actor-copy route and its bypass still reach the two subsequent interface-update services `0x0C6350` and `0x0C64C0`. The body does not copy any edited buffer contents back to the actor. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x0A2304–0x0A2314`, `0x0A2365–0x0A23C5`; `decompiled/client/functions/004a2300.c`.]

Mode exactly 1 takes a different source route: it resizes the client buffer to two groups, empties group index 1 by setting its logical end to its begin, and appends entries from the client collection `+0x800D3C` into that group, from the header's previous link backward until its first node has been included. Append service `0x086700` copies each entry quantity and copy-constructs its item; it does not require positive quantity or a category match. This branch does not export the cached actor's inventory. The collection's producer and interface meaning are unclosed. In `0x0889E0`, this preliminary refresh occurs before the mode is forced to zero and before the later explicit selected-actor copy. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x0A2314–0x0A2363`, `0x086700–0x086775`; `decompiled/client/functions/00487380.c`, `00486700.c`.]

These are client buffer exports through element-copy services, rather than raw pointer sharing with actor entry storage. They retain zero quantities, negative raw quantities, category-zero entries, and empty groups in ordinary well-formed input. The element and member-copy sequences themselves perform no item consumption, stock decrement, purchase payment, network packet construction, random draw, or actor-inventory mutation. The subsequent interface services and later transaction consumers are not closed by those facts. Allocation/error handling, concurrent buffer refresh, cached actor lifetime, and atomic publication of the whole copied state remain unclosed.

## Descriptor production is a separate boundary

Original world creation supplies actor constructor `0x03B690` with an identifier address from a separate descriptor base, then retains the newly constructed actor pointer. Later on that reached route, it passes descriptor `+0xF6C` as source and actor `+0x11DC` as destination to inventory member assignment `0x04AFF0`. That wrapper assigns the groups through `0x04AD30`, then the source-relative `+0x0C` scalar, item `+0x10`, and scalars `+0x128/+0x12C`. The descriptor's `+0xF6C` is therefore a producer-side inventory object copied into a different actor object; it is not the actor consumer offset or a player-interface buffer. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20FFD5–0x20FFE2`, `0x2101F0–0x2101F6`, `0x2103A6–0x2103BF`, `0x04AFF0–0x04B02F`; `decompiled/client/functions/0044aff0.c`.]

The descriptor creation, item generators, group categorization, and all conditions admitting this world creation route are not closed here. Neither this copy nor a repeated numeric offset establishes that every actor has the same descriptor ancestry, group count, contents, or freshness. Received/constructed actors and player-interface consumers require their own producer and caller evidence.

## Bounded fatal inventory enumeration

The inventory tail of original helper RVA `0x19B180` is reached only for actor role byte `+0x60` exactly 1. Its earlier function admission also rejects actor `+0x7E` 16-bit mask `0x0800`; roles zero and three are earlier rejected as well. This identifies a reached fatal/drop consumer, not the complete fatal-call admission or the complete loot routine. Earlier generated-item and RNG branches in that routine remain outside this bounded inventory tail. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19B1C0–0x19B1E8`, `0x19C163–0x19C19D`; `decompiled/client/functions/0059b180.c`.]

The tail requires a nonempty outer begin/end range and a signed positive group count computed as its byte span divided by 12. It visits groups in increasing index order and entries through increasing `0x11C` addresses. Empty inner ranges are skipped. An entry must have item category byte nonzero and signed quantity strictly positive; the explicit nonzero-quantity check is followed by that signed-positive check. It then advances a local unit counter from zero while it is signed below the entry's current quantity, rereading that quantity at the loop test. Unlike the health-item scan, negative quantities produce no per-unit requests here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19C16D–0x19C200`, `0x19C323–0x19C39F`.]

For each such unit, category 1 plus item byte `+1` equal to 1 suppresses drop submission and the scatter draws for that unit. Other admitted items make two direct RNG calls for horizontal scatter, construct a three-component raw position relative to the actor with zero third-axis scatter, then make a further direct RNG call for a supplied scalar before calling world helper `0x19AEB0`. The source item pointer is entry `+4`, passed again on every submitted unit in group/entry/unit order; the entry's whole quantity word is not passed as one batched item count. The enumeration itself does not decrement quantities, clear item bytes, erase entries, or empty groups. The excluded item still advances the local unit counter. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19C200–0x19C33E`.]

Submission is not an unconditional guarantee of a published drop. Reached helper `0x19AEB0` constructs private drop-record storage, copies the supplied position, and makes its own first RNG call before resident chunk lookup. Only a successful resident lookup reaches source-item assignment through `0x02C5E0`, followed by another RNG call. Terrain scanning then precedes append service `0x04D590` and output service `0x18E7D0`. A missing initial chunk therefore bypasses the item copy and publication after that first helper-local draw. The item assignment preserves the same copy coverage stated above. The complete drop record, final terrain position, nested publication behavior, output ownership/transport, generator branches, and total RNG sequence of this fatal routine are expressly unclosed. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19AED8–0x19AFBF`, `0x19B11F–0x19B163`; `decompiled/client/functions/0059aeb0.c`.]

## Validity and scope limits

All ordinary traversal and copy conclusions require valid group/entry pointers, correctly aligned ranges, representable collection lengths, successful reached allocation services, and resident actor pointers. Pointer-inequality loops are not bounds validators for reversed or malformed ranges. Neither the health scan nor the talk copies establishes a lock, lifetime pin, atomic quantity/item snapshot, or protection against concurrent storage replacement. Item-copy omissions are explicit and must not be replaced by an invented full-byte or all-zero contract.

The health branch's threshold/duration dependencies, full target scoring, all descriptor and received-state producers, player inventory widgets, transaction rules, inventory-add/stacking rules, fatal item generators, imported RNG internals, and server behavior remain outside this audit. The three established consumers use different quantity admissions and ordering rules; no general inventory-wide count policy or symbolic group meaning follows from them.
