# Alpha character creation facing and camera height

**FINAL / STABLE / HELD**

This contract covers the controlled actor's ordinary pose and camera when the native creator opens. Evidence paths are relative to the original Alpha evidence directory. Executable addresses are RVAs. The supported `binary/Cube.exe` is the 32-bit x86 image with preferred base `0x400000`, timestamp `0x51EA955E`, image size `0x3BC000`, and SHA-256 `84A7A132A84D4282338E7EA45A1940D32066D64E39CBAFED8F2418D5A6DC30BF`.

## Actor facing and pose

The controlled actor's logical X, Y and Z rotation angles are the three binary32 fields at actor `+0x28`, `+0x2C` and `+0x30`, in degrees. Construction zeroes them and the client startup explicitly sets them to positive zero again. Creator admission calls the actor reset at `0x046330`, whose payload reset at `0x0465C0` zeroes the same three angles. Admission explicitly writes positive zero to them once more. It also clears the separate movement vector at actor `+0x34` through `+0x3C`. The reset leaves the live actor position in place. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x03B6CB–0x03B6D6`, `0x03C12C–0x03C16B`, `0x06205E–0x0620C6`, `0x046338–0x04633F`, `0x0465EF–0x046619`, `0x083F73–0x083FC5`]

The actor constructor also starts the displayed angle triple at actor `+0x1374` through `+0x137C` at zero. The creator reset clears the display interpolation timer at actor `+0x1398`. The ordinary simulation uses that zero-timer state to copy the live actor position and logical angles into the drawable position and angles at `+0x1350` and `+0x1374`. The actor body renderer uses the displayed angles as its world X, Y and Z rotations. Zero yaw leaves the native horizontal axes unrotated. An explicit heading-relative direction in actor processing is the positive Y unit vector rotated by the logical Z angle, so this direction is positive Y at zero yaw. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x03B836–0x03B88F`, `0x04646B`, `0x21FB80–0x21FB87`, `0x21FD36–0x21FD53`, `0x01F439–0x01F4C7`, `0x21D7AC–0x21D80C`]

While the creator is active, its dedicated update checks the name and control state, and its appearance selector updates the character model. The traced creator-specific update and appearance callback contain no automatic replacement for the reset facing. The active creator bypasses the regular gameplay input call at `0x0982B9`. General actor simulation can still update the pose before drawing. For example it interpolates the logical Y angle in its physical actor update. The reset angles specify the initial pose and heading input, rather than the final placement of every articulated part after animation. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x0896AF–0x0899A6`, `0x02C080–0x02C3F8`, `0x098259–0x0982CB`, `0x2191C0–0x21920A`]

## Vertical correction and camera focus

Actor `+0x1180` is a binary32 vertical correction in world units. Its constructor value is positive zero. The creator reset clears nearby motion and presentation fields while retaining this live correction. Terrain collision can update the correction, including during a change of the actor's supported elevation, and the ordinary simulation damps it toward zero. The per-step decay coefficient is binary32 0.01 for a negative value and 0.02 for a nonnegative value. The body renderer also subtracts this correction from the drawable vertical position before applying other local pose offsets. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x03B6FF–0x03B731`, `0x04644D–0x046488`, `0x21CBD3–0x21CC1E`, `0x21D4E0–0x21D583`, `0x21F081–0x21F0C8`, `0x01F17D–0x01F1B8`]

The creator's desired camera focus is the live actor X and Y, and the actor's live Z minus the `+0x1180` correction. Actor position starts at `+0x10`; its three components have signed 64-bit storage in units of 1/65,536. Native subtraction converts the correction to those same units before subtracting it from actor Z. A zero correction puts the desired focus at the actor's position origin. This creator branch supplies no additional eye-height or half-body-height term. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x09C44A–0x09C50C`, `0x09C682–0x09C6BB`, `0x09C60A–0x09C621`, `0x012220–0x012255`, binary32 scale at `0x2FCE7C`]

The current focus lives at client `+0x158` and survives the entry callback. Before tracking, the client compares its binary32 squared distance from the actor's uncorrected position to 2,500. A value strictly above 2,500 replaces the current focus with that uncorrected position. The creator then advances the current focus toward the desired one. Horizontal components use a per-millisecond approach coefficient of 0.025, and the vertical component uses 0.005. The previous start or selection camera can leave a terrain-relative focus 50 world units above its selected terrain height. The creator's first update can therefore carry over a vertical difference even when the actor correction is zero. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x083EFE–0x08414F`, `0x09C367–0x09C442`, `0x024860`, binary32 threshold at `0x302ABC`, `0x09C60A–0x09C621`, `0x09C682–0x09C76A`, `0x0573D0`, `0x0AC150`, `0x0C860D–0x0C8633`]

The admitted camera angles are (90 degrees, 0 degrees, 0 degrees) and its requested distance is 5. The camera update rotates positive Z by those angles and subtracts the distance-scaled direction from the focus to form the camera position. Without terrain shortening or recovery, these settings place the eye five world units on the positive Y side of the current focus at its height in fixed-point coordinates. The resulting view is built in the client update and consumed by the ordinary scene renderer. A terrain hit can shorten the distance, and a blocked eye point can trigger position recovery relative to the actor. [`binary/Cube.exe` `0x083EFE–0x083F4E`, `0x09C2E6–0x09C367`, `0x0243D0`, `0x088E50`, `0x09C76F–0x09C802`, `0x09C95A–0x09CA7F`, `0x09CAE1–0x09CCF2`, `0x0AE3BA–0x0AE4AB`]

The exact first-present camera height depends on the preceding focus, elapsed update time, the live actor position and correction, and terrain clearance. The static trace does not establish an exact first visible animation frame or the painted silhouette of the unrotated model.
