# Alpha descriptor inventory storage and activation transfer

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD after complete author readback.

This contract covers the original descriptor inventory object beginning at descriptor `+0xF6C`, its ordinary value assignment to a newly activated actor, and bounded constructor, reset and entry-production relations. The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`, image base `0x00400000`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`. Evidence paths are relative to `disassembled alpha cube world`; bare function filenames below are in `decompiled/client/functions`. Native instruction entries in the cited regions were compared with that image and `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`. No prior contract, reconstruction, replacement implementation or runtime observation supplies evidence here.

## Storage and destination identity

Let D be the descriptor and A the newly created actor in world routine RVA `0x20C510`. The activation passes D `+0xF6C` as the source and A `+0x11DC` as the destination of assignment helper `0x04AFF0` at call site `0x2103B3`. The inventory object's established extent is `0x130` bytes. Descriptor-relative offsets therefore cannot be reused as actor-relative offsets. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x2103A6–0x2103BF`, helper `0x04AFF0–0x04B031`; original file `0044aff0.c`.]

| Inventory-relative member | Descriptor storage | Actor storage | Established representation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `+0/+4/+8` | `+0xF6C/+0xF70/+0xF74` | `+0x11DC/+0x11E0/+0x11E4` | Three 32-bit pointers delimiting the outer collection's beginning, logical end and allocation end. |
| `+0x0C` | `+0xF78` | `+0x11E8` | An additional full 32-bit value, copied separately; its broader selection/UI meaning is unclosed. |
| `+0x10..+0x127` | `+0xF7C..+0x1093` | `+0x11EC..+0x1303` | A separate `0x118`-byte item value, outside the nested collections. |
| `+0x128` | `+0x1094` | `+0x1304` | Full 32-bit accumulator with the concrete item-type-12 additions described below. |
| `+0x12C` | `+0x1098` | `+0x1308` | Full 32-bit accumulator with the concrete item-type-13 additions described below. |

The outer collection contains consecutive `0x0C`-byte group headers. Each header in turn contains three 32-bit beginning/end/allocation-end pointers for a consecutive inner collection. The inner element size is `0x11C` bytes: a full 32-bit entry count at element `+0`, followed by the `0x118`-byte item at element `+4`. The entry count and the item's own 16-bit member at item `+0x10` are distinct values. Vector lengths are determined from the pointer extents, divided by `0x0C` or `0x11C`; they are not determined by summing entry counts. [Evidence: helpers `0x04AD30`, `0x04AA20`, `0x04A3C0`, `0x04A600`; files `0044ad30.c`, `0044aa20.c`, `0044a3c0.c`, `0044a600.c`.]

The group ordinal is storage and routing state rather than a rule forcing every contained item to have a single kind. For an insertion request with signed full group argument -1, classifier `0x07FA30` reads the item's first byte and selects these ordinals: 0 for types 3 through 9, 23 and 24; 2 for type 11; 3 for types 19 and 20; and 1 for every other byte value. A concrete explicit nonnegative group argument bypasses that classifier. If needed, insertion grows the outer logical size to the selected ordinal plus one, creating empty intervening group headers. There is no universal four-group construction rule for the descriptor object. Arbitrary negative group arguments other than the recognized -1 selector are not established as safe inputs. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x06EC0A–0x06EC51`, `0x07FA30–0x07FA6D` and its image-resident dispatch tables at `0x07FA70/+0x07FA80`; resize `0x087380`, empty-header construction `0x027A80`.]

## Fresh values and bounded resets

Descriptor constructor `0x1ADB20` calls common inventory constructor `0x03C020` on D `+0xF6C`. Actor constructor `0x03B690` calls the same helper on A `+0x11DC`. The common helper initially makes all three outer pointers zero and stores zero at inventory `+0x0C`; there are initially no logical groups or inner entries from this helper. It initializes the separate item's copied fields to zero except for item word `+0x10`, which becomes 1. Its `0x100`-byte payload at item `+0x14` is zero-filled, its final full 32-bit member at item `+0x114` is zero, and both inventory accumulators are zero. The five item bytes omitted by the copy operation below are also omitted by this constructor's stores; a blanket zero-byte guarantee across the entire item is not established. [Evidence: files `005adb20.c`, `0043b690.c`, `0043c020.c`; native RVAs `0x1ADBC1–0x1ADC08`, `0x03B7A4–0x03B7F5`, `0x03C020–0x03C099`.]

Fresh inner-entry constructor `0x03C0A0` makes its full 32-bit count zero, initializes the same item fields with item word `+0x10` equal to 1, and zeroes the item payload and final 32-bit member. The corresponding fresh-entry range constructor `0x04A500` repeats those defaults at stride `0x11C`. An entry whose count is zero can therefore still contain a separately initialized item value; zero count is not evidence that every byte is zero. [Evidence: `0043c0a0.c`, `0044a500.c`; native RVAs `0x03C0A0–0x03C0F0`, `0x04A500–0x04A571`.]

Assignment from an empty source is a logical reset of the destination's nested collection. Outer assignment destroys the destination's former inner allocations and sets its outer logical end back to its beginning. An empty source inner collection similarly sets the matching destination group's logical end to its beginning. Capacity may be retained. These resets do not imply a zero fill of old capacity or item gaps. The top-level helper then separately replaces the additional scalar, separate item and both accumulators from the source. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x04AA50–0x04AA7B`, `0x04AD60–0x04AD9F`, `0x04AFFB–0x04B024`; inner-allocation cleanup `0x239CF0–0x239D2D`.]

Two reached actor reset blocks demonstrate a different policy from fresh construction: they construct a default inventory, assign its empty collection and other members to actor `+0x11DC`, then resize the actor's outer collection to four empty groups. One is in `0x04BE40`, at `0x04BEE1–0x04BF84`; another uses the pointer at client `+0x8006D0` in `0x0772B0`, at `0x0772FD–0x07739E`. The complete caller admission and any later population in these routines are outside this contract. Their explicit four-group step does not occur in the activation assignment at `0x2103B3`.

## Exact value-copy boundary

On an ordinary successful assignment between valid nonoverlapping objects, helper `0x04AFF0` first assigns the outer collection, then copies inventory `+0x0C` as 32 bits, then assigns the separate item at `+0x10`, then copies accumulator `+0x128`, then accumulator `+0x12C`, both as 32 bits. The outer and inner logical element counts and order are preserved. Existing destination storage can be reused; insufficient capacity can cause allocation and replacement. The source's heap pointers and capacities are not substituted as shared destination ownership. The copy is a value relation with separately owned nested storage, not an invocation of the entry insertion/classification helper. [Evidence: `0044aff0.c`, `0044ad30.c`, `0044aa20.c`, `0044a410.c`, `0044a700.c`.]

Outer groups are processed in increasing source order; entries within a group are copied in increasing source order. The helpers assign existing entries and construct any additional entries with the same semantic contents. An inner entry's full 32-bit count is written before its item copy. Excess destination entries disappear from the logical extent; excess destination groups and their inner allocations are removed. Self-assignment is admitted by the nested collection helpers without rebuilding those collections. Allocation failure, corrupted pointer extents, overlapping but distinct ranges and exception recovery are not covered by the ordinary relation. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x04AD30–0x04AEDF`, `0x04AA20–0x04AB8F`, entry ranges `0x04A3C0–0x04A400`, `0x04A4B0–0x04A4F6`, `0x04A600–0x04A644`.]

The item assignment at `0x02C5E0`, also used for existing inner entries, transfers the following item-relative fields in order. Its construction counterpart `0x00EE70` transfers the same extent and fields for newly created values.

| Item-relative field(s) | Transfer width/order |
| --- | --- |
| `+0`, then `+1` | One byte each. |
| `+4`, then `+8` | One full 32-bit value each. |
| `+0x0C`, `+0x0D`, `+0x0E` | One byte each, in that order. |
| `+0x10` | One 16-bit value. |
| `+0x14..+0x113` | All 32 consecutive eight-byte payload records, in increasing order; within each, its first four bytes are transferred individually in increasing order, followed by its last full 32 bits. |
| `+0x114` | One full 32-bit value after the payload. |

Item bytes `+2`, `+3`, `+0x0F`, `+0x12` and `+0x13` are not transferred. No meaning or persistence guarantee is assigned to those gaps. In an existing destination they can retain previous contents; an allocated destination does not gain a defined source value for them. Every one of the 32 payload records is copied regardless of the value at item `+0x114`; the operation does not copy only a used prefix, clamp that final member or treat it as an entry quantity. The helper preserves bits without numeric conversion. [Evidence: `0042c5e0.c`, `0040ee70.c`; native RVAs `0x02C5E0–0x02C674`, `0x00EE70–0x00EF12`.]

## Signed entry counts and bounded production

Activation assignment performs no count-positive test. Full counts zero, -1 or any other negative value survive the copy in place and in order, just as positive counts do. It does not discard zero entries, compact holes, combine matching items, recompute quantities from item word `+0x10`, sort groups or run automatic classification. A negative count is not made zero or treated as a vector length. This preservation statement concerns valid collection extents even when a contained signed count is zero or negative. It does not validate malformed collection pointers.

Entry producer `0x06EBE0` establishes why a counted entry can differ from an ordinary one-unit item. After group selection and any outer growth, it copies the input item into a temporary value. Its initial full signed increment is 1. For item first-byte types 12, 13, 21, 25, 20, 24 and 23, and for type 11 except sub-byte 14, the increment instead becomes the sign-extended 16-bit item word `+0x10`; the temporary item's word becomes 1. Type 0 has already returned without insertion. Every other admitted type retains increment 1 and retains its original item word. The original input item's word is not overwritten by this normalization of the temporary value. Thus an ordinary type-3 input with item word 5 produces a one-unit entry whose item word remains 5, while an ordinary type-11/sub-byte-12 input with item word 5 contributes count 5 with stored item word 1. These are distinct native quantities, without inferred UI names. [Evidence: `0046ebe0.c`; native RVAs `0x06EC1D–0x06ECA3`.]

This producer does not reject a zero or negative sign-extended increment. For the counted types, item word 0 can create or replace an entry with count 0; item word `0xFFFF` supplies signed increment -1. Count additions and accumulator additions use 32-bit wrapping arithmetic. No saturating maximum, lower clamp or automatic removal after a result reaches zero appears in the inspected insertion helper.

Before inner insertion, input type 12 with byte `+0x0D` equal to 10, 11 or 12 adds the increment to inventory accumulator `+0x128`, scaled respectively by 1, 10,000 or 100, and returns without creating an inner entry. Other type-12 byte values continue into inner insertion. Input type 13 adds the increment to inventory `+0x12C` and returns without an inner entry. Group growth precedes these accumulator-only returns. No currency or other gameplay name is needed to establish these field mutations. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x06ECA3–0x06ED12`.]

For inner insertion, the helper scans the selected group's existing entries in increasing order. It remembers the first entry whose count is exactly zero; negative counts are not remembered as free slots. For input types 1, 10, 11, 12, 13 or 21, an accepted item-identity comparison against an existing entry takes priority over remembered free slots and adds the increment to that entry's full count. This comparison is attempted without first requiring that existing count to be positive, so zero and negative existing counts remain eligible. With no matching entry, the first remembered zero slot has its item assigned before its count is set; if there is no zero slot, a newly constructed entry is appended with the temporary item and the increment. An exact-zero increment can therefore leave a populated item with zero count. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x06ED12–0x06EE9A`; first-byte admission `0x07F9F0–0x07FA0F`, comparison `0x02F4A0`.]

The equality dependency compares the item header fields and full member `+0x114`, then, only while that member is signed-positive, compares the first four bytes of each successive eight-byte payload record. It does not compare the item gaps or the payload record's last 32 bits. This is narrower than bytewise equality of the full copied extent. Ordinary item values with a payload count from 0 through 32 fit the established payload storage; larger positive counts and malformed item values are not made safe here. Nonpositive equal payload-count members bypass the payload loop. [Evidence: `0042f4a0.c`, `00428940.c`; native RVAs `0x02F4A0–0x02F564`, `0x028940–0x028968`.]

There are concrete descriptor-generation sites using both explicit routing and direct one-unit construction. At `0x1C9C59` and `0x1C9C80`, the reached prefix of `0x1C9B30` sends respectively type/sub-byte pairs 11/12 and 24/0, with item word 1, into descriptor `+0xF6C` using explicit group 0. This confirms that an explicit producer can place type 11 in group 0 even though automatic classification would select group 2. A later reached site at `0x1C9F3D` sends type/sub-byte 1/7 and a generated item word into the same explicit group 0; insertion still contributes a single unit independently of that word. This contract does not reconstruct the whole generator's admission, item distribution or total draw order.

A separate bounded replacement producer, `0x1C9F90`, is called with descriptor `+0xF6C`, for example at `0x1D2670`. It destroys the old inner allocations, resets the outer logical extent, resizes it to one group, then resizes that group's inner collection to ten entries. In increasing entry order it writes full count 1 before calling item generator `0x1F8750` once for that entry and assigning the returned item. On an ordinary completed call this gives ten entries of count 1 regardless of the generated items' internal words. It does not pass those items through `0x06EBE0` or run its counted-type normalization. The separate inventory item, `+0x0C` scalar and two accumulators are not reset by this collection-only replacement. The generator's numeric contents, internal random calls and caller-wide generation conditions remain outside this bounded relation. [Evidence: `005c9f90.c`, resize `0044d660.c`; native RVAs `0x1C9F90–0x1CA088`, call boundary `0x1D2651–0x1D2678`.]

## Activation order and amount-query boundary

The new actor's pointer has already been placed in the world actor map under the descriptor's full identifier at `0x21000C`, before the following inventory transfer. Earlier direct copies include descriptor equipment at `+0x120` into actor `+0x300` and the descriptor's five full 32-bit base values beginning at `+0xF58` into actor `+0x178..+0x188`. The inspected continuation then installs the reached behavior pointer at actor `+0x13E4`, calls four-byte collection assignment from D `+0x10AC` to A `+0x13E8`, copies D's full `+0x10B8` to A `+0x13F4`, and assigns D's four-byte collection at `+0x10BC` to A `+0x13F8`. Only after those configuration operations does it assign D's entire inventory value to A `+0x11DC`. This is an ordered series of mutations to an already published actor pointer, not proof of atomic publication or receiver synchronization. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x20FFF2–0x21000E`, `0x21005E–0x21009C`, `0x21036A–0x2103B8`.]

Immediately after inventory assignment returns, there is one direct call to amount/stat helper `0x044DB0` with A as receiver, at `0x2103BA`. Its returned x87 scalar is stored as binary32 at actor `+0x16C` at `0x2103BF`. This query is after configuration and inventory publication, and before the later descriptor-word mask-`0x0200` test and any resulting actor-component scaling at `0x2103CA`. The scoped caller has no intervening second inventory assignment or second amount query at this boundary. It does not rerun the query after that later scaling. [Evidence: native RVAs `0x210376–0x2103E3`.]

The query's direct actor reads use the copied full `+0x190`, byte `+0x1A8`, byte `+0x60`, base binary32 member `+0x178`, class/state bytes `+0x140/+0x141`, and equipment values. Its possible item-stat helper calls are checked in order at actor item offsets `+0x990`, `+0xAA8`, `+0x530`, `+0x648`, `+0x760`, then `+0x878`, admitted by first-byte values 3, 3, 4, 6, 5 and 7 respectively. Its direct body does not iterate the newly assigned inventory groups or test their entry counts. The complete stat formula, imported power routine behavior and transitive item-stat calculations are not reconstructed here; transfer ordering alone must not be expanded into a claim that positive inventory counts contribute to this amount. [Evidence: `00444db0.c`; native RVAs `0x044DB0–0x044FDE`.]

## Persistence, random boundaries and limits

The activation copy leaves D's source inventory logically intact. It does not transfer ownership of D's nested allocations, consume its counts or reset its standalone item/counters. Later edits to independently owned actor entries do not, by this copy relation alone, edit descriptor entries. Every copied full count is retained even if an earlier descriptor-item refresh skipped a zero count or admitted a negative one; this assignment itself has no such item-refresh admission test.

The inspected inventory constructor, semantic item/entry copy helpers, group classifier, identity comparison and insertion helper contain no direct `rand` or `srand` call. The activation's earlier optional descriptor refresh, the wider actor constructor/setup and the concrete item generators are separate random boundaries; no total activation or generation draw count is asserted here. In particular, `0x1C9F90` reaches its unclosed item generator once per ordinary completed entry after that entry's count store.

This contract assumes ordinary valid object and vector storage, completed allocation/copy calls and the supported original client image. It does not establish exhaustive descriptor inventory producers, every actor reset caller, full world generation or loot distributions, UI/keyboard meanings of group/scalar fields, item payload semantics, packet formats, short-record recovery, exceptional allocation/unwinding behavior, concurrent visibility or a server counterpart. Numeric gaps and opaque fields retain the limits stated above; no unsupported field name or count repair is inferred.
