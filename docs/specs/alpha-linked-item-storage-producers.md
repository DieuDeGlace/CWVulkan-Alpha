# Linked-item storage and snapshot producers

This is an independent, bounded original-client contract. The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`, with base `0x00400000`. All routine addresses below are RVAs. Relative evidence paths refer to the original Alpha evidence root. Original native instructions were checked directly, including receive and world-update blocks missing from exported function bodies. No replacement implementation, runtime observation or earlier project contract supplies evidence here.

For the actor resolved from a credit record's first identifier, `+0x1020` is an inline item in its thirteen-item state block: zero-based index **12**, the last item, with stride `0x118`. The destination `+0x1D48` is a separate inline item in that **same actor**, outside that state block. It is a retained comparison snapshot, not an inventory entry, a pointer to the linked actor's item, or a field of the credit record. The snapshot's adjacent 32-bit comparison value is actor `+0x1D44`.

## Storage identity, construction and value transfer

Creature construction RVA `0x03B690` initializes its state beginning at actor `+0x10` through RVA `0x03C100`. That state initializer calls item-block constructor `0x03BC00` at state `+0x2F0`, therefore actor `+0x300`. The block contains thirteen `0x118`-byte items. Item-block assignment RVA `0x04AF00` explicitly visits offsets zero through `+0xD20` in that stride, making actor `+0x1020` index 12. It spans actor `+0x1020–+0x1137`. Actor `+0x1138` begins a separate eleven-word block and is not part of this item. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0043b690.c`, `0043c100.c`, `0043bc00.c`, `0044af00.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x03B6CB–0x03B6D1`, `0x03C33B–0x03C341`.]

Construction gives each item category/subtype bytes zero, 32-bit words at item `+0x04/+0x08` zero, bytes `+0x0C/+0x0D/+0x0E` zero, the 16-bit field at `+0x10` the value 1, all 256 bytes at `+0x14–+0x113` zero, and the final 32-bit field at `+0x114` zero. The item constructor RVA `0x02F3E0` establishes the same defaults when used separately. Alignment gaps at `+0x02–+0x03`, `+0x0F` and `+0x12–+0x13` have no all-zero guarantee from these field initializations. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0043bc00.c`, `0042f3e0.c`.]

The actor constructor separately sets `+0x1D44` to zero and constructs the item at `+0x1D48` with those same semantic defaults. The corresponding snapshot level word at actor `+0x1D58` is 1, its final word at `+0x1E5C` is zero, and its 256-byte body begins at `+0x1D5C`. This item ends at `+0x1E5F`, within the `0x1E60`-byte creature allocation. It has value storage in the actor, rather than a separately allocated backing item. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x03BB81–0x03BBDC`; `decompiled/client/functions/0043b690.c`; allocation example RVAs `0x06BC02–0x06BC26`.]

Item assignment RVA `0x02C5E0` transfers, in order, bytes `+0x00/+0x01`, 32-bit words `+0x04/+0x08`, bytes `+0x0C/+0x0D/+0x0E`, the 16-bit word `+0x10`, all thirty-two eight-byte entries at `+0x14`, then the final 32-bit word `+0x114`. The entry body is copied in full regardless of that final word's value. The alignment gaps listed above are skipped. Constructor-copy helper `0x00EE70` transfers the same fields. These operations preserve supplied bits; they do not normalize category, derive an inventory index, recompute experience or allocate an item payload. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0042c5e0.c`, `0040ee70.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02C5E0–0x02C672`.]

The retained item is compared through RVA `0x0688F0`, which negates item equality `0x02F4A0`. Equality checks category and subtype, `+0x04`, byte `+0x0E`, `+0x08`, byte `+0x0C`, word `+0x10`, byte `+0x0D`, and `+0x114`, in that order. When the common signed 32-bit `+0x114` value is positive, it then checks that many entries: the fourth byte of each eight-byte entry, followed by its first three bytes through RVA `0x028940`. It does not compare the entry's last four bytes, inactive entries beyond the count, or alignment gaps. Nonpositive counts perform no entry loop. No count-at-most-32 validation occurs in this comparator; larger positive counts are outside the established valid-item boundary. Snapshot assignment and snapshot equality therefore cover different byte sets. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/004688f0.c`, `0042f4a0.c`, `00428940.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02F4A0–0x02F562`.]

## State assignment and reset producers

State assignment RVA `0x04B040`, when applied to actor `+0x10`, assigns the thirteen-item block through `0x04AF00`, including index 12. State constructor-copy RVA `0x066050` similarly invokes the thirteen-item constructor-copy helper `0x059B10`. These state operations also transfer the eleven 32-bit words beginning at actor `+0x1138`. They do not extend to actor `+0x1D44/+0x1D48`. Assigning a new state can thus replace the current item and its accompanying comparison word without replacing the retained snapshot. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0044b040.c`, `00466050.c`, `00459b10.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x04B355–0x04B376`.]

The kind-0 receive route supplies a concrete lifetime boundary. It constructs an absent actor with the ordinary `0x1E60`-byte constructor, takes a temporary copy of actor state at `+0x10`, decodes into that temporary, and, after successful decoding, assigns it back through `0x04B040` for an actor that differs from client `+0x8006D0`. The temporary copy at RVA `0x06BC62` is from the actor into temporary state; it is not an incoming snapshot assignment. The subsequent assignment is at RVA `0x06BDFF`. The retained item remains outside those state copies. Packet-mask decoding, every malformed-input route and broader receive admission are not closed here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x06BBFE–0x06BC62`, `0x06BC74–0x06BCCB`, `0x06BDF2–0x06BE04`.]

Ordinary descriptor admission is another source: world-update RVAs `0x21005E–0x21006B` assign the thirteen items from descriptor `+0x120` to actor `+0x300`. The source for actor index 12 is therefore descriptor `+0xE40`. This proves a copy origin, not that every generated descriptor has a nonempty item there. Descriptor constructor `0x1ADB20` also uses the thirteen-item initializer. Nested generation of particular descriptor items remains outside scope. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005adb20.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, cited world-update instructions.]

Reset differs from construction. Actor reset RVA `0x046330` calls state reset `0x0465C0` at actor `+0x10`; the latter clears the entire `0xE38`-byte item block with zero bytes. Index 12 then has a zero level word, rather than the constructor's 1. The established reset routine does not explicitly initialize `+0x1D44` or reconstruct `+0x1D48`. It must not be treated as a fresh actor constructor or an unconditional snapshot invalidation. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/00446330.c`, `004465c0.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x04662E` onward, item-block clear.]

The original stream writer transfers the complete `0x118` bytes beginning at actor `+0x1020`; its reader assigns those bytes when the input extent fits. These are raw-span transfers, so they include the item's alignment gaps, unlike field-wise item assignment. The established stream sequence does not serialize the retained item by treating it as another member of the thirteen-item block. Full-format admission, durability and every transport/storage consumer remain outside this contract. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0044d790.c`, `0044be40.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x04DBC9–0x04DBDB`, `0x04C425–0x04C432`.]

## Local item replacement and member writers

The original equipment-selection helper `0x07B1B0` can return local actor `+0x1020` as an item address. The input routine `0x07B600` then has concrete replacement routes for it. Here the native item categories are **`0x13` and `0x14`**, decimal 19 and 20; neither is decimal category 13.

- At RVAs `0x07D1FC–0x07D277`, an admitted inventory item with category `0x13`, or category `0x14` unequal to the current item, is copied temporarily. The old current item is first passed to inventory-add helper `0x06EBE0`, the temporary replaces actor `+0x1020`, then the inventory entry count is decremented. Counts falling to zero or below are clamped to zero and that entry's category/subtype are cleared. This path does not update the retained item.
- The equipment-address route at RVAs `0x07C0DC–0x07C190` accepts the selected address only when it is local actor `+0x1020` and the carried count is zero or its item category is `0x13/0x14`. A signed carried count at most one, or a non-`0x14` carried item, takes the swap at `0x07C4A0–0x07C4CC`. A carried `0x14` stack greater than one takes a one-item replacement only when unequal to the current item; a nonempty old current item is returned through inventory-add before replacement and carried-count decrement. The snapshot is not assigned in these shown sequences.

[Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0047b1b0.c`, `0047b600.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, cited ranges. Upstream input dispatch and every UI restriction are not reconstructed here.]

A specialized interaction in the enclosing client routine also produces a fresh item in this storage. After its preceding identity/state checks, it requires the current item category `0x14`, locates a resident record with selector 7 whose amount is at least binary32 1 and whose embedded owner identifier equals the local actor's own eight-byte ID. The admitted route constructs an empty item, assigns it to actor `+0x1020`, writes the selected creature's own ID into the local actor's forward identifier `+0x11C8`, then writes item category `0x13`, the low byte of the creature's 32-bit kind as subtype, the low 16 level bits into item `+0x10`, and its full experience word into item `+0x04`. No retained-item copy appears in that construction sequence. The wider interaction and resident-record producer are bounded dependencies. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x094A97–0x094BFA`.]

There are direct member writers as well. Near the start of world update, when world `+0xB8` is nonnull, category `0x13` with subtype `0x18` changes to subtype `0x19` if the signed item level word is greater than 1. This precedes the later comparison/refresh pass. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20C8AB–0x20C8DA`.]

The original name-input route accepts a text length below 16 and current category `0x13`. It stores that 32-bit length at item `+0x114`; for each character, it stores the low byte of its UTF-16 code unit in the fourth byte of the corresponding eight-byte body entry and zeroes that entry's first three bytes. It does not clear later inactive entries or assign the retained item. The original rejection text is `Pet name too long`, supporting the native UI use of this encoding as a pet name. The writes are enclosed by the enter/leave wrappers called with client `+0x2E4`; those wrappers address client `+0x8003A4`. This protection does not establish synchronization of every other writer. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/0047e1b0.c`, `00601cb0.c`, `00601e90.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x07E57B–0x07E604`.]

## World comparison and retained-item publication

World RVA `0x20C510` gathers nonnull actors with role byte `+0x60` equal to zero into a temporary list at RVAs `0x20CA3D–0x20CA50`. There is no positive-health test at that list insertion. Earlier work can already have zeroed an actor's health before insertion. The comparison/refresh pass described here uses that selected list, rather than treating every resident actor as eligible.

This pass runs only on the world `+0xB4 == 0` route. It is additionally skipped when helper `0x077220`, called on world `+0x94`, reports its word at relative `+0x10` zero: the concrete tested world word is `+0xA4`. No symbolic meaning is assigned to that word here. Within the pass, only current item category `0x13` reaches retained-item comparison and publication. Other categories take link-removal handling and do not clear or replace the snapshot on that route. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20DDE5–0x20DEBB`, `0x20E419–0x20E48C`, `0x20E906–0x20E9C5`; `decompiled/client/functions/00477220.c`.]

The category-`0x13` route first attempts forward-link recovery. A nonzero forward identifier can resolve directly; otherwise a search can recover a role-5 actor whose reverse owner identifier equals the selected actor's **own** ID. After that work, a nonzero forward identifier and nonnull lookup result are needed to reach comparison. It compares actor `+0x1D44` with current actor `+0x1138`, then, only when those words are equal, compares current item `+0x1020` with retained item `+0x1D48`. Either unequal word or unequal item triggers linked-actor reset and reconstruction work. Neither equality comparison itself checks that the linked actor has role 5 or positive health. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20E492–0x20E5EF`.]

The first word at actor `+0x1138` is part of the separately initialized/copied eleven-word block described above. Its full semantic producer set is not closed here; it is retained as a raw 32-bit reconstruction input. A change to it can trigger reconstruction even when the compared item is equal. Conversely, changing only padding or an entry byte ignored by item equality does not by itself establish an item mismatch.

Existing-link reconstruction reads subtype unsigned from source item `+0x01`, level signed from its 16-bit `+0x10`, and experience as the full 32 bits at `+0x04`, and writes them to linked actor `+0x64/+0x190/+0x194`. For an admitted name count below 16 it also reads the encoded fourth body bytes into the linked actor's name storage. Appearance/configuration helpers and owner-relationship work follow. Their full numeric, allocation, behavior and random effects are not expanded here. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20E5E6–0x20E6B8`; `decompiled/client/functions/00446330.c`, `005ee7a0.c`. Invalid negative name counts are outside the valid-item boundary.]

At RVAs `0x20E6BD–0x20E6DA`, this category route **always** stores current `+0x1138` into `+0x1D44`, then assigns the current item into `+0x1D48`. This happens after the existing-link comparison/reconstruction attempt, even if the forward ID was zero or lookup yielded no actor. It precedes the later decision to allocate a missing linked actor. Creation, when admitted by that later branch, allocates a fresh creature, publishes it, resets it, sets role 5 and reads the current item's subtype, signed level and experience. Successful actor creation is not a prerequisite for the earlier snapshot publication. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20E6BD–0x20E70D`, `0x20E7DC–0x20E87C`.]

Later in the same zero-mode route, a separate map pass looks up each actor's forward identifier. If the actor pointer equals world `+0xB8`, lookup is nonnull and the current item category is `0x13`, it writes the linked actor's low 16 level bits into current item `+0x10`, then its full experience word into current item `+0x04`. It does **not** refresh `+0x1D48` or `+0x1D44` there. This pass follows snapshot publication and still runs when the earlier `+0xA4` gate skipped that publication. Thus the current item and retained item have no unconditional end-of-invocation equality guarantee. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20EA07–0x20EAA9`.]

## Credit-consumer copy and admission

Credit consumer RVA `0x1A0BF0` resolves the credit record's first eight-byte key to actor A and its next eight-byte key to another actor. Both results must be nonnull. These are map lookups; there is no requirement here that a resolved object's own ID equal the entry key. Earlier local-target cleanup and local experience handling run before the following branch. [Evidence: `decompiled/client/functions/005a0bf0.c`; `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x1A0BF0–0x1A0CF7`.]

The linked-credit branch requires world `+0xB4 == 0`, then looks up A's `+0x11C8` identifier without a numeric-zero bypass. A mapped key-zero actor can therefore be the result. The result must be nonnull and its binary32 health must compare ordered greater than zero; zero, negative and unordered values fail the shown comparison. The branch does not require A to be world `+0xB8`, A's role to be zero, the linked actor's role to be 5, a reciprocal owner-ID match, or source item category `0x13`. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x1A0CF7–0x1A0D30`.]

It first adds the credit record's 32-bit word `+0x14` to the linked actor's experience `+0x194`, then calls level-up RVA `0x047B00`. After level-up, it copies that actor's resulting full experience word to A's current item `+0x04` at actor `+0x1024`, then the resulting low 16 level bits to current item `+0x10` at actor `+0x1030`. Finally it assigns A's complete current item, by the field-wise helper `0x02C5E0`, to A's retained item at `+0x1D48`. It does not update A's `+0x1D44`. The experience addition uses native 32-bit wraparound; the level mirror narrows by retaining the low 16 bits, not by clamping. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x1A0D32–0x1A0D6D`; `decompiled/client/functions/00447b00.c`, `0042c5e0.c`.]

For the compared item fields, this final copy acknowledges the item changes made by the credit branch before a later maintenance comparison. It is not a guarantee that the accompanying word at `+0x1138` still equals `+0x1D44`, that another writer cannot change the item afterward, or that all credits reach this branch. In particular, role and item-category restrictions on ordinary maintenance must not be imported into the credit consumer.

## Lifetime and explicit limits

Construction establishes an empty retained value and zero comparison word, not a separate valid/invalid flag. The established later invalidation mechanism is comparison against current word/item on an admitted maintenance route. Source clearing, state reset, ordinary item replacement, another category, a missing link or later experience/level mirroring does not imply immediate clearing of the retained item. The snapshot remains part of its actor's storage until a concrete writer replaces it or the actor's allocation ceases to exist. The ordinary erase/destructor inventory, every untraced actor clone, all callbacks and all source-item generation paths are not exhaustively closed.

Item construction, copying and comparison perform the stated byte/integer operations and do not themselves draw random numbers or perform floating-point rounding. The health gate is a binary32 comparison, and level-up/configuration dependencies can perform additional numeric and mutation work. Their complete math, random streams and exception behavior are outside this storage contract. The name-input lock proves only that shown protected write sequence; no whole-frame freshness, global synchronization, transport ordering or server counterpart is inferred.
