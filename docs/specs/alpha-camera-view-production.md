Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD after complete saved-document readback. The complete route contracted here is the final camera-matrix construction at client frame RVAs `0x09CBE3–0x09CEDB`, including its fixed/float conversions, separate horizontal rebasing, two shake calculations, and subsequent amplitude decay. Earlier following and obstruction work is bounded at explicit inputs and observed publication sites. It is not claimed complete.

## Numeric units and reusable operations

The fixed coordinate vectors consist of three signed-eight-byte values, each stored as low/high four-byte pieces. Float conversion helper RVA `0x02C4A0` converts each signed integer to binary32 through an explicit x87 store, reloads/stores that binary32 value, and only then multiplies it in binary32 by exact `1/65536`, bits `0x37800000`. Thus integer-to-float rounding precedes scaling. A raw fixed displacement of `65536` corresponds to one resulting float-coordinate unit. This establishes a numerical unit relation without assigning a world-distance name. Evidence: `S/0042c4a0.c`, native `0x02C4A0–0x02C4FD`, image data at virtual address `0x006FCD94`.

The opposite conversion helper `0x02C460` handles components in ascending order. It first multiplies each binary32 input by exact binary32 `65536`, stores the product as binary32, then passes it to integer-conversion helper `0x28D946` and writes the resulting full eight-byte component. For ordinary finite values within the signed result range, that helper's correction produces truncation toward zero; nonfinite values, conversion overflow, traps and exceptional controls are not a safe-result guarantee. This conversion is not a binary64 product retained until integer conversion. Evidence: native `0x02C460–0x02C49D`, `S/0068d946.c`, and its original native body.

Fixed vector subtraction/addition helpers operate through full low/high pieces with carry/borrow, retaining eight-byte results. Negation helper `0x012260` produces the full two-piece two's-complement negation. Helper `0x068E70`, used for a vector's opposite in the final rebuild, copies all three components and applies fixed scalar multiplication by raw `-65536`: each component uses the low-eight-byte product followed by signed division by `65536`. In the ordinary range where that product does not overflow it is exact negation; it must not be extended to arbitrary signed-eight-byte magnitudes as an overflow-free unary negation. Evidence: `S/00412260.c`, `S/00468e70.c`, `S/0042c900.c`.

Each angle is binary32 in degrees at the three rotation-helper interfaces. The helper first multiplies by binary32 bits `0x3C8EFA35`, approximately `0.01745329238474369`, then widens that rounded result to binary64. It calls the imported cosine first and sine second, narrowing each returned scalar to binary32 before matrix arithmetic. The supported image requests `MSVCR110.dll!_libm_sse2_cos_precise` and `_libm_sse2_sin_precise`, through IAT virtual addresses `0x006FC444` and `0x006FC45C`. No separate exact-pi conversion is inferred. Evidence: `S/004243d0.c`, `S/004244f0.c`, `S/00424610.c`, original instructions and import directory.

For clarity, describe the sixteen physical matrix components as four consecutive stored rows. The rotation helpers mix only the following row pairs; each of the four component pairs uses the saved old values. Products are separate binary32 operations followed by one binary32 sum/difference, without a fused operation or binary64 matrix accumulation.

| Helper RVA | Row-pair relation with its rounded cosine and sine |
| --- | --- |
| `0x0243D0` | New row one is old row two times sine plus old row one times cosine. New row two is old row two times cosine minus old row one times sine. |
| `0x0244F0` | New row zero is old row zero times cosine minus old row two times sine. New row two is old row two times cosine plus old row zero times sine. |
| `0x024610` | New row zero is old row one times sine plus old row zero times cosine. New row one is old row one times cosine minus old row zero times sine. |

Their other rows are retained. In particular, these operations do not rotate the existing fourth stored row. This storage convention is descriptive and can equivalently be transposed for a column-vector interpretation; it does not by itself identify the world's handedness.

Translation helper RVA `0x024990` reads a three-component binary32 vector and updates the fourth stored row only. Each new fourth-row component combines its x-weighted first-row value and y-weighted second-row value, then adds the z-weighted third-row value, then the previous fourth-row component. Products and successive additions are binary32, including products involving stored zeros. The two-coordinate helper `0x034B80` has the corresponding x/y contributions followed by the old fourth-row value, without a z contribution. Evidence: `S/00424990.c`, `S/00434b80.c` and original instructions. These are bounded input/output relations, not an instruction-by-instruction implementation recipe.

## Complete final construction of the matrix pair

The final phase first rebuilds companion matrix `C+0x1EC`: identity at `0x09CBE9`, translation by the float conversion of final fixed eye `E` at `0x09CC09`, then rotations with angle component two at `0x09CC22`, component one at `0x09CC3B`, and component zero at `0x09CC54`. The angle values come from the client angle vector identified above. This is a forward-oriented companion relation; independently rounded reconstruction does not promise an exact numerical inverse of either later matrix.

For matrix `C+0x22C`, identity initialization occurs at `0x09CC5F`. It then applies helper `0x0243D0` to the sign-bit-inverted component zero, `0x0244F0` to the sign-bit-inverted component one, and `0x024610` to the sign-bit-inverted component two, at `0x09CC7F`, `0x09CC9F`, and `0x09CCBF`. These angle inversions flip binary32 sign bits; they are not floating subtractions from zero. After those rotations, it obtains the opposite fixed eye through `0x068E70`, converts that full fixed vector to floats, and applies three-coordinate translation at `0x09CCF2`. Under the ordinary nonoverflowing fixed-negation bound, this is the absolute-eye view construction: opposite eye position is converted before the final translation products.

Only after `C+0x22C` has been built, the frame publishes two rebasing members. At `0x09CCF7–0x09CD0F`, it negates the followed-anchor x component `F.x` and copies the resulting full eight bytes to `C+0x1D8`. At `0x09CD14–0x09CD2C`, it does the same for `F.y` into `C+0x1E0`. The sources are the retained component pointers into `C+0x158` and `C+0x160`, not the final eye x/y components. No cell division, floor, chunk snap, or integer-grid quantization occurs in these two negations and copies. Evidence: component pointer establishment in `0x09C44A–0x09C4C7`, negation/copy helpers `0x012260` and `0x011DF0`, and the final native publication sites.

Matrix `C+0x26C` is then independently reset to identity at `0x09CD37`. It receives a two-component translation from the shake calculation below at `0x09CDDE`. It applies the same sign-bit-inverted angle components, in the same helper order as `C+0x22C`, at `0x09CDFE`, `0x09CE1E`, and `0x09CE3E`. The shake translation already in the fourth stored row survives these row-pair rotations.

The final translation input to this second view is obtained from the fixed opposite eye minus the fixed vector composed of `C+0x1D8`, `C+0x1E0`, and fixed zero. Under the ordinary nonoverflowing negation bound, its raw fixed components are therefore `F.x` minus `E.x`, `F.y` minus `E.y`, and opposite `E.z`. Importantly, these fixed operations occur before the three signed-integer-to-binary32 conversions and scale multiplications. This differs in rounding from separately converting two large absolute positions and subtracting their floats. Three-coordinate translation applies that converted result at `0x09CEB1`. Evidence: native `0x09CE43–0x09CEB1`, fixed vector constructor `0x03B610`, opposite/subtraction helpers `0x068E70/0x02C7A0`, and conversion `0x02C4A0`.

Consequently the two views share the stated inverse-angle construction, but their translations use distinct origins: the first uses absolute opposite eye; the second uses horizontal followed-anchor rebasing and an additional view-coordinate x/y shake. Vertical origin is not rebased to `F.z` by this construction. This is a relation among verified inputs, not a claim that the followed anchor equals creature position or that both matrix bases are bit-identical under changing imported/control state.

## Shake calculation and decay order

The shake amplitude is the binary32 member `C+0x1E8`. The inspected early constructor stores positive zero to this member at `0x059EA6`; that is an identified initialization site, not a full inventory of all later positive producers. The final phase reads the current amplitude once and multiplies it by exact binary32 `0.5` at `0x09CD3C–0x09CD52`. This rounded half amplitude supplies both shake components.

World clock getter RVA `0x088B80` reads the full four-byte member at world-relative `+0x8000BC`, using world receiver `C+0x2E4`. The final phase calls the getter twice. The first result is interpreted as signed 32-bit, converted to binary32, multiplied by frequency bits `0x3D23D70A` (approximately `0.04`), and passed to scalar cosine wrapper `0x00E420`. After its binary32 result is stored, it is multiplied by the half amplitude. This first computed component is the y input to the later two-coordinate translation.

The second getter call occurs after that first cosine and multiplication. Its result is separately interpreted as signed 32-bit, converted to binary32, multiplied by frequency bits `0x3CF5C28F` (approximately `0.03`), passed through the same cosine wrapper, stored as binary32, and multiplied by the same half amplitude. This second computed component is the x input. The caller places the second result as the first argument and the first result as the second argument of helper `0x034B80`, at `0x09CDDE`. The two clock reads must not be collapsed merely because they commonly observe equal values. Evidence: native `0x09CD58–0x09CDDE`, `S/00488b80.c`, `S/0040e420.c`.

Wrapper `0x00E420` widens its already rounded binary32 argument to binary64, reaches the imported precise cosine, narrows its returned low scalar to binary32, and returns it through the x87 scalar result path. This adds no degrees-to-radians multiplier. Thus the shake frequencies multiply raw clock values into the cosine argument directly; the complete world-clock producer and unit/lifetime/wrap policy remain unclosed. It is not justified to replace that clock with a wall-clock or to infer a physical frequency in hertz from the constants alone.

The amplitude is decayed only after the rebased view's final translation. At `0x09CED6`, smoothing helper `0x0ABA20` receives the amplitude member, a positive-zero target, the original four-byte frame-duration argument retained at frame entry `0x088F31–0x088F34`, and coefficient bits `0x3BA3D70A` (approximately `0.005`). Helper `0x0AC150` derives a scalar weight from a signed count: starting at binary64 zero, it performs the count's repeated binary64 approach toward one using the widened binary32 coefficient, then narrows the final weight to binary32. A count at or below zero performs no approach iteration and returns zero. This weight is not specified by an exponent or elapsed-seconds substitution. Evidence: `S/004aba20.c`, `S/004ac150.c`, and their original instructions.

The blend computes binary32 one minus the rounded weight, multiplies that by old amplitude, independently multiplies target by the rounded weight, then adds the two binary32 products. It stores the result to the amplitude member. Therefore the just-constructed view uses the pre-decay amplitude, and the stored decayed value is available afterward. No amplitude-zero branch skips the two clock/cosine calculations on this reached route. Exceptional input/control behavior of smoothing and its caller-duration admission is not a universal damping guarantee.

From `0x09CBE3` through the completed rebased-view translation, the ordinary direct math order comprises the companion's three cosine/sine pairs, the absolute view's three pairs, first shake cosine, second shake cosine, and the rebased view's three pairs: eleven cosine and nine sine requests. The direct route has no `rand` or `srand` call. This is a scoped call-order count; earlier following, obstruction queries, connection setup, and unclosed nested callbacks are not covered by a claim of global random-stream absence.
