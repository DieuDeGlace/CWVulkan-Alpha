# Alpha periodic hit: application and fixed-stat details

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD. Original-only source clarification, dated 2026-11-08. This independently worded contract covers the direct local application of periodic-generated category-0 hit records, the fixed stat calculation selected by that call, and a bounded inventory of original consumers of target float `+0x1190`. Earlier held artifacts were neither reopened nor changed.

## Evidence and terminology

The supported original client is `binary/Cube.exe` under the `disassembled alpha cube world` evidence root: 3,885,568 bytes; SHA-256 `84A7A132A84D4282338E7EA45A1940D32066D64E39CBAFED8F2418D5A6DC30BF`; PE32 machine `0x014C`; preferred image base `0x00400000`. Citations below use paths relative to that evidence root and RVAs relative to that image base. This scope used original functions, original instructions and original image constants only, after reading the assigned source-author policy. No implementation, reconstruction, SDK, extractor, tests, captures, probes, logs or implementation history was consulted.

**Numeric role** means the entity byte at `+0x60`. “Role 0” and “role 6” below mean literal values in that byte. They do not mean the character-class byte at `+0x140`. The two fields are distinct; the applier's vector-ownership and health-eligibility tests discussed here use `+0x60`. No player/enemy/profession names are assigned to those numeric roles by this document.

The motion fields must also be kept distinct. The three velocity floats are at target `+0x34`, `+0x38`, `+0x3C`. A separate stored hit-response vector occupies target `+0x4C`, `+0x50`, `+0x54`. Those are separate storage locations, and this applier does not add the hit's impulse to either vector. The hit record's impulse is at record `+0x38`, `+0x3C`, `+0x40`; its signed stun duration is at record `+0x18`; its amount is the float at record `+0x10`; category is the byte at record `+0x45`. [A1]

The component results below describe finite amounts and the native single-precision magnitude comparisons. They are the final direct results for a nonfatal local application. Fatal downstream routines, later simulation integration of the stored response, and a complete nonfinite-input policy are outside this contract.

## Admission and write order

The periodic producer supplies a zero impulse initially. Its successful-stun path can replace the vertical event component with `10.0`, yielding `(0,0,10)`. The ordinary final event has category 0 and is appended to the outgoing collection before local application. The hit builder calls the local applier only while world byte `+0xB4` is zero; that is also required by the periodic call site. Failure to apply a queued event does not retract that preceding append. [A2]

The applier at RVA `0x195A60` resolves the target identifier again. No target entry, a null stored target pointer, or target health `+0x16C` already nonpositive causes a return before the vector and control writes. When world pointer `+0xB8` is null and the target has numeric role 0, the applier appends/forwards the record and returns before those writes. It also resolves the source identifier independently; a zero identifier is not explicitly excluded from lookup. [A1]

For a positive amount, target scalar `+0x1190` is reduced and clamped below at zero before the motion stage. If target ability byte `+0x68` is 84, this same positive-amount path writes ability 0 and signed target timer `+0x12C` equal to 3000. An amount of zero or a negative amount does not perform that ability-84 cancellation or that scalar reduction. Contact/threat bookkeeping for a resolved source and nonnegative amount also precedes the motion stage. [A1]

Target flag `0x2000` in the word at `+0x7E` imposes another admission condition: the target's threat collection must contain a positive entry whose identifier resolves to a nonnull numeric-role-0 creature. Without such an entry, the applier returns before its vector writes, stun-duration application and health change. Earlier scalar, ability and contact bookkeeping may already have happened. Thus this is partial application, rather than an all-or-nothing event discard. [A1]

After those gates, category 0 reaches the following order: possible stored-response replacement; possible clearing of the three velocity floats; the additional timer at target `+0x130`; then the stun timer at target `+0x12C`; then the health portion. The periodic-generated records have their additional record timer at `+0x1C` equal to zero, so they leave target `+0x130` unchanged. [A1]

## The two vectors and the three requested cases

The stored-response replacement has its own eligibility gate. Numeric role 6 is excluded. For another role, replacement is permitted when world `+0xB4` is zero and the role is not 0, or when the target pointer equals world pointer `+0xB8`. In the periodic caller's zero-mode case, this means every nonzero role except 6 is eligible, while role 0 requires that target-pointer equality. This test does not inspect the character-class byte at `+0x140`. [A1]

For a nonnegative amount, the candidate vector begins as the event impulse. When target timer `+0x12C` is currently positive and the event stun duration is nonpositive, all three candidate components are multiplied by `0.1`. “Currently” includes a timer just set to 3000 by the preceding ability-84 cancellation. The applier compares the candidate's computed squared magnitude with the stored response's computed squared magnitude. It replaces **all three** stored components only when eligible and the candidate magnitude is at least the stored magnitude. Equality admits replacement. Otherwise it preserves all three stored components. There is no componentwise maximum, accumulation, or preservation of old horizontal components during a successful replacement. [A1]

For a nonnegative amount and a positive event stun duration, the three velocity floats at `+0x34`, `+0x38`, `+0x3C` are then each written to zero. That clear does not depend on the stored-response eligibility test, the result of its magnitude comparison, the target's numeric role, or the later health-eligibility predicate. [A1]

| Periodic category-0 record at this stage | Velocity at `+0x34 / +0x38 / +0x3C` | Stored response at `+0x4C / +0x50 / +0x54` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nonnegative amount; stun duration zero; impulse `(0,0,0)` | Every component is preserved. | Eligible targets receive `(0,0,0)` only when the stored vector's computed squared magnitude is zero; otherwise every component is preserved. |
| Nonnegative amount; positive stun duration; impulse `(0,0,10)` | Every component is cleared to zero. | Eligible targets receive the complete replacement `(0,0,10)` when the stored vector's computed squared magnitude is at most 100; otherwise every component is preserved. |
| Negative amount, with either of the above event impulses or stun durations | Every component is preserved by this stage. | Every component is preserved: the entire candidate-vector/magnitude/replacement stage is skipped. |

The zero-magnitude condition is a condition on the native floating calculation, not necessarily on each stored component being exactly zero. Very small nonzero components can have squares that underflow to zero. Likewise, unusually large components can overflow the squared calculation. The contract is the magnitude comparison actually performed, not an ideal real-number norm. The incoming sum is formed from squared Y and X, followed by squared Z; the stored sum is formed from squared X and Y, followed by squared Z. Each arithmetic instruction is single precision. [A1]

For a positive stun duration, category 0 raises target timer `+0x12C` to the larger of its current signed value and the event value. This later timer update also occurs for a **negative** amount, despite that amount skipping both vector operations. With event duration zero the applier preserves this timer unless the earlier positive-amount ability-84 cancellation set it to 3000. Consequently a zero-stun hit can set a control timer without clearing velocity through the positive-stun branch. A positive-stun hit can clear velocity while preserving an older, larger stored response. [A1]

The health portion is later than these writes. A positive amount reaches health subtraction only if predicate RVA `0x03E310` accepts the target and, when the source resolves, also accepts the source. That predicate accepts numeric roles 0, 1, 3 and 5; it accepts role 6 except species identifiers 140, 141 and 142 at `+0x64`. Other numeric roles fail it. Failing this predicate does not roll back motion or timer writes already performed. A negative ordinary category-0 amount instead reaches healing, capped by target maximum-health routine RVA `0x044DB0`, without those positive-damage role checks. [A1, A3]

The applier's general nonzero-mode/nonlocal-target health gate also follows the motion and timer stage. It is not an extra restriction for the audited periodic caller, whose local application is made in zero mode. If health becomes nonpositive, the direct applier additionally clamps health to zero, clears target `+0x6C`, queues its death sound and calls further death/world routines. This document does not claim that those unaudited downstream routines preserve the motion/control state until the final return. [A1]

## Target scalar `+0x1190`: confirmed consumers, unproved label

This scalar is a four-byte float separate from health `+0x16C`, the source ratio integer at `+0x70`, target float `+0x170`, and the neighboring float at `+0x1194`. Constructor RVA `0x03B690` initializes it to zero. Positive hit amounts lower it before the later health predicate, so that hit effect is observable even when the later positive-damage predicate refuses health subtraction. Its semantic resource name is not established here. [A1, A4]

The inspected original consumers support the following bounded roles:

- Probability routines RVAs `0x03E9E0` and `0x0444A0` scan the resident buff list for type 11. Without that override, the returned threshold is the target's base routine `0x03EA40` result plus `0.15` times `+0x1190`; with the resident type-11 override, the threshold is 1. The boolean routine consumes a random draw divided by `32767` and makes a strict comparison with that threshold. This scope does not assign the probability an official textual combat-stat name. [A5]
- Simulation checks ability byte 79 at RVA `0x2195BA`. In that path, the later `+0x1190` update incorporates the length of the velocity vector at `+0x34`, as well as native influence/stat terms. Outside that ability path, flag `0x400` at `+0x124` selects a recharge calculation; the alternative decreases the scalar. The inspected paths apply zero/one bounds, and a resident type-3 buff sets it to 1. A subsequent update multiplies `+0x1190` by a time-dependent coefficient and adds the result to target `+0x170`, capped above at 1. The complete evolution coefficients, input-to-flag binding and all exceptional paths are not specified by this consumer inventory. [A6]
- Candidate-selection routine RVA `0x02CB20` reads a candidate's positive `+0x1190` when rescaling squared distance before the candidate range/relationship tests. Its divisor has a lower-bound operation. The precise influence/power coefficient in that candidate-selection calculation is outside this narrow contract. [A7]
- Controller/UI routine RVA `0x088EE0` tests the controlled creature's scalar at RVAs `0x092FB8` and `0x0930D2`. Within the inspected gate, a positive value is sufficient to select the enabled branch that supplies boolean 1 to the control object; zero permits other ability/resource checks. This proves a controller/UI gate, not a particular keyboard binding or named movement command. [A8]
- Rendering routine RVA `0x0AC260` uses a positive scalar to select extra drawing paths and supplies a draw factor of one minus `0.75` times that value at RVA `0x0BA92B`. The complete shader/visual interpretation is not established in this scope. [A9]

These original reads and writes establish that the scalar participates in motion-dependent state, probability, resource coupling, candidate selection and rendering. They do not justify renaming it as health, mana, stamina, or a specific native UI label. This inventory is not a full contract for the scalar's lifecycle.

## Completed fixed target-stat relation

The periodic hit selects RVA `0x0467A0` on the target object. The following describes its complete scalar relation and the fixed item-stat dependency it calls, without assigning a recovered stat name. These routines make no random draw and do not mutate the entity or item records. [A10–A12]

For any floating level input, define a **level factor** mathematically as 2 raised to three times the quantity “one minus the reciprocal of one plus `0.05` times level minus one,” with the inner level subtraction grouped as “level minus one.” A **rank factor** is 2 raised to one quarter of the numeric rank. These factors have native precision rules below; the mathematical descriptions alone do not authorize algebraic rearrangement. [A10, A12]

For the target, level is the signed integer at `+0x190`, converted to float. Rank is the unsigned byte at `+0x1A8`. The common factor is the target rank factor multiplied by its level factor. For a target whose numeric role byte `+0x60` is nonzero, the initial stat is target float `+0x188` multiplied by this common factor. For numeric role 0, the initial stat instead uses target level factor multiplied by 2, followed by multiplication by `+0x188`; the rank factor does not enter that initial base. [A10]

Target flag `0x20` at `+0x7E` adds the original common factor to that initial stat, including for numeric role 0. Therefore the role-0 base override does not remove rank from this optional bonus. Then eligible item contributions are added in the following fixed order: target item at `+0x530` when its category byte is 4; at `+0x648` when category is 6; at `+0x760` when category is 5; and at `+0x878` when category is 7. No other slot contributes in this routine. No branch here reads character class `+0x140`. [A10]

The contribution helper at RVA `0x0C7AF0` accepts item category 4, 5, 6 or 7; another category returns zero. Its item level is the signed 16-bit value at item `+0x10`, converted to float, plus `0.1` times the signed 32-bit value at item `+0x114`, converted to float. Its rank is the unsigned byte at item `+0x0C`. The helper at RVA `0x045F60` returns this item's level factor multiplied by its rank factor. [A11, A12]

The item weight starts at 1 for category 4 and at `0.5` for categories 5, 6 and 7. Item tag `+0x0D` equal to 1 or 19 multiplies that weight by the stored single-precision constant `0.85`; tag 26 or 27 multiplies it by `0.75`; other tags leave the weight unchanged. The contribution is the item factor multiplied by that final weight. This closes the item-stat arithmetic used by all four eligible target slots. [A11, A12]

Every elementary arithmetic step before a power is binary32: subtracting one from level; multiplying by `0.05`; adding one; dividing one by that denominator; subtracting the quotient from one; and multiplying by 3. The resulting exponent is promoted to binary64 for the native power operation, whose result is explicitly converted back to binary32. Rank is converted to binary32, multiplied by `0.25`, promoted for its power, then likewise converted back. Products of the two factors, the target base, item weights and sequential slot additions use binary32 at each native step. The item's `+0x114` term is multiplied by `0.1` before the signed-short level term is added. [A10–A12]

The role-0 override explicitly evaluates the power of 2 with exponent 1, converts that result to binary32, multiplies it by the target level factor, then multiplies by `+0x188`. The optional common-factor addition and each slot addition follow in the order stated. No level clamp, denominator guard or result clamp appears in these fixed-stat routines. Exact cross-platform replication of the original runtime's binary64 power and floating-point environment still requires separate verification; no missing item term or unknown numeric coefficient remains in the relation audited here. [A10–A13]
