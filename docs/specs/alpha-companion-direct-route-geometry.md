## Call boundary and meaning of the result

The call at RVA `0x03AF8E` supplies the world receiver in ECX and four 32-bit stack arguments, in this order at callee entry: subject actor pointer, subject position pointer, target position pointer, and one binary32 scalar. The position pointers are exactly subject `+0x10` and target `+0x10`. The scalar is one binary32 addition of subject `+0x80` and target `+0x80`, loaded as binary32. The helper returns a Boolean in AL and removes sixteen argument bytes. Its original decompile's apparent void return is incomplete: the image has explicit success returns and an additional final comparison through RVA `0x20A9E0`. The native instructions take precedence. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x03AF67–0x03AF95`, `0x209480–0x2095FF`, `0x20A8C2–0x20A9E0`; `decompiled/client/functions/0043ace0.c`, `decompiled/client/functions/00609480.c`.]

A missing entry or null mapped actor bypasses the geometry call. The subject is a distinct caller argument. This geometry boundary does not compare the actors' own identifiers or use an actor identifier as a spatial-record key. The lookup comparator, receiver ownership, target retention, and reasons this behavior receiver exists are outside this audit. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x03ACFA–0x03AD39`; `decompiled/client/functions/0043ace0.c`.]

Immediately before the call, the caller converts the three signed 64-bit target-minus-subject position differences to binary32 and scales them by `1/65536`. It forms the squared three-dimensional separation with binary32 products and additions, horizontal second-axis square plus first-axis square, then third-axis square. It writes binary32 zero to subject `+0x40`, `+0x44`, and `+0x48`. A comparison of binary32 `16.0` with that squared separation bypasses the geometry call only when `16.0` is ordered greater; equality and unordered comparison reach the call. In particular, the helper's final non-proximity result is true when its private simulated displacement from the original subject position has squared length strictly greater than `625.0`; it can therefore return true after progress without satisfying the target-proximity test. No simulated coordinate is returned or stored into either actor position. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x2095EB–0x209600`, `0x20A8C2–0x20A9E0`, constant RVA `0x31E200`.]

## Numeric representation and constant boundaries

Each supplied position is three consecutive signed 64-bit integers, occupying 24 bytes. The horizontal components begin at offsets zero and eight, and the third component at sixteen. Native paired subtraction carries/borrows across each complete 64-bit component before numeric conversion. Actor `+0x80`, `+0x84`, and `+0x88` are read here as binary32 size operands. The supplied scalar is retained separately; it is not recomputed from the target during the helper. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20949A–0x2095E6`, `0x209607–0x2096EE`, `0x209A30–0x209AAE`.]

The ordinary finite arithmetic described below preserves each native boundary. Signed 64-bit differences are loaded by x87 integer conversion, stored to a 32-bit float slot, loaded and stored as binary32 again, and then used by SSE scalar binary32 operations. Thus conversion to binary32 occurs before multiplication by `1/65536`; there is no binary64 subtraction of already scaled positions. Products, sums, reciprocals, and size scaling identified as binary32 have separate native operations, without a fused multiply-add. The helper does not install its own x87 control word or MXCSR settings. Rounding modes, subnormal treatment, exception masks, and imported-math behavior remain ambient inputs. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x2094D1–0x20958E`, `0x209662–0x20978B`, `0x209943–0x209A27`, `0x20A8C2–0x20A9D3`.]

## Initial proximity and iteration budget

The first test converts subject-position minus target-position horizontal differences as above, squares them separately, and adds second-axis square to first-axis square. The scalar is squared separately in binary32. Initial horizontal proximity requires the squared scalar to be ordered strictly greater than the horizontal squared distance. Only then is the third-axis difference converted, scaled, and made absolute. Its magnitude must be ordered strictly less than binary32 subject `+0x88` plus the supplied scalar. Both tests passing returns AL true before any terrain or spatial-record query. Horizontal equality fails this initial shortcut; unordered comparisons also fail it. There is no nonnegative-size, finite-value, identifier, role, or ownership check added at this point. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x2094D1–0x209603`.]

Otherwise the helper copies the entire supplied subject position into private working storage. It forms target-minus-subject horizontal differences, converts and scales them, and treats the third direction component as zero. The squared length is evaluated as second-axis square plus first-axis square, then plus zero. Only an ordered length-squared greater than `2500.0` triggers square-root normalization and a separate binary32 multiply by `50.0` for each horizontal component. Each normalized component uses reciprocal times its original component, then times `50.0`. The resulting two components are used to calculate the iteration budget, not as the actual per-iteration movement increments. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209607–0x209757`.]

The budget is the native signed 32-bit truncating conversion of binary32 one plus the binary32-narrowed square root of second-axis square plus first-axis square plus `0.5625`, in that addition order. The integer conversion is `CVTTSS2SI`, distinct from the x87-to-64-bit helper. A nonpositive budget skips all iterations. Otherwise the counter begins at zero and each iteration processes the three axes in order. There is no elapsed-time input to this helper and no frame-duration multiplier. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209757–0x2097A0`, `0x20A89D–0x20A8C2`.]

## Per-iteration supplied-input kernel

At the beginning of each iteration the helper again measures the private working position against the supplied target position. The horizontal proximity gate here admits ordered equality: the squared scalar must be ordered greater than or equal to horizontal squared distance. The third-axis test remains strictly ordered less than subject `+0x88` plus the scalar. Passing both returns true immediately. The target position is read again; it is not a permanently captured copy of the target. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x2097A0–0x209915`.]

On failure, the two target-minus-working-position horizontal differences are converted and scaled anew. Their squared length adds second-axis square, first-axis square, and zero. Only an ordered positive result triggers square-root normalization to a horizontal unit direction. The third direction component is then the fixed binary32 `0xBF35C28F`. This direction triplet is retained for the complete three-axis pass; it is not recomputed after the first or second axis changes working position. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209915–0x209A47`.]

For each axis, in first-horizontal, second-horizontal, third-axis order, the selected binary32 direction is multiplied by binary32 `65536`, converted through RVA `0x28D946`, and added as a complete 64-bit increment to only that working coordinate. For the ordinary fixed vertical operand that increment is exactly `-46530`. The increment is retained so that this same axis change can later be undone. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209A50–0x209AAE`, `0x20A881–0x20A8A9`.]

The first query after every tentative axis addition is RVA `0x19F0A0`, with world as receiver, the private working triplet, the address of subject `+0x80`, and a zero final flag. Its true result means that its admitted terrain-cell scan found a blocking type. It does not write the supplied position. Because the final flag is zero, that query's own optional spatial-object branch is not reached. The route helper performs the separate spatial-record scan described below when the terrain result is false, and also on the third-axis pass even if the terrain result is true. A true terrain result on a horizontal pass bypasses that separate record scan and enters the possible raised-box handling directly. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209A84–0x209ACB`; `decompiled/client/functions/0059f0a0.c`; native RVAs `0x19F0A0–0x19F44F`, `0x19F5CB–0x19F5DD`, `0x19F834–0x19F84A`.]

If both the terrain and reached spatial-record checks are clear, the tentative axis addition remains. A third-axis blockage is undone. A blockage caused by a spatial record is also undone, without raised-box handling. A horizontal terrain blockage reaches raised-box handling only when bit `0x0100` of subject's 16-bit field `+0x7E` is clear. If that bit is set, the axis addition is undone. These decisions do not terminate the whole outer iteration: the next axis and later iterations still run. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20A10A–0x20A152`, `0x20A87D–0x20A8BC`.]

## Terrain query and shared storage dependencies

RVA `0x19F0A0` calculates half of each binary32 subject size operand, then multiplies each half by `65536` and converts it to signed 64 bits, in first-axis, second-axis, third-axis order. It calculates lower working-coordinate-minus-half-size quotients by signed division by `65536`, retaining signed 32-bit grid coordinates. It independently repeats the half-size conversions for the upper working-coordinate-plus-half-size bounds. There is no special negative-coordinate correction in this query's bound conversion. It visits inclusive integer bounds with first axis outermost, second axis next, third axis innermost, but queries only cells on at least one of the six boundary surfaces. On the first admitted blocking cell it returns true; after all admitted cells it returns false. Empty or inverted bounds do not gain an extra rejection check. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19F0A0–0x19F2B9`, `0x19F2B9–0x19F44F`, `0x19F5CB–0x19F5DD`, `0x19F834–0x19F84A`; `decompiled/client/functions/0059f0a0.c`.]

Both this terrain query and the route helper's later raised-box scan access resident world tables directly. Horizontal integer cell coordinates must each be within `0..0xFFFFFF`. Quotient-by-256 chunk coordinates must each be within `0..0xFFFF`; quotient-by-64 region coordinates within `0..0x3FF`. The region-pointer table begins at world `+0xBC`, with first region coordinate the major component of a 1024-by-1024 index. The chunk-pointer table begins at region `+0x10018`, with first chunk remainder the major component of a 64-by-64 index. Within a chunk, terrain columns are reached through chunk `+0xA8`, using second cell remainder times 256 plus first cell remainder and a 32-byte column stride. No request to generate, load, or repair a missing region or chunk occurs on these paths. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19F2B9–0x19F407`, `0x20A5E0–0x20A74B`; `decompiled/client/functions/0059f0a0.c`, `decompiled/client/functions/00609480.c`.]

Each column supplies signed 32-bit base third coordinate at `+0x10`, a pointer to four-byte cell entries at `+0x18`, and signed 32-bit entry count at `+0x1C`. A missing/out-of-range horizontal route, missing region/chunk, missing computed column, or third coordinate below column base selects that query's missing sentinel. A third coordinate inside the stored span reaches RVA `0x02F730` with the relative index and column receiver. Above the span, the selected sentinel depends on whether the third coordinate is positive. A retrieved cell with low five bits of byte `+3` equal to zero, third coordinate at or below zero, and bit `0x40` of that byte clear is replaced by the lower sentinel. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x19F3B0–0x19F42C`, `0x20A708–0x20A750`, `0x02F730–0x02F7DB`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042f730.c`.]

## Separate resident spatial-record checks

The separate scan begins from each current horizontal raw coordinate divided by `65536` as signed 64 bits, narrowed to a signed 32-bit quotient, then divided by eight with truncation toward zero. It examines the inclusive three-by-three neighborhood centered on those two results. First neighborhood coordinate is outermost; second is inner. Only coordinates in `0..0x1FFFFF` proceed. Division by 32 supplies chunk coordinates, which reach the same world region/chunk tables. The object-bucket storage is chunk `+0xAC`, indexed by second remainder times 32 plus first remainder, with an eight-byte bucket stride. Each admitted bucket is traversed through its resident linked entries in their stored next-pointer order. There is no sorting, deduplication, loading, or generator call in this scan. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209ACB–0x209C80`, `0x20A0B8–0x20A105`.]

Each linked entry supplies a record pointer at entry `+8`. Record's signed 32-bit selector at zero excludes values 7, 6, and 9. The record pointer itself is then looked up in the subject's collection rooted at `+0x1468`, using unsigned 32-bit pointer comparison and exact equality; a present pointer is excluded. For selectors 1, 8, 2, 3, and 5, record byte `+0x30` must be nonzero. Other selector values do not receive that byte gate. This is a pointer-membership filter, not comparison with an actor's own ID or world creature-map key. The collection's producer, membership lifetime, and purpose remain unclosed. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209C80–0x209D31`.]

The geometry uses three signed 64-bit record coordinates at `+8/+0x10/+0x18`, a 32-bit orientation operand at `+0x20`, and three binary32 sizes at `+0x24/+0x28/+0x2C`. Odd orientation, including a negative odd operand, swaps the first two local size values; even orientation does not. These local values are not written back to the record. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209D31–0x209D7E`.]

Before any overlap tests for a record, the signed 64-bit working-minus-record difference along the current axis is converted to binary32 without the `1/65536` scale, multiplied in binary32 by that axis's retained direction, and converted to signed 64 bits by RVA `0x28D946`. Only a result strictly less than zero proceeds. This conversion gate is significant: a small negative product that truncates to zero is excluded. The comparison zero itself is obtained through a separate binary32 zero-times-65536 conversion. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209D7E–0x209E11`.]

The subsequent overlap order is first horizontal axis, second horizontal axis, then third axis. For each horizontal axis, record half-size is multiplied by `65536` and converted before subject half-size is similarly converted. Admission requires record coordinate minus its converted half-size to be less than or equal to working coordinate plus subject half-size, and working coordinate minus subject half-size to be strictly less than record coordinate plus record half-size. The third-axis test first converts subject half-height; record base must be less than or equal to working third coordinate plus that amount. It then converts the record's entire third size times `65536`; working third coordinate minus subject half-height must be strictly below record base plus that converted full height. These are complete signed 64-bit comparisons. There is no minimum size or nonnegative-size clamp. The first admitted record sets blockage and disallows the raised-box alternative for that axis. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x209E11–0x20A10C`.]

The terrain blockage flag carries into the third-axis record scan. A known blockage may end neighborhood traversal early even without a newly admitted record. None of these record reads invokes a descriptor/model callback. The original record constructors, selector meanings, publication, and consistency with terrain are not established by this consumer. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20A0B8–0x20A12A`.]

## Raised-box alternative and terminal result

For an admitted horizontal terrain blockage, the helper calculates converted signed 64-bit half-sizes in axis order. It separately converts the binary32 triplet zero, zero, `0x3F8147AE`, after multiplying each component by binary32 `65536`. It constructs a lower corner from working position plus that converted offset minus half-sizes, and converts the lower corner to signed 32-bit grid coordinates through RVA `0x02F040`. It then independently repeats the half-size and offset conversions, constructs the corresponding upper corner, and converts it with the same helper. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20A152–0x20A583`.]

RVA `0x02F040` converts the third component first, then second, then first. Each conversion is signed 64-bit division by `65536`, retaining a 32-bit quotient. For every negative signed raw component it additionally subtracts one from that quotient, including an exactly integral negative coordinate; nonnegative components receive no subtraction. This differs from the initial terrain query's truncating-only bounds and must not be replaced by a uniform mathematical floor assumption. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x02F040–0x02F0FE`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042f040.c`.]

The raised box scans all cells in the inclusive bounds, rather than only its surfaces, with first axis outermost, second next, third innermost. It uses the terrain lookup and type test described above. A blocking cell sets a retained flag, but the remaining third-coordinate cells in that same inner run are still visited. The flag is tested after that run; on blockage the current tentative horizontal increment is undone, with no third-coordinate adjustment. Empty/inverted ranges do not receive an extra rejection. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20A583–0x20A7C1`, `0x20A87D–0x20A89D`.]

When the raised scan remains clear, the helper retains the tentative horizontal movement and replaces its private third coordinate. It first converts subject half-height times `65536` to signed 64 bits and subtracts that quantity from the working third coordinate. If this signed result is negative it subtracts another raw `65536` before signed division by `65536`; otherwise it divides directly. Only the low 32-bit quotient is retained and sign-extended. That quotient is scaled back by a 16-bit left shift. The native sequence then subtracts the signed 64-bit conversion of binary64 `-66191.36`, adds the converted subject half-height, and finally adds the signed 64-bit conversion of binary32 `66191.359375`. In the stated ordinary finite domain these two constant conversions are `-66191` and `66191`, so their combined raw displacement is `132382`. This replacement affects only private working storage. It is not a write to subject `+0x20`, and it is not derived from a queried terrain surface height. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20A7C1–0x20A87B`, constants RVAs `0x31E220/0x31E208`.]

After all three axes, the outer counter advances and the next iteration performs the target-proximity test anew. Exhausting the budget falls through to the final displacement test. Each working-minus-original-supplied-subject component is converted to binary32 and scaled by `1/65536`. The final binary32 sum is second-axis square plus first-axis square, then third-axis square. AL is true only when that sum is ordered strictly greater than binary32 `625.0`. Equality or unordered comparison yields false. There is no final target-distance requirement, no route-length output, no contact record, and no publication of the working triplet. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20A89D–0x20A9E0`.]

## Mutation, randomness, and limits

Within the ordinary supplied-input helper, all tentative position changes, direction vectors, overlap sizes, grid bounds, and progress counters are private stack state. The inspected paths contain no actor-position store, actor-health store, actor identifier change, world aggregate append, event publication, allocation, random-number call, seed read, seed write, or procedural generation call. Terrain/object presence still depends on already resident world data, whose generation and seed history are outside this scope. The defensive shared-cell writes of RVA `0x02F730` and the separate caller-side clearing/steering effects above are explicit exceptions to a broader purity claim. [Evidence: original native body RVA `0x209480–0x20A9E0`, reached query RVA `0x19F0A0`, conversion/division helpers RVAs `0x28D946/0x28D9E0`, and cell accessor RVA `0x02F730`.]

No lock, ownership transfer, or lifetime pin is established in this helper. Subject size fields and exclusion-collection links can be reread during the run; target coordinates are reread during the proximity and direction stages. The final displacement also reads the original supplied position pointer again. Thus the arithmetic is closed for stable supplied inputs and valid resident tables, not a proof of an atomic snapshot under mutation. Upstream size producers, actor construction/defaults, behavior admission, collection population, chunk/column/object lifetime, concurrent changes, and the caller's false-result route construction remain unresolved. No whole-frame schedule, role priority, server equivalence, or environmental meaning follows from this contract.

The ordinary domain assumes valid readable actors and position pointers, valid bucket/list/column storage, finite representable conversion operands, nonoverflowing signed grid/count arithmetic, and terminating resident lists. The native function adds no general validation for those assumptions. NaNs, infinities, negative or malformed sizes, failed square-root imports, integer overflow, damaged links, and concurrent invalidation cannot be assigned a normalized Boolean policy from this bounded audit. The specifically identified ordered/unordered comparison outcomes are established; they do not close floating-point traps or every exceptional intermediate. The security-cookie checks at the helper/query exits protect their stack frames; their failure-service behavior and imported runtime internals are outside this contract. [Evidence: `binary/Cube.exe`, RVAs `0x20958E–0x2095FF`, `0x20970E–0x209791`, `0x2098AE–0x20990F`, `0x20A9CC–0x20A9E0`, `0x19F5CB–0x19F5DD`, `0x19F834–0x19F84A`.]
