# Alpha visibility ray geometry

Status: FINAL / STABLE / HELD. This contract covers the original ray query and its scoped native dependencies, not a general collision system.

The supported image is `binary/Cube.exe` under the relative evidence root `disassembled alpha cube world`, preferred image base `0x400000`, SHA-256 `84a7a132a84d4282338e7ea45a1940d32066d64e39cbafed8f2418d5a6dc30bf`. The principal evidence is `decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVA `0x1A35D0–0x1A4708`, and the individually identified original function file `decompiled/client/functions/005a35d0.c`. The native instructions, rather than the decompiler's apparent void return or omitted conversion operands, establish the numeric relations below. Across this routine and its scoped spatial, column, conversion and default-record initialization helpers, 1,244 instruction entries were checked against the supported image with no byte mismatch. Constants were read from that image. No reconstruction, runtime material, or earlier contract supplies facts here.

**Query interface and output.** The receiver is a world object. Five 32-bit stack slots contain, in order, a pointer to three signed 64-bit starting coordinates, a pointer to three binary32 direction components, a binary32 maximum parameter, a byte-valued requested voxel category, and a byte-valued additional-geometry option. Only the low byte of each option slot is tested. The query removes 20 argument bytes on a normal return. It returns an x87 value loaded from its final binary32 travel parameter; it does not return a hit pointer, material, position, face normal or Boolean. Direction normalization and a nonzero direction are not checked or supplied by the routine. Therefore the parameter is a physical distance only to the extent justified by the caller's direction. Input pointers are read again during the query and are not atomically snapshotted. (`decompiled/client/assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A35D0–0x1A369F`, `0x1A46F2–0x1A4708`.)

The query first performs voxel traversal, then clamps an overshooting travel parameter to the supplied maximum. A nonzero additional-geometry byte subsequently permits spatial-record side faces to shorten that value. The additional pass does not reclassify voxels or restart traversal after shortening it. Its work can occur even when the voxel traversal was skipped or stopped immediately. The maximum is not silently replaced by a positive default, and there is no final lower clamp. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3681–0x1A3696`, `0x1A3A62–0x1A3A9D`, `0x1A468C–0x1A4708`.)

**Coordinate and conversion conventions.** Each starting coordinate is divided as signed 64-bit integer by exactly 65,536 through native helper `0x28D9E0`, in X, Y, Z order. Division truncates toward zero. Only the low 32 bits of each quotient become a signed-tested voxel coordinate. For each negative original 64-bit coordinate, that stored coordinate is decremented once with 32-bit wrap. This extra decrement is unconditional on the remainder: a start of −65,536 produces initial voxel −2, whereas −1 produces voxel −1 and zero produces voxel zero. It is consequently not interchangeable with mathematical floor at exact negative multiples. Quotient overflow beyond the retained 32 bits is not rejected. These reads and divisions precede the positive-maximum test. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A35E6–0x1A3681`; `decompiled/client/functions/0068d9e0.c`.)

Within this document, a fixed-coordinate displacement is converted by loading its signed 64-bit integer through x87 and storing it as binary32 before subsequent scalar single-precision arithmetic. A travel-dependent displacement is formed by multiplying a direction component by the current binary32 travel parameter, then multiplying that rounded binary32 result by binary32 65,536.0. The stored binary32 result is passed through native conversion helper `0x28D946` to a signed 64-bit integer and added to the original starting coordinate with 64-bit wrap. All three direction-times-parameter products are formed first, in X, Y, Z order; multiplication by 65,536.0 and integer conversion then follow in X, Y, Z order. This preserves two separate binary32 multiplication boundaries and an integer conversion; it is not one extended-precision product.

For finite binary32 inputs whose integer conversion is representable under the ordinary masked x87 environment, `0x28D946` produces truncation toward zero. It consumes its x87 argument and returns the full 64-bit result. The helper performs its own fractional adjustment after the x87 integer conversion, with no imported math call, seed call or allocation. Overflow, invalid conversion and unmasked floating-point exceptions are outside that ordinary relation. The ray routine does not install an x87 control word or MXCSR mode. Its scalar SSE operations, x87 stores, incoming rounding controls, and status/exception effects remain distinct. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A37A7–0x1A388E`, helper `0x28D946–0x28D9BA`; `decompiled/client/functions/0068d946.c`.)

The stored binary32 constants are 65,536.0 at RVA `0x2FCE7C`, its exact reciprocal `2^-16` at `0x2FCD94`, 10.0 at `0x345E74`, 0.5 at `0x345DA0`, and approximately `0.000001` at `0x31DAF4` with bits `0x358637BD` and exact binary32 value `9.999999974752427e-7`. These values govern scaling, candidate-step bounds, and direction eligibility; none is inferred from an action or duration label.

**Voxel lookup and category.** With an ordinary finite maximum strictly greater than zero, traversal begins at travel parameter positive zero and the initial voxel just described. Its current X/Y coordinates must both be signed nonnegative and strictly below `0x1000000` to reach spatial lookup. It divides them by 256 with integer truncation toward zero and calls world spatial lookup `0x034D10`. A missing spatial object selects the missing-cell fallback record.

For a present spatial object, the column address is its pointer at `+0xA8` plus 32 times the row-major local column index: local Y times 256 followed by local X. The local indices are the current voxel coordinates modulo 256. A computed column address of zero selects the missing-cell fallback. This is a test of the computed address; an invalid null array base combined with a nonzero index is not validated as an unloaded array. A valid allocated column array is a prerequisite to normal lookup.

The column's signed 32-bit `+0x10` is its base Z, `+0x1C` is its signed-tested element count, and `+0x18` is its element pointer. A current Z below base Z selects the missing-cell fallback. Base Z plus count is a 32-bit wrapped addition for the upper comparison. At or above that upper boundary, Z strictly greater than zero selects the upper fallback, while Z at most zero selects the lower fallback. Inside the interval, `0x02F730` is called on the column with signed 32-bit Z minus base Z; records occupy four bytes. If the returned record's last byte has low five bits zero, current Z is at most zero, and that byte's mask `0x40` is clear, it is replaced for this query by the lower fallback. A record with that mask set is retained. No RGB-like byte is used to choose the traversal category. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A36A0–0x1A3774`.)

The three world fallback records have the following original initializer results. Raw byte sequences are listed in increasing address order; they do not establish broader material names or producer lifetimes.

| Fallback | Storage RVA | Four initialized bytes | Low-five-bit class | Query category |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lower | `0x36B7CC` | `FF FF FF 82` | 2 | 0 |
| Upper | `0x36B7D0` | `FF FF FF 00` | 0 | 0 |
| Missing | `0x36B7D4` | `C8 C8 C8 01` | 1 | 1 |

These bytes are written by original native initializers `0x2FAB30`, `0x2FAAD0`, and `0x2FAB00`, respectively. The ray reads the current shared records rather than reconstructing those constants on each access. The initializer values establish ordinary initialized behavior; this audit does not prove that arbitrary external or indirect writes can never alter the shared storage. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x2FAAD0–0x2FAB57`.)

The selected voxel record is classified only from the low five bits of its last byte: classes 0 and 2 yield category byte 0, and all other classes yield category byte 1. Traversal continues only while that resulting byte equals the requested category byte exactly. Requested byte 0 therefore continues through class 0/2 and stops on other classes; requested byte 1 does the reverse. Values 2–255 match neither category and stop at the first queried voxel. There is no truth-value normalization of that requested byte. With initialized fallbacks, missing/outside/below-column lookup stops category-0 traversal and permits category-1 traversal; upper/lower fallback does the reverse. A start already in the other category returns a voxel-stage travel parameter of zero. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3774–0x1A3796`.)

**Traversal step and ties.** After a queried voxel matches the requested category, a signed counter is checked against 500. It starts at zero. A value greater than 500 terminates before the next advance; equality at 500 still allows an advance. The counter is increased only after a match and successful counter admission. Thus there can be 501 advances and, if the positive maximum still permits another iteration, a 502nd voxel lookup/classification before the counter limit stops traversal. The material/category comparison precedes the counter test, so even that terminal iteration first queries its voxel. The counter does not bound the later spatial-record pass. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A378D–0x1A37C8`.)

For each admitted advance, the current fixed-coordinate point is reconstructed from the original start and direction times the current travel parameter, using the binary32 and signed-conversion relation above. Candidate axes are considered in X, Y, Z order. An axis is eligible only when its binary32 direction squared is at least the stored `0x358637BD` threshold; equality is eligible. For a positive direction, its boundary is the signed 32-bit current voxel coordinate increased by one, then expanded to signed 64-bit and multiplied by 65,536. For a nonpositive direction, its boundary is the current signed voxel coordinate expanded and multiplied by 65,536, without that increase. The boundary minus reconstructed current point is a wrapped signed 64-bit difference.

For an eligible axis, that difference is converted to binary32, divided by the binary32 direction, stored, converted to signed 64-bit with `0x28D946`, converted back through x87 to binary32, and multiplied by `2^-16`. This integer quantization occurs before the rescaling; it is not a direct continuous distance to the boundary. The chosen increment begins at binary32 10.0 with X as the default axis. An axis replaces it only with a strictly smaller increment. Equal candidate increments retain the earlier axis, giving X before Y before Z when equal eligible values are below 10.0. A candidate exactly 10.0 does not replace the default. If no eligible candidate is strictly below 10.0, the increment remains 10.0 and the chosen axis remains X even if X was not eligible. No absolute-value or nonnegative clamp is applied to an axis candidate. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3826–0x1A3A1C`.)

Exactly one voxel coordinate changes for an advance. Its signed 32-bit value is increased by one when that chosen direction component is strictly positive; otherwise it is decreased by one, with wrap. The increment is then added to the current travel parameter in binary32. Simultaneous face crossings do not advance several coordinates together; a later equal-time iteration can inspect an adjoining cell before another tied axis advances. A zero direction is not rejected: with no eligible axis it repeatedly decrements X using the default 10.0 increment, subject to the maximum and counter termination. This is the observed fallback relation, not an assertion that zero direction describes a meaningful geometric ray. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3A1C–0x1A3A62`.)

Another voxel is queried only while the new travel parameter is strictly below the maximum. Equality ends the traversal, so a voxel entered exactly at the maximum need not be queried. If an advance overshoots, only the returned travel bound is clamped down; the current voxel coordinates are not recomputed at the clamped parameter. If the original maximum is zero or negative, voxel traversal is skipped after initial coordinate conversion. The initial zero parameter is retained for either sign of zero; a strictly negative maximum replaces it during the upper clamp. There is no initial direction normalization, minimum positive length, or lower-bound repair. Nonfinite comparisons and conversion faults are explicitly outside the ordinary finite relation. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3681–0x1A3696`, `0x1A3A36–0x1A3A93`.)

**Additional spatial-record geometry.** A zero low byte in the additional option returns after the voxel-stage clamp. Any nonzero low byte enables a separate pass, regardless of the requested voxel category. Its candidate region is the inclusive X/Y rectangle between two spatial coordinates. The start side is derived by dividing original X/Y by 65,536 with truncation, retaining the low 32 bits, then dividing those signed values by 256 with truncation. Unlike initial voxel coordinates, this derivation does not subtract one for negative original positions. The other side uses the traversal's final current voxel X/Y divided by 256 with signed truncation. Minima and maxima are selected independently for X and Y. The final voxel is retained even after maximum-length overshoot was clamped, so this rectangle is not recomputed from a continuous ray endpoint. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3A93–0x1A3B88`.)

Rectangle traversal visits X in increasing order and, for each X, Y in increasing order, including both endpoints. Coordinates outside signed interval 0 through 65,535 are skipped. Spatial lookup is inlined here: world `+0xBC` supplies the top-level pointer grid indexed by X/64 then Y/64, and a nonnull top-level object supplies its pointer grid at `+0x10018`, indexed by local X times 64 followed by local Y. Null pointers are skipped; no load request, fallback geometry, or world publication is generated. Each present spatial object supplies a contiguous record range from pointer `+0x0C` to pointer `+0x10`; the latter is sampled before that object's record traversal. Records are visited in increasing address order at stride `0x188`, until pointer equality with that end. There is no distance sorting, duplicate suppression, or expansion of the rectangle according to record dimensions. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3B88–0x1A3C63`, `0x1A469C–0x1A46F2`.)

A record participates only when its full 32-bit kind at `+0` equals 3, 4, 5, or 8, and byte `+0x30` is nonzero. Its position comprises signed 64-bit X/Y/Z at `+0x08/+0x10/+0x18`. Binary32 dimensions are read from `+0x24/+0x28/+0x2C`. A signed 32-bit selector at `+0x20` swaps the first two dimensions when it is odd, including negative odd values; even values retain them. This is an extent swap only, with no angle, trigonometric rotation or absolute-value normalization. The byte and selector are numeric admissions, not inferred activation or orientation names. No further record-identity, creature, health, ownership, material, or voxel-category test is added. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3C63–0x1A3CE9`.)

The additional geometry consists of four vertical side-face candidates, considered in this order for each qualifying record: negative-X face, positive-X face, negative-Y face, positive-Y face. There are no top or bottom face candidates. An exactly zero X direction, of either sign, skips both X faces; exactly zero Y skips both Y faces. The per-axis squared threshold used by voxel stepping is not used here. Consequently a finite small nonzero horizontal component can reach face divisions even when it was ineligible for voxel stepping. A purely vertical direction cannot be shortened by these side-face tests. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3CE9–0x1A3D27`, `0x1A3F8E–0x1A4005`, `0x1A41D4–0x1A4218`, `0x1A4460–0x1A44D7`.)

For a horizontal dimension, its half-extent is formed by binary32 multiplication by 0.5, then binary32 multiplication by 65,536.0, then signed 64-bit truncation through `0x28D946`. A side plane is the record's corresponding fixed X or Y plus or minus that half-extent, with 64-bit wrap. The negative face is evaluated before the positive face regardless of direction sign. The plane-minus-original-start displacement is converted as signed 64-bit to binary32, multiplied by `2^-16` in binary32, and divided by the corresponding direction component in binary32. The resulting candidate parameter must be at least zero and strictly less than the current shared travel bound. A candidate equal to that bound is rejected. The same bound initially comes from voxel traversal and is shortened immediately after each accepted face. Thus equal-distance later faces or records do not replace an earlier result, though no hit identity is returned.

For a time-admitted X face, the routine converts direction times candidate parameter to three fixed displacements in X, Y, Z order, preserving the two single-precision multiplication boundaries. It uses the resulting Y/Z displacements added to the original start. The cross-face Y coordinate must be at least record Y minus its fixed half-Y extent and strictly less than record Y plus that extent. Its Z coordinate must be at least record Z and strictly less than record Z plus its fixed full-height extent. Full height is formed by multiplying binary32 `+0x2C` by 65,536.0 and converting to signed 64-bit; there is no halving of height. Y faces use the corresponding half-open X interval and the same half-open Z interval. Lower boundaries are included; upper boundaries are excluded. All coordinate additions/subtractions retain 64-bit wrap, and interval comparisons use signed 64-bit order. The record's Z is the lower height boundary, not the center of a symmetric Z interval. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3D08–0x1A41CC`, `0x1A41F4–0x1A4694`.)

These tests are side-face intersections with finite horizontal extents and an upward interval from the record's Z. They do not establish closed-volume intersection for a start inside the record or a ray entering only through its top or bottom. The routine does not reorder negative dimensions, validate positive height, or introduce a record-sized swept volume. The arithmetic and comparisons are established; meaningful geometric interpretation assumes ordinary finite dimensions with representable fixed conversions.

The native query order is observable even when candidates fail. For a nonzero horizontal direction, that axis's half-extent conversion occurs before the two time tests. Each time-admitted face performs all three direction/displacement conversions before its transverse comparisons. The transverse half-extent is converted next; the lower transverse and lower Z checks precede the upper transverse check. Full height is converted only after those checks pass, before the upper Z check. Positive and negative faces repeat their own transverse/height conversions when admitted, and Y-face work follows the X-face work. A closer result does not stop later cell or record traversal. Even a zero shared bound still permits metadata, direction and extent work, although no ordinary nonnegative face parameter can pass the strict shortening test. (`assembly.txt`, RVAs `0x1A3D08–0x1A469C`.)

**Scoped helper effects and randomness.** Spatial helper `0x034D10` rejects signed coordinates below zero or at least 65,536. It queries `0x034A90` with coordinate/64 and returns the selected second-level pointer. The latter rejects signed top-level coordinates outside 0–1023 and reads the world pointer grid at `+0xBC`. Neither helper allocates, activates, publishes, loads a cell, calls a gameplay callback, or draws/seeds randomness. The optional geometry pass's inlined lookup has the same grid identity and ordering for its admitted nonnegative coordinates. (`assembly.txt`, helpers `0x034A90–0x034AC3`, `0x034D10–0x034D85`; individually identified files `decompiled/client/functions/00434a90.c`, `00434d10.c`.)

Column helper `0x02F730` returns element pointer plus four times the signed index for an index at least zero and strictly below the signed count. In a valid nonoverflowing voxel-column interval, the ray's earlier checks admit this ordinary element route. The helper also has its own fallback routes: a negative index may set mask `0x01` in shared initialization flags at RVA `0x36AFCC` and writes bytes `00 00 00 01` to shared record `0x36AFC8`; an index at or above the count may set mask `0x02` and writes bytes `FF FF FF 00` to shared record `0x36AFD0`. The flag bit is added on first use, while the record bytes are rewritten on each respective fallback call. These are separate records from the three world fallbacks above. Wrapped or otherwise exceptional column metadata must not be treated as proof that all queries are globally write-free. (`assembly.txt`, RVA `0x02F730–0x02F7DB`; `decompiled/client/functions/0042f730.c`.)

The ray routine itself writes local working state and returns the scalar result; it does not mutate the caller's start/direction arrays, spatial record fields, column arrays, world maps, actors or output-event queues on ordinary valid calls. Its direct native callees are the two numeric helpers, spatial lookup, column access, and the security-cookie check. The scoped spatial/default initialization helpers have no further gameplay generators. There is no imported random API, seed call, random-state save/restore, allocation, virtual dispatch or gameplay callback in the audited ray path or those scoped ordinary helpers. Conversion and floating-point status effects remain, as do the column helper's fallback globals. Runtime security-failure handling and faults are outside normal return behavior; no broader runtime state contract is inferred from random-call absence.

**Limits.** This contract establishes finite-input arithmetic and native query order with valid, stable pointers and containers. It does not establish initialization scheduling of the whole world, production/ownership/lifetime of the spatial records, loader concurrency, persistent material semantics, geometry outside the visited rectangle, or a relationship between this query and every other native collision caller. The record-range endpoint is not a safe-count validation; malformed ranges, dangling pointers, or mutation during traversal can invalidate the ordinary relation. There is no null-pointer recovery for malformed world/column storage beyond the specific checks stated above.

Nonfinite inputs, overflow during float-to-int64 conversion, quotient narrowing outside the retained signed-32 coordinate range, wrapped column bounds, unmasked floating-point exceptions, nondefault denormal handling, and arithmetic near extreme fixed-coordinate limits remain explicitly outside the ordinary geometric interpretation. The specified instruction-width wrap, conversion placements and equality rules are preserved rather than replaced by mathematically continuous traversal. In particular, the finite ordinary contract does not promise that all degenerate or extreme inputs produce a nonnegative parameter, or that the step-limit exit means an unobstructed ray. These limits do not supply inferred collision behavior for unclosed exceptional cases.
