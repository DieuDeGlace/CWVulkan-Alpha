ALPHAVULKAN · CLEAN-ROOM SPECIFICATIONRevision 85 · 2026-11-22Target: Cube World Alpha (Cube.exe and Server.exe)

AlphaVulkan Clean-Room Implementation

AlphaVulkan is a 64-bit Vulkan replacement engine for Cube World Alpha that aims for 1:1 behaviour with the player's own Alpha client and server. This document has four parts: the interoperability facts any implementation may reuse, the functional specification the engine must meet, the material those facts come from, and the tests that confirm the behaviour matches the original.

Part A

Interoperability facts

Facts the engine needs to read the player's Alpha installation and saves, talk to original Alpha clients and servers, and behave the same. They are functional facts rather than creative expression, so the contracts state them as plain values, each cited by RVA in the Alpha image it comes from.

A1What may be copied

Stated directly in specs and code
  • File-format layouts: store schemas, record fields, widths, versions and encodings
  • Key derivations and unscrambling tables needed to open the player's own files
  • Packet ids, handshake codes, byte layouts, the port and section order
  • Constants, thresholds, timings, divisors and magic numbers, with their exact bit patterns
  • Record offsets and identifiers that saves or packets carry
  • The order of decisions and random draws, where generated worlds or combat must match
  • Dictionary keys, store keys and file names the game itself looks up
Never copied
  • Code, pseudocode or decompiler output, or its structure and expression
  • Names a decompiler or the Infinite-Cubes reconstruction invented
  • Game content: models, sounds, music, pictures, dictionary text, item and creature tables. These are read from the player's installation at run time.
  • The player's saves, captures and recordings
Rule of thumb
A value that a save file, a packet or a matching result depends on is a fact and may be written down. Anything that is the game's content, or how its programmers chose to write it, stays out. Where a table is game content (for example the 2,857 voxel models or the two dictionaries), the contract says where the game finds it and the engine reads it from the owner's install through the setup tool.

A2File formats

Generated zone items. An early cave item precedes loose items on generated furniture. Feature items follow, then surface vegetation. Each placement supplies its own origin and scale. Items with origins 0 and 2 settle through air and water. Origin 1 retains the placement height. The saved zone is applied after this traversal. The seven original placement sites establish the order and field values.

Generated item groups. Groups of eight or more items sharing category, subtype and material produce one resident type-9 record for categories 1 and 11. Records appear in ascending key order at the horizontal center of their zone. Loading a saved item list leaves them in place. The original count and lifetime record traces the threshold and zone teardown. A gameplay or map use remains unverified.

Generated spawn groups. After the item groups, spawns whose time window ends at 86,400,000 ms are counted by their signed controller and creature type. Each group of five or more with controller 1, 5 or 6 adds one resident type-10 record holding only the creature type, at the zone’s horizontal center, in ascending signed key order. The window’s start, the clock and positions are not tested. The saved-zone overlay neither restores nor recounts them. The original type-10 record establishes no reader of these records.

Saved zone items. A missing zone row keeps the generated items. An existing row filters saved items by signed day, then compares the survivors in order. A difference replaces the list and marks the zone modified. An edit-only zone writes zero items while its modified flag is clear. A marked zone writes the resident items in order. The original-client zone record gives the comparison fields and the separate object overlays. Successful pickup and placement mark the destination. Item timers, static toggles and terrain edits have their own outcomes under the modification rules.

Original client save interchange. Original Alpha opened and saved one engine-created character and world. Two fresh Vulkan sessions reopened the resulting save at its stored visit with all 172 ordered terrain edits and 29 map tiles. Both kept the complete character record written by original Alpha. Multiple characters, worlds and longer sessions still need acceptance.

Engine save import. AlphaSetup can copy the player's character and world catalogs and their associated world and map databases into a fresh engine Save folder. It preserves the original records and adds the required water profile to each copied world database. On local world entry the selected character resumes at the exact signed fixed-point coordinates for that world’s seed and name. A first visit starts at the world’s home anchor. The actor and its drawable position agree before the first motion update. Setup instructions.

A3Network protocol

The dedicated server builds each outgoing NPC snapshot from creature construction defaults. It sends the current terrain contact, body rotation and head pitch. The descriptor identifying the spawn and its raw home position travel in separate fields. A fresh pet has no assigned dialogue destination.

On receipt of fatal credit, the client resolves both creatures before making any writes. When the designated creature receives experience, it adds the record amount, then looks up the designated creature again for level processing. At level 1, 146 experience reaches level 2 with 96 left. A total of 147 reaches level 3 with none left. Each level increase replaces health after the experience debit. Fatal-credit and level contract.

TCP over IPv4 on the fixed port 12345; neither image has a datagram path or a port setting. Every message starts with a u32 id, values little-endian. Handshake: the server sends 17, the client answers with protocol version 3, the server replies 16 (accepted, then the player id, a creature record and packet 15 with the seed) or 3 (wrong version). The "server full" reply 18 exists but can never be sent by the original server.

  3. Contractauthor

    The author reads only the assigned evidence, writes code-free prose citing RVAs, reads it back against the evidence and marks it held.

  4. Intakeimplementer

    The implementer checks the hash, reads the whole contract and records it in docs/CLEANROOM.md.

    Change the engine where it differs, in its own structure, with a focused regression for each behaviour change.

    Run the full gate (D2) and review the live-run screenshots.

  7. Freeze and publishshared

    An exact-path commit, audited, pushed and confirmed on the remote.

E4Exposure-stop rule

Rule
A role exposed to prohibited material stops and reports it without repeating the contents. Ignoring it does not restore separation. Further work on that topic needs a new author context, and the exposure is recorded in docs/CLEANROOM.md.

E5Publication

  • Every tested checkpoint is committed and pushed to the public repository; history is never rewritten or force-pushed.
  • The audit refuses to run unless the changed paths match the checkpoint's exact list; files another author is still editing are held back.
  • Held-contract hashes, credentials and whitespace are checked, and a public mirror of the tree is scanned for prohibited content.
  • A push counts as done only after the remote head is confirmed.
  • Never published: owner assets, the game's data, saves, captures, recordings, private diagnostics or the local handoff.

E6Release gates

  • Specification provenance: every remaining inherited contract re-certified, and every held contract's hash recorded (docs/provenance-audit.md).
  • Native acceptance: captures from the original game compared for gameplay, visuals and generated worlds; save interchange with the original client.
  • Human acceptance: controller play on real hardware, and multiplayer against original Alpha clients and servers.
